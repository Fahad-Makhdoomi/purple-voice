Bilingual pre-screening AI gives applicants the choice of language on a screening call. That matters in hiring, housing and programme enrolment where many people speak a second language at home and feel less sure on the phone in the first. A voice agent can ask the question in both languages and carry on in the chosen one. It is useful, but it is not magic: quality varies by language, and every language you offer should be tested by someone who speaks it.

## What an AI voice agent does for bilingual screening

- **Offers a choice up front.** A short greeting in both languages, then continues in whichever the caller picks.
- **Asks the same questions in each language.** The substance is identical so answers remain comparable.
- **Answers questions in the caller's language.** Pay, schedule and location content is written in both languages in the knowledge base.
- **Keeps the summary readable.** Summaries can be written in your team's working language so reviewers do not need to read both.
- **Routes by language.** A campaign can be set per language if your list is already split.
- **Hands off when unsure.** If the caller prefers a third language or the call becomes confusing, the agent offers a human callback.

## What to check per language

| Check | Why it matters | Who does it |
|---|---|---|
| Greeting and consent wording | Natural phrasing builds trust | Fluent speaker |
| Question clarity | Literal translations often sound odd | Fluent speaker |
| Numbers, dates, times | Easy to mishear | Test calls |
| Names and place names | Frequently misrecognised | Spot-check transcripts |
| Summary accuracy | Reviewers rely on it | Reviewer with that language |
| Handoff wording | Callers need to know a person is coming | Fluent speaker |

## A sample call

*Illustrative only; the second language is shown in English translation.*

**Agent:** Hello. This is an automated assistant. Would you like to continue in English or in Spanish?

**Caller:** In Spanish, please.

**Agent:** *(in Spanish)* Of course. I am calling about your application for the kitchen assistant job. Do you have a few minutes?

**Caller:** *(in Spanish)* Yes.

**Agent:** *(in Spanish)* The shifts are in the evening. Are you able to work from four to eleven?

**Caller:** *(in Spanish)* Yes, but I need to leave early on Thursdays.

**Agent:** *(in Spanish)* Understood, I will note it. Would you like an interview on Monday or Wednesday?

**Caller:** *(in Spanish)* Monday.

**Agent:** *(in Spanish)* Booked. A team member will confirm in writing.

## What to set up first

1. **Decide the languages with data.** Look at which languages applicants ask for, not at which you hope to support.
2. **Confirm availability with your setup.** Language and voice quality depend on the AI configuration managed for your account, so check with the Purple Voice team and test before launching.
3. **Write both scripts natively.** Have a fluent speaker write or review them; avoid literal translation.
4. **Duplicate the knowledge base.** Pay, hours and location in each language, kept in sync.
5. **Use campaign hours and retries as usual,** up to five attempts, and consider separate campaigns per language.
6. **Add disclosure and consent in both languages.**
7. **Test with native speakers on your own phone,** including accents and fast speech.

## Bilingual pre-screening AI and fairness

Offering a language is an access feature, not a fairness guarantee. Questions still need to be job-related and the same for everyone; see [fair AI pre-screening](/solutions/voice-ai-agent-for-pre-screening-fairness). Be careful about language requirements as a knockout; if the role really requires a language, say so in the advert and read [knockout questions](/solutions/voice-ai-agent-for-knockout-questions). For customer-facing equivalents, see [multilingual customer care](/solutions/ai-voice-agent-for-multilingual-customer-care), and the [pre-screening overview](/solutions/voice-ai-agent-for-pre-screening) shows the base workflow.

## Where it falls short

- Not every language is equally well supported, and quality in the second language may be lower than in the first. Test, do not assume.
- Regional accents, code-switching and strong background noise cause errors.
- The agent cannot assess language proficiency for a role that needs it. That needs a human or a proper assessment.
- Rules on language and hiring vary. This is general information, not legal advice; check what applies.

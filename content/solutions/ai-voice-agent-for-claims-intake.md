AI first notice of loss calls let an AI voice agent take a policyholder's first report in a fixed, complete order: safety, then who, what, when and where. It records the call, produces a summary and passes it to your claims team or adjuster, while anything involving injury, liability or payment goes to a person. It suits agencies and small carriers whose claims line is busy at the worst possible times, such as after storms.

## Why intake is a good job for an agent

Intake is structured. Most of the value lies in getting the same facts every time and getting them fast, before memories drift. Humans on a busy line tend to skip questions or take notes in their own style, and the adjuster ends up calling back for details. A consistent script reduces those callbacks.

It is also the moment when a person is stressed, so the agent has to be calm, plain and ready to hand over.

## What an AI voice agent does for claims intake

- **Asks about safety first.** Before any form-filling, it checks nobody is hurt.
- **Takes the story in order.** What happened, when, where, and who was involved.
- **Collects the basics.** Policy holder name, best contact number, vehicle or property affected.
- **Captures other parties and witnesses** as the caller gives them, without arguing about fault.
- **Reads it back.** The caller can fix a wrong detail before the call ends.
- **Gives next steps.** What the team will do and when the caller can expect contact, from your knowledge base.
- **Flags severity.** Urgent cases are marked at the top of the summary and transferred.

## Call type, response and escalation

| Situation | Agent response | Escalation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor property damage | Records details and photos instructions | Summary to claims inbox |
| Vehicle accident, no injury | Takes facts, other driver details | Summary, flag if disputed |
| Injury mentioned | Advises emergency services, offers transfer | Live transfer to a person |
| Water, fire or storm damage | Records damage, advises on safety | Priority flag to adjuster |
| Caller asks "Will this be covered?" | Explains the claim team decides | Booked callback |

## A sample call

Illustrative only.

**Agent:** This is the automated claims assistant for an insurance agency. First, is anyone hurt or in danger?
**Caller:** No, nobody is hurt. A tree came down on our fence last night.
**Agent:** I am glad nobody is hurt. Can you tell me the address and roughly when it happened?
**Caller:** Our home address, around two in the morning.
**Agent:** Thank you. Is the fence the only damage, or did anything get into the house?
**Caller:** Just the fence and a shed roof.
**Agent:** I have fence and shed roof damage from a fallen tree at about two this morning. A claims handler will contact you. Can I confirm the best number to reach you?

## What to set up first

1. **Write the safety branch** and test it more than anything else. Include a line for emergency services and a live transfer.
2. **Draft the intake script** with your claims team, matching the fields in your claims system.
3. **Add the knowledge base**: what documents to gather, what the team does next and expected response times.
4. **Set the recording and disclosure line** to match your jurisdiction's rules.
5. **Route out of hours** using the [after-hours setup](/solutions/ai-voice-agent-for-insurance-after-hours) so storms at night are covered.
6. **Enable warm transfer** to the on-call adjuster or claims line for severe cases.
7. **Test on your own phone** with an upset, rambling caller and see whether the agent keeps order without sounding robotic.

## Where it falls short

The agent does not assess damage, coverage or fault, and it should say so when pushed. A caller who is crying or angry may be better served by a person from the first sentence, and some teams choose to let callers press a key or say "person" at any time. Noisy roadside calls and unfamiliar place names can cause errors, so reading back matters. Claims often have regulatory timelines and record rules; treat the agent's output as a draft report until a handler reviews it.

Handling of claims data and recording consent differs by country. This is general information, not legal advice; check current rules. See the [insurance compliance page](/solutions/ai-voice-agent-for-insurance-compliance).

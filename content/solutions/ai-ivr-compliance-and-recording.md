AI IVR recording and consent comes down to two plain duties: be honest that the caller is talking to an AI, and be open about recording and transcription. How much formal consent you need depends on where you and your callers are. This page is general information, not legal advice; rules change, so check current requirements with a qualified adviser before you launch.

It is written for owners and operations leads about to put an AI agent on a real phone line, who want to start in the right place rather than patch things later.

## What an AI phone line may record

With Purple Voice an agent can produce, for each call:

- **A recording** of the conversation.
- **A transcript** of what both sides said.
- **An AI summary** with the key details.
- **Data you collect**, such as names, numbers and appointment details.

Each of those is information about a person. Treat them as such: collect what you need, keep it for a defined purpose, and limit who can see it.

## Disclosure and consent: what to consider

| Question | Why it matters | Where to look |
|---|---|---|
| Are you recording? | Many places require notice, some require consent | Local recording and wiretap rules |
| Is the caller told it is an AI? | Honesty, and some expectations or rules | Local and sector guidance |
| Whose consent is needed? | One party or all parties, depending on place | Where each party is located |
| What data do you hold? | Privacy and data protection rules apply | GDPR, PECR, state or national laws |
| How long do you keep it? | Retention limits and purpose limits | Your policy and sector rules |
| Do you transfer to a person? | The recording may continue across the transfer | Your wording and setup |

Because callers can be anywhere, a conservative approach is to give clear notice at the start of every call, whichever rules might apply. It is general advice, and a local adviser should confirm it.

## A sample opening and exchange

Illustrative wording, not legal text.

Agent: Hello, this is the assistant for the practice. I'm an AI, and this call may be recorded and transcribed so we can help you and keep a record. How can I help?
Caller: I'd rather not be recorded.
Agent: Understood. I can put you through to a colleague who can help. One moment.

Decide in advance what happens when someone objects: stop recording, transfer to a person, or take a message. Then make sure the agent follows it, and test it.

## What to set up first

1. **Draft the opening notice** with three elements: who you are, that it is an AI, that the call may be recorded.
2. **Decide the objection route.** A person, a callback or no recording.
3. **Write it into the agent prompt** and test by objecting on your own phone.
4. **Limit access** to recordings, transcripts and summaries to those who need them.
5. **Set retention.** Pick a period and delete on schedule.
6. **Keep a simple record** of your wording and when it changed.
7. **Review sector rules.** Health, finance and insurance often carry extra requirements, see [AI IVR for clinics](/solutions/ai-ivr-for-clinics).
8. **Have an adviser check** before launch.

For a longer look at the outbound side, read [TCPA and AI voice calls](/blog/tcpa-and-ai-voice-calls), [how to disclose AI on calls](/blog/disclose-ai-on-calls) and the [sales call compliance checklist](/resources/sales-call-compliance-checklist).

## Where AI IVR recording and consent falls short

A notice does not make a call lawful on its own; it is one step among several. Multi-region callers make "one rule" hard, and a transfer to a person in another location can change the picture. Callers may not hear or understand a quick disclosure on a bad line, so keep it plain and repeat it where it matters. Recordings can contain sensitive details the caller volunteered, such as health information, which raises the bar on access and retention. None of this is legal advice, and nothing here replaces a review of your own situation.

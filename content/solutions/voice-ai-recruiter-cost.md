AI recruiter cost has three parts: the Purple Voice plan, the phone number and the AI usage, the last two billed by those providers. To compare it with manual screening, divide the total monthly cost by the candidates actually screened. The worked example below uses made-up numbers you can replace with your own.

New to the tool? Start with the [voice AI recruiter overview](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter).

## What makes up the cost

- **Purple Voice plan.** Free is $0 with 100 minutes a month. Basic is $19 a month with 1,000 minutes. Pro is $299 a month with 20,000 minutes. Enterprise is custom.
- **Phone number.** Rented from Twilio, Plivo or SuperPhone, and billed by them.
- **AI usage.** Billed by the AI provider, not Purple Voice. The team manages the AI layer for you.
- **Your time.** Writing the prompt, reviewing summaries and handling warm transfers.
- **Not counted here.** Any ATS or other software you already pay for.

## Plan comparison

| Plan | Monthly price | Minutes | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | 100 | Piloting one role |
| Basic | $19 | 1,000 | One steady campaign |
| Pro | $299 | 20,000 | Several roles or clients, webhooks and API |
| Enterprise | Custom | Custom | Large or special requirements |

Phone and AI provider charges are on top of every plan.

## A worked example (illustrative)

*These numbers are assumptions for illustration, not benchmarks.*

Suppose you receive 400 applications for a role and want to phone-screen all of them. Say 55 percent answer, giving 220 connected calls. If the average call lasts 3 minutes, that is 660 minutes, which fits within Basic's 1,000. Add some minutes for unanswered calls that ring and for retries.

Add the Basic plan at $19, then your own estimate for phone and AI usage from your providers. Suppose that came to $X in total. Cost per screened candidate is $X divided by 220. Compare that with a recruiter spending 8 minutes per manual screen: 220 screens is about 29 hours. Value those hours at your own rate and compare.

This is not a promise of savings. Your pick-up rate, call length and provider rates will change the result.

## What to set up first

1. Count your real monthly applicants and decide how many deserve a phone screen.
2. Check your phone and AI provider rates and add them to your sheet.
3. Run a pilot on the free plan with the same prompt you plan to use.
4. Read the call durations in Calls and replace the assumed average with the real one.
5. Check how many contacts connect after retries, set up to 5.
6. Use the [ROI calculator](/resources/ai-calling-roi-calculator) to compare with manual time.
7. Pick the plan that fits your measured minutes, not your guess.

## What an ai recruiter cost model leaves out

Cost per screened candidate ignores quality. A cheaper screen that misses good people is not cheaper. Pair the cost view with [metrics such as contact and completion rate](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter-metrics) and compare against your [human recruiter workload](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter-vs-human-recruiter). More background on plans is in [AI voice agent pricing explained](/blog/ai-voice-agent-pricing-explained).

## Where it falls short

Costs vary. A low pick-up rate raises the cost per screened candidate, and long calls use minutes fast. A weak prompt can waste minutes on rambling. The free plan caps contacts at 100 per campaign. Savings depend on whether recruiters really use the freed time for better work, and that is a management choice, not a feature.

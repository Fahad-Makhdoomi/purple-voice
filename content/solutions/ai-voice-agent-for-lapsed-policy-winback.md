Lapsed policy win back calls are outbound calls to customers whose cover has ended. An AI voice agent asks what happened, records the reason and books a review with your team for those who are open to returning. It is for agencies with a list of past customers they know they should call and never quite do.

## Why lapsed customers are worth a call

A lapsed customer already knows you, has been through your onboarding and may have left for a reason you can fix. Some forgot to pay, some switched on price and some no longer needed the cover. Each of those is a different conversation, and a list of mixed reasons is hard to triage without asking. An agent does the asking at scale.

It sits between [renewal calls](/solutions/ai-voice-agent-for-policy-renewals), which try to prevent the lapse, and [reactivating old leads](/blog/reactivate-old-leads-with-ai-calls), which works with people who never bought.

## Reasons, responses and next steps

| Reason for lapse | Likely tone | Agent response | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Missed payment or oversight | Embarrassed | Confirms, offers help to resolve | Human to reinstate |
| Price | Defensive | Listens, does not argue | Adviser reviews alternatives |
| Switched provider | Neutral | Thanks, asks if they want a later review | Note and revisit in a year |
| Sold the car or home | Practical | Congratulates, asks about new needs | New quote |
| Unhappy with service | Cold | Apologises, offers a human | Priority callback |

## What an AI voice agent does on win-back campaigns

- **Opens kindly.** "We noticed your policy ended last month" without blame.
- **Asks one question.** What happened? The answer decides the rest.
- **Hears out a complaint.** It records the words and does not defend.
- **Offers options.** A review slot, not a discount.
- **Updates your list** through summaries, so you know who is open and who is not.
- **Respects "no".** Opt-outs are logged and not retried.

## A sample call

Illustrative only.

**Agent:** Hello, this is an automated assistant calling on behalf of an insurance agency. Your home policy ended recently, and I wanted to check whether that was intentional. Is now a good time?
**Caller:** Hmm, I think the card expired.
**Agent:** That happens often, and it is easy to sort out. I cannot restart the policy myself, but I can book someone from the team to help. Would tomorrow suit?
**Caller:** Yes, in the afternoon.
**Agent:** Booked for tomorrow at two. They will talk you through your options. Thanks for your time.

## What to set up first

1. **Clean the list.** Remove open complaints, disputed claims, deceased customers and anyone who asked not to be contacted.
2. **Segment by time since lapse.** Recent lapses get a different opener from year-old ones.
3. **Write a soft prompt** that never implies customers did something wrong, and always discloses it is an AI.
4. **Add exit lines** for "I bought elsewhere" and "do not call me".
5. **Set hours and two or three retries**, not five, for customers who left.
6. **Link calendar slots** for advisers who handle reinstatements.
7. **Test on your phone** and have a colleague play an irritated former customer.

## Where it falls short

The agent cannot reinstate cover, waive a gap or explain whether a claim during the lapse would be paid. It should not hint at any of these. Calls to people who left unhappy can make things worse if they feel chased, so the segment excluded by complaints matters. Win-back rates vary widely by list quality and by how long ago people lapsed, and you should treat early results as a test, not a forecast.

The consent you hold for past customers and the rules for outbound calls vary by country. This is general information, not legal advice; check current requirements and see the [compliance page](/solutions/ai-voice-agent-for-insurance-compliance).

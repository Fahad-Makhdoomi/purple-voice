Recruiter capacity with AI is about moving hours, not removing people. When an AI voice agent takes first-contact screening, scheduling and reminder calls, a recruiter's day shifts from dialling and chasing towards assessing candidates and advising hiring managers. This page maps that change hour by hour, shows how to size it for your own team, and lists what a recruiter should still do personally.

## A recruiter's day, before

Take an illustrative desk working a mix of volume and mid-level roles. The morning starts with applicants that arrived overnight. Each needs a screening call, many go to voicemail, and several need a second attempt. Late morning is spent arranging interviews, mostly by message and phone tag. After lunch come hiring-manager updates, followed by a round of confirmation calls and the paperwork behind them. By late afternoon, the recruiter has made many calls and had few deep conversations.

The pattern is common: time follows the volume of simple tasks, and the complicated work is squeezed into what is left.

## A recruiter's day, with an agent on scripted calls

| Time block | Before | With an AI voice agent |
|---|---|---|
| 8:30 to 10:00 | Screening calls to overnight applicants | Read summaries from calls already made; shortlist |
| 10:00 to 11:30 | Interview scheduling, phone tag | Handle exceptions and complex bookings only |
| 11:30 to 13:00 | Candidate follow-ups | Call strong candidates personally |
| 14:00 to 15:30 | Hiring manager updates and briefs | Same, with better data from summaries |
| 15:30 to 17:00 | Reminder and confirmation calls | Review the confirmation list; chase unreachables |

The shape matters more than the numbers. The agent's calls happen outside the recruiter's working hours in some cases, and the recruiter's time moves to work that needs judgement.

## What an AI voice agent does for recruiter capacity

- **Handles first-contact screening.** Calls are made in volume the same day applicants apply.
- **Takes scheduling off the desk.** Interviews are booked on Google Calendar during the call.
- **Prevents empty slots.** Reminder calls reduce no-shows and the rebooking they cause.
- **Re-engages dormant candidates.** [Re-engagement calls](/solutions/ai-voice-agent-for-candidate-reengagement) find live candidates in the database.
- **Creates a clearer paper trail.** Every call has a summary and transcript, so handovers between recruiters are simpler.
- **Covers evenings and weekends.** Inbound candidate calls outside office hours reach the agent.
- **Surfaces who needs a person.** Warm transfer or a flagged summary directs recruiter attention.

## Sizing it for your team

Work out three numbers, using your own data rather than industry averages:

1. Scripted calls per recruiter per week.
2. Average minutes per call, including voicemails and retries.
3. Percentage of those calls that actually require judgement.

Multiply the first two for total hours, then take off the third. The remainder is the capacity you could redirect. For example (illustrative only), 60 calls a week at 8 minutes each is 8 hours; if a quarter need judgement, around 6 hours could go to an agent. Check the maths against minutes available on your plan. Our [ROI calculator](/resources/ai-calling-roi-calculator) can help.

## What to set up first

1. **Choose one call type** and measure it for two weeks before and after.
2. **Write the prompt and knowledge base** from your best recruiter's script, not a template alone.
3. **Set the handoff rules** so strong candidates and complex questions reach a human.
4. **Connect Google Calendar** for booking.
5. **Use campaign hours and up to 5 retries** that suit your candidates.
6. **Enable consent confirmation and AI disclosure,** then test on your own phone.
7. **Decide where freed time goes.** Without a plan, capacity disappears into more admin.

## Where it falls short

Capacity gains depend on how scripted your calls really are. A desk doing senior search will see little change; a desk doing volume will see a lot. The agent also creates new work: reading summaries, tuning scripts and handling exceptions. A badly written script produces a lot of unusable calls faster. And metrics can mislead; hours saved are only valuable if recruiters use them on better candidate conversations.

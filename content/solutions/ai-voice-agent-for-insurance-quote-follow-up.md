Insurance quote follow up calls are short check-ins to people who asked for a quote and then went silent. An AI voice agent calls them, asks if they have questions, finds out what stopped them and books a licensed adviser for those still interested. It is for agencies with a pile of unconverted quotes and no time to ring them all.

## The quiet middle of the funnel

Between a quote being sent and a policy being bought sits a long stretch where nothing happens. Some people have already bought elsewhere, some got busy and some had one unanswered question. Agencies often do not know which is which because nobody asked. A friendly call reveals it quickly, and the reasons you collect are worth having even when the quote is lost.

Fresh enquiries have their own pattern, covered under [auto insurance leads](/solutions/ai-voice-agent-for-auto-insurance-leads). This page is for the ones who got the quote and stalled.

## Where quotes get stuck

| Why it stalled | What the agent says | What happens next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price felt high | Asks how it compares, records the answer | Adviser reviews options |
| Still comparing | Offers to answer questions, asks their timeline | Callback before their deadline |
| Did not understand a term | Gives a plain-language answer from the knowledge base | Adviser if it is cover advice |
| Bought elsewhere | Thanks them, asks if they would share why | Closed with a reason |
| Life changed | Notes it and offers a fresh quote | New enquiry |

## A sample call

Illustrative only.

**Agent:** Hello, this is the automated assistant for an insurance agency. We sent you a van insurance quote last week. I am calling to see if you have any questions. Is now okay?
**Caller:** Oh, yes. I have been meaning to reply.
**Agent:** No rush. Is there anything that has held you up, such as the price or a part of the cover you were unsure about?
**Caller:** I did not understand the excess.
**Agent:** The excess is the amount you pay towards a claim before the insurer pays the rest. For how it applies to your quote, an adviser can walk you through it. Shall I book a short call?
**Caller:** Yes, tomorrow at lunch.

## What an AI voice agent does on quote follow-ups

- **Refers to the specific quote.** Product and date from your list make the call relevant.
- **Asks one open question.** "What got in the way?" beats a hard close.
- **Explains plain terms.** Excess, no-claims status and renewal dates, using your knowledge base. Not advice.
- **Records the reason.** Price, timing or competitor, per call, so you can see patterns later in [Insights](/blog/how-ai-voice-agents-work).
- **Books a human** on Google Calendar when the person wants to continue.
- **Stops politely.** If the caller is done, the agent closes the loop and does not chase.

## What to set up first

1. **Pick the window.** Quotes one to three weeks old are usually the sweet spot. Older ones belong in a [win-back list](/solutions/ai-voice-agent-for-lapsed-policy-winback).
2. **Add the quote context** to the contact list: name, product and date.
3. **Write a short prompt** with one open question and a clean exit.
4. **Fill the knowledge base** with plain definitions and what the adviser covers.
5. **Set retries** to two or three, with different hours.
6. **Connect Google Calendar** for adviser slots.
7. **Test on your phone**, saying "I bought elsewhere" and "your price is too high" to hear both paths.

## Where it falls short

A call cannot fix an uncompetitive price. It will not persuade someone who has already decided, and it should not try. It cannot confirm that a competitor's quote is comparable, which needs an adviser. Some people see a call about a quote they requested as pestering; keep it to one question and honour a no. If your quotes have no phone numbers or consent for calls, there is nothing to dial.

Whether you may call people about a quote depends on the consent you captured and the rules where you operate. This is general information, not legal advice; read [TCPA and AI voice calls](/blog/tcpa-and-ai-voice-calls) and check current rules.

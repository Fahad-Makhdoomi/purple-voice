AI IVR for small business means a phone agent that answers when you cannot: it greets the caller, answers the usual questions, books a slot or takes a message, and sends you a summary. It is aimed at owner-run firms, such as salons, trades, small practices and studios, where the person who does the work also answers the phone.

The cost of the old arrangement is quiet. Every missed call while you are with a customer or on a roof is a person who tries the next business on the list.

## What an AI IVR does for a small business

- **Picks up every call.** No ringing out, no voicemail you return at 6 pm.
- **Answers the repeat questions.** Prices, opening hours, parking, whether you cover a postcode. These live in the knowledge base.
- **Books on your calendar.** Appointments land in Google Calendar without a back-and-forth.
- **Separates urgent from routine.** A burst pipe gets transferred to you; a quote request gets booked for Thursday.
- **Collects the right details.** Name, number, address, what they need, so your first callback is productive.
- **Leaves a paper trail.** Transcript, AI summary and recording for every call.
- **Costs less than a part-time hire.** Free to start, with usage-based costs from your phone and AI providers.

## Setting up an AI IVR for a small business

1. **Choose one number.** Start with the line printed on your van or website.
2. **Write a short prompt.** Three paragraphs: who you are, how you want callers treated, what the agent must never promise (like firm prices on complex jobs).
3. **List your ten most-asked questions** and put the answers in the knowledge base.
4. **Connect Google Calendar** and block the times you do not want booked.
5. **Name your transfer rule.** For example: leaks, no heating, complaints or any caller who asks for you go to your mobile.
6. **Set the consent wording** so callers know they reach an AI and that calls may be recorded. See [recording and consent](/solutions/ai-ivr-compliance-and-recording).
7. **Test-call it** from your own phone, including awkward questions, before you give anyone the number.

## A sample call

Illustrative, for a small hair salon.

Agent: Hello, this is the booking assistant for the salon. How can I help?
Caller: Do you do men's cuts on Saturdays?
Agent: Yes, we do, from 9 until 4. Would you like to book one?
Caller: Is there anything around 11?
Agent: There is a slot at 11:15 with Dan. Shall I book that?
Caller: Please. Name's Sam.
Agent: Booked, Sam. Can I take a mobile number in case the stylist needs to reach you?

## Where it falls short for a tiny business

It cannot read your mood, apply a favour for a regular, or decide to waive a fee. If your brand rests on the owner's personal voice, you may prefer the agent only after hours or only when you are busy, and keep the rest for yourself. It also needs honest setup: if your price list changes weekly and nobody updates the knowledge base, callers get stale answers. And noisy workshops or poor mobile reception on the caller's side will cause the odd misheard detail, which is why the agent should repeat names and numbers back.

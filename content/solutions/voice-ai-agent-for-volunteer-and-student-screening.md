Volunteer pre-screening AI suits organisations where enthusiasm outruns coordinator time: a food bank with two hundred sign-ups after a local news story, a school starting a mentoring programme, a charity run that needs marshals in six weeks. A voice agent phones each person, confirms they understand the commitment and books the next step. People who are not a fit are told kindly; people who are get a quick path in.

## What an AI voice agent does for volunteers and students

- **Calls while interest is high.** Sign-ups cool within days; a call within hours keeps them engaged.
- **Explains the role clearly.** Tasks, time commitment, training dates and any checks required come from your knowledge base.
- **Confirms availability.** Weekday evenings, a one-off event date, a term-long placement.
- **Notes interests and skills.** Languages, driving, first aid, a subject they could mentor, recorded as stated.
- **Mentions requirements honestly.** If a background check applies, the agent says so and notes whether the person is willing to proceed.
- **Books orientation or interview.** Real slots on Google Calendar, with group sessions if you run them.
- **Spots drop-offs.** The not-reached list and summaries show who is still keen and who has moved on.

## Programme types

| Programme | Agent's job | Stays with staff |
|---|---|---|
| One-off event | Confirm date, role preference, arrival details | Final rota |
| Ongoing volunteers | Availability, commitment, orientation booking | Vetting and training |
| Mentoring or tutoring | Interest, subject and schedule fit | Safeguarding decisions |
| Student programme enquiries | Programme interest, key dates, documents needed | Admissions decisions |
| Short-notice campaigns | Call list for who can come this weekend | Confirming attendees |

## A sample call

*Illustrative only.*

**Agent:** Hello, this is an AI assistant calling for the community food project. You signed up to volunteer on our website. Do you have a couple of minutes?

**Caller:** Sure.

**Agent:** The main need is Saturday mornings, three hours, packing food parcels. Would that work for you?

**Caller:** I can do Saturdays, but only two of the four each month.

**Agent:** That is fine, many volunteers do that. Is there anything that would stop you lifting boxes of around ten kilos?

**Caller:** No, that's fine.

**Agent:** Thanks. New volunteers attend a short orientation. Would Wednesday at six or Saturday at nine suit you?

**Caller:** Wednesday.

**Agent:** Booked. The coordinator will have a summary and will see you there.

## What to set up first

1. **Write a one-page role description.** Tasks, hours, where, training, any checks and what a volunteer can expect.
2. **Decide what is a requirement.** Age minimums, check consent or training dates; keep the list short and explain each in plain words.
3. **Set the calling hours for volunteers.** Evenings and weekends usually reach more people than office hours, with up to five retries.
4. **Build orientation slots** on Google Calendar, including group sessions.
5. **Write the prompt for warmth.** Thanks first, questions second; volunteers are giving time, not applying for pay.
6. **Add consent confirmation and AI disclosure,** and offer a human callback.
7. **Test on your own phone** with someone who is unsure about the commitment.

## Where it falls short

- It cannot assess safeguarding suitability. Roles with children or vulnerable adults need formal, human-led vetting.
- It cannot verify identity, age or qualifications from a phone call.
- Some volunteers prefer a human voice, particularly older supporters. Offer a callback.
- Data protection rules apply to volunteer and student data. This is general information, not legal advice; check what applies to you.

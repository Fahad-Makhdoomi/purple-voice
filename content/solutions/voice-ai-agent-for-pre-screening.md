Pre-screening is the first call in any process that has more applicants than hours: a few factual questions, asked the same way every time, to decide who deserves a longer conversation. A voice AI agent for pre-screening makes that call for you, records the answers and gives your team a short summary to act on. It suits recruiters, property managers, clinics, nonprofits and anyone whose phone screens follow a script.

## What a voice AI agent does for pre-screening

The agent handles the repeatable part of the conversation and nothing else. In practice that means:

- **Calls every applicant promptly.** An outbound campaign works through your list within calling hours and retries people who do not pick up, up to five attempts.
- **Asks a fixed set of questions.** Availability, location, right to work or eligibility, notice period, budget, whatever your process needs, in the same order for everyone.
- **Answers basic questions back.** Pay range, shift pattern, viewing times or location come from a knowledge base you write, so the agent does not improvise.
- **Handles the awkward moments.** Interruptions, "can you repeat that?" and callers who are mid-commute are normal; the agent keeps the thread.
- **Books the next step.** If someone clears your criteria, the agent can offer a slot on your Google Calendar, or warm-transfer them to a person while you are available.
- **Writes it all down.** Every call has a transcript, an AI summary and a recording, so reviewers read two lines instead of listening to ten minutes.

## Screening work: before and with an agent

| Stage | Done by hand | With an AI voice agent |
|---|---|---|
| First contact | Whoever has time, often days later | Agent calls within your campaign hours |
| Questions asked | Varies by person and by mood | Identical wording and order |
| Missed calls | Voicemail, then forgotten | Retries on your schedule, not-reached list in analytics |
| Notes | Typed up after, or not at all | Transcript and summary per call |
| Reviewer's time | Mixed in with the calling | Spent on reading summaries and deciding |

## A sample call

*Illustrative only.*

**Agent:** Hello, this is an automated assistant calling on behalf of the hiring team about your application. Is now a good time for a few quick questions?

**Caller:** Sure, I've got five minutes.

**Agent:** Thanks. First, are you able to start within the next four weeks?

**Caller:** I'd need two weeks' notice, so yes.

**Agent:** Good. Is the main site, about thirty minutes from the city centre, workable for you?

**Caller:** Yes, I drive.

**Agent:** Last one: would you like a call with a team member on Thursday morning or Friday afternoon?

**Caller:** Thursday works.

**Agent:** Booked. You will get the details shortly, and the team has a summary of what we covered.

## What to set up first

1. **Write the question list.** Five to eight questions is plenty. Mark which ones end the screen if answered a certain way, and read [knockout questions in pre-screening](/solutions/voice-ai-agent-for-knockout-questions) before you decide.
2. **Build the agent prompt.** Keep it to the script, the tone and the handoff rules. Our [agent prompt template](/resources/ai-voice-agent-prompt-template) is a starting point.
3. **Fill the knowledge base.** Pay, location, schedule, process and the three questions people always ask.
4. **Set campaign hours and retries.** Call when people can answer, with up to five retries at spaced hours.
5. **Connect Google Calendar.** Let the agent offer real slots for the next step.
6. **Turn on consent confirmation.** Make sure the agent introduces itself as an AI and confirms the person is happy to continue.
7. **Make a test call to your own phone.** Interrupt it, answer badly, ask something off-script, and fix the prompt.

## Where it falls short

- It screens on what people say, not on how well they would do the job. A screen that tells you someone is available is not evidence they are good.
- It cannot verify claims. Licences, certificates and right to work still need documents checked by a person.
- Accents, bad lines and background noise can cause misheard answers. Summaries should flag uncertainty and reviewers should be able to open the recording.
- Hiring, housing, health and similar decisions can carry legal duties. This is general information, not legal advice; check current rules for your region before automating any decision step.

The safest design is simple: the agent gathers and organises, and a person decides. How AI calls work in practice is covered in [how AI voice agents work](/blog/how-ai-voice-agents-work), and the disclosure side in [disclosing AI on calls](/blog/disclose-ai-on-calls).

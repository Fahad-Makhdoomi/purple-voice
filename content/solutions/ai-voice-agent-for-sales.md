An AI voice agent for sales is a phone agent that does the first layer of a sales call workflow: it calls new leads, answers inbound calls, asks qualifying questions, answers product questions and books the meeting. The rep joins when the conversation is worth a person's time. It is built for sales leads, founders and agency owners who have more conversations to start than hours to start them.

## What an AI voice agent does for sales

Sales phone work splits into a handful of repeatable jobs. These are the ones an agent takes on:

- **Outbound first contact.** Upload a list and the agent calls within the hours you set, retrying unanswered numbers up to 5 times with retry hours you choose.
- **Inbound answering.** Calls to your sales line are picked up straight away, including evenings and weekends, instead of rolling to voicemail.
- **Qualification.** It asks your criteria in a natural order, handles interruptions and records the answers.
- **Product and pricing questions.** It answers from the knowledge base you provide and says plainly when it does not know.
- **Meeting booking.** It checks availability and books directly on Google Calendar.
- **Warm transfer.** When a caller is ready to talk to a person, the call is transferred to a human with context.
- **Records.** Every call produces a transcript, an AI summary and a recording you can review in the Calls section.

## Where the agent sits in the sales workflow

| Stage | What the agent does | What stays with the rep |
|---|---|---|
| New lead arrives | Calls quickly, confirms interest | Anything unusual or high value |
| Qualification | Asks criteria, records answers | Judging fit on edge cases |
| Booking | Offers slots, confirms on Google Calendar | The meeting itself |
| Inbound enquiry | Answers, qualifies, routes | Negotiation and custom quotes |
| Old pipeline | Re-contacts and sorts by interest | Re-opening live opportunities |

## A sample call

This is an illustrative exchange, not a recording.

> **Agent:** Hi Sam, this is the assistant calling on behalf of a small payroll software company. You asked about a demo on our site this morning. Is now an okay time for two minutes?
> **Caller:** Sure, quickly.
> **Agent:** Great. How many people are on your payroll today?
> **Caller:** Around forty, growing.
> **Agent:** And are you moving off a current provider, or choosing for the first time?
> **Caller:** Moving off a spreadsheet, honestly.
> **Agent:** That is common for that size. I can book a 30-minute demo with a specialist. Would Thursday at 2 pm or Friday at 10 am suit you?
> **Caller:** Thursday works.
> **Agent:** Booked for Thursday at 2 pm. You will get the details from the team. Anything you want them to cover?

The agent never promised a feature it was not given, and the summary tells the rep the company is moving off spreadsheets with about forty staff.

## What to set up first

1. **Write the agent prompt.** State the offer, the five or six questions, the definition of qualified and the transfer rule. The [AI voice agent prompt template](/resources/ai-voice-agent-prompt-template) is a starting point.
2. **Add a knowledge base.** Pricing structure, common objections, service area and policies. Leave out anything you would not tell a stranger.
3. **Connect your phone number** (Twilio, Plivo or SuperPhone) and Google Calendar.
4. **Place a test call on your own phone.** Interrupt it, ask something odd, go off script.
5. **Set campaign hours and retries.** Respect time zones, and use up to 5 retries with sensible gaps.
6. **Turn on consent confirmation** where your rules call for it, and decide on your disclosure line.
7. **Pick the transfer number** and who answers it.

## Where it falls short

- **Complex selling.** Multi-stakeholder deals, negotiation and anything consultative belong to a person.
- **Emotional or sensitive calls.** An agent can recognise frustration but should hand off rather than improvise.
- **Thin inputs.** A vague prompt or a sparse knowledge base produces vague answers. The quality of the brief is most of the result.
- **Noisy lines and strong accents** can still cause misheard details, which is why transcripts and a confirmation step on names and numbers matter.
- **Call permissions.** Calling rules, consent and disclosure depend on where you and your leads are. Read [TCPA and AI voice calls](/blog/tcpa-and-ai-voice-calls) and check current rules; this is general information, not legal advice.

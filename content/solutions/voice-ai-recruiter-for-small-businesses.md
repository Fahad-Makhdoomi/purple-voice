An AI recruiter for small business takes on the part of hiring that owners find hardest to fit in: ringing applicants back, asking the same few questions and arranging interviews. It suits a salon, trades firm, clinic, shop or café where the person hiring is also serving customers and has no HR department.

If you want the broader definition first, read about the [voice AI recruiter](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter).

## What it does for an owner-led business

- **Answers applicants promptly.** An agent can call new applicants or answer inbound calls while you are with a customer.
- **Asks your questions.** Experience, availability, transport, pay expectations, anything you would ask in a first call.
- **Explains the job.** Hours, pay, location and what a normal day looks like, from the knowledge base you write.
- **Books interviews.** It uses your Google Calendar so interviews fit around your real week.
- **Keeps notes.** Every call has a summary and a transcript, so you remember who said what.
- **Tells you who was not reached.** You can follow up personally with the ones who matter.

## The owner's week, before and after

| Task | Before | With a voice AI recruiter |
|---|---|---|
| Returning applicant calls | Evenings, in a rush | Agent calls in set hours |
| Repeating the pay and hours | Every call | Agent answers from your notes |
| Arranging interviews | Back-and-forth | Booked on your calendar |
| Remembering who said what | Scribbled notes | Summaries and transcripts |
| Meeting candidates | Many, unfiltered | A shorter, better-informed list |

## A sample call

*Illustrative only.*

**Agent:** Hello, this is an AI assistant calling on behalf of a small plumbing firm about your application for the apprentice role. Do you have a minute?
**Caller:** Yes, I do.
**Agent:** Do you have a driving licence?
**Caller:** Yes, I passed last year.
**Agent:** The role starts at eight and involves travelling between jobs. Does that work for you?
**Caller:** Yes, that is fine.
**Agent:** The owner would like to meet you. Could you manage Thursday at four?
**Caller:** Yes.
**Agent:** Booked. You will get a confirmation.

## What to set up first

1. Write the job in plain words: pay, hours, location and the two or three things you cannot compromise on.
2. Add an honest note to the knowledge base about what you do not know yet, so the agent says so instead of guessing.
3. Choose calling hours when your applicants are likely to answer, and keep retries at a sensible number, up to 5.
4. Connect Google Calendar and block out times you cannot interview.
5. Say clearly in the opening that it is an AI assistant, and let people ask for you directly.
6. Test it on your own phone before a single applicant hears it.

## Where it falls short

Small businesses hire on feel, and the agent has none. It will not spot who gets on with your team or who is lying about experience. Applicants for local jobs sometimes prefer to talk to the owner, so offer that path. If you only hire once every few years, the time to set it up may exceed the time saved. Check the rules on calling and consent where you operate.

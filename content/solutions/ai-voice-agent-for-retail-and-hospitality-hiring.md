An AI voice agent for hospitality hiring screens applicants for retail floors, restaurants, hotels and bars, then books trial shifts for the ones who fit the rota. It matters most in the weeks before a season, when a manager has two weeks to hire a team and a stack of forms to sort. The agent checks availability and basic experience quickly so the manager spends time meeting people, not chasing them.

## Hiring against a date

Seasonal hiring is not hard to understand, but it is hard to do in time. Applications come in clusters after an advert goes up. Applicants often apply to several venues at once and accept the first reply. If your reply takes five days, you are hiring from those left over.

The questions are also repetitive. Can you work weekends? Are you eighteen or over? Have you worked service before? A manager asking these on the phone is spending scarce front-of-house time on administration. An agent asks them the same day.

## What an AI voice agent does for hospitality hiring

- **Calls applicants the day they apply.** Speed is the main advantage in a competitive local labour market.
- **Checks shift availability.** Weekends, split shifts, late finishes and holiday dates are collected in plain terms.
- **Confirms age and experience requirements.** Where the role has a minimum age or needs prior service experience, the agent asks and notes the answer.
- **Describes the role accurately.** Hours, pay, tips policy, uniform and location are explained from your knowledge base.
- **Books trial shifts or informal interviews.** Slots come from Google Calendar so managers are not double booked.
- **Answers common questions.** Where to report, who to ask for and what to wear are answered once, consistently.
- **Warm-transfers keen candidates.** Where a manager is on site, a strong candidate can be put through straight away.

## Role-by-role fit

| Role | What the agent asks | Who decides |
|---|---|---|
| Server or bar staff | Weekend and evening availability, service experience | Manager after a trial shift |
| Retail assistant | Weekday and weekend hours, till experience | Store manager |
| Kitchen porter or prep | Early starts, standing for long periods | Head chef |
| Hotel housekeeping | Morning availability, physical tasks | Housekeeping lead |
| Seasonal event staff | Specific dates | Operations lead |

## A sample call

Illustrative. A seasonal restaurant role.

**Agent:** Hi, this is an AI assistant calling for the restaurant team about your application for a server role. Have you got a minute?
**Applicant:** Sure.
**Agent:** The shifts are mostly evenings and weekends from the first of June. Can you work Friday and Saturday nights?
**Applicant:** Yes, I am free all weekend, but not Sundays after July.
**Agent:** That is helpful. Have you worked in a restaurant or bar before?
**Applicant:** A cafe for a year.
**Agent:** Thank you. The next step is a paid trial shift on a quiet evening. Would Tuesday at 6 pm suit you?
**Applicant:** Yes, that works.
**Agent:** Booked. Please bring black shoes, and ask for the duty manager on arrival.

## What to set up first

1. **Write the real rota needs** first, so the agent can ask against a concrete pattern.
2. **Fill the knowledge base** with pay, uniform, tips policy, location, parking and who to ask for.
3. **Set trial-shift capacity** in Google Calendar so you do not book fifteen people for the same quiet Tuesday.
4. **Choose calling hours.** Avoid the lunch and dinner rush if applicants are already working in hospitality.
5. **Use retries,** up to 5, with retry hours that fit.
6. **Enable consent confirmation and AI disclosure.**
7. **Test-call your own phone** with an eager student, a busy parent and someone who is not sure.

## Where it falls short

Front-of-house hiring is partly about presence and warmth, neither of which a phone screen measures well. An applicant with a perfect rota fit may still be wrong for the team. The agent should not be used to turn people away on subjective grounds. It also does not manage rotas, and availability changes quickly with young workers; confirm again before the first shift. For cohorts with varied accents or phone quality, read transcripts rather than trusting a single summary line.

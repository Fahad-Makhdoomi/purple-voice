Pre-screening nurses and care workers with AI works best on the unglamorous part: asking who is free when, who can travel where, and which certificates a person says they hold. Care coordinators and healthcare staffing teams often spend their mornings on those calls. A voice agent does the first pass, flags likely matches and leaves registration checks, references and suitability to people who are accountable for them.

## What an AI voice agent does for care and nursing screening

- **Confirms practical availability.** Days, nights, weekends, minimum hours a week and notice needed.
- **Checks travel and transport.** Postcode area, whether they drive, and how far they will go for a visit or shift.
- **Records stated credentials.** The agent asks which certificates or registrations they hold and notes what they say, without verifying it.
- **Describes the work.** Client types, shift lengths, pay band and training from your knowledge base, so candidates know what they are applying for.
- **Calls your bench for gaps.** Ask the list of already-screened workers who can cover Saturday, and receive the yeses in a summary.
- **Books coordinator calls.** Strong matches get a slot on your Google Calendar, or a warm transfer during office hours.

## A sample call

*Illustrative only.*

**Agent:** Hello, this is an AI assistant calling for a home-care provider about your application as a care worker. Is this a good time?

**Caller:** Yes, I'm on a break.

**Agent:** Thanks. Which days could you work in a normal week?

**Caller:** Monday to Thursday, mornings and early afternoons. Fridays occasionally.

**Agent:** Do you drive, and which areas could you cover?

**Caller:** I drive. Anywhere within about twenty minutes of town.

**Agent:** And which care certificates do you hold at the moment?

**Caller:** Level 2, and a moving and handling certificate from last year.

**Agent:** Thank you. I'll note that. The team will check those and call you to arrange an interview.

## What to set up first

1. **Write the real shift map.** Days, times, areas and the number of hours that make a placement workable.
2. **Decide what is verified later.** Registration, right to work, references and background checks stay with your team; say so in the prompt.
3. **Build the knowledge base.** Pay, travel allowances, training, uniform and the hiring process.
4. **Set respectful calling hours.** Care staff often work shifts, so use campaign hours that include late afternoon and early evening, with retries up to five.
5. **Connect Google Calendar** for coordinator interviews.
6. **Turn on consent confirmation** and AI disclosure at the start of the call.
7. **Test call yourself** with a candidate who works nights and cannot talk long.

## Care worker screening: what the agent should not ask

Stay away from health, family and similar personal details. Keep questions to availability, transport, experience and stated credentials. ## Where it falls short

- It cannot verify registration, references or checks. Treat everything recorded as "stated", not "confirmed".
- It cannot judge compassion, judgement or fitness to care. Those come from interviews and formal vetting.
- Safeguarding concerns raised on a call should route to a person immediately. Put that rule in the prompt and test it.
- Regulation of care and healthcare hiring varies. This is general information, not legal advice; check the rules in your region.

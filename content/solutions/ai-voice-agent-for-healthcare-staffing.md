An AI voice agent for healthcare staffing handles the calls that fill the diary: availability checks, shift confirmations, screening questions about specialty and location, and reminders. It does not verify registrations, give clinical advice or replace a compliance team. It is for agencies placing nurses, healthcare assistants, allied health staff and locums, where a last-minute vacancy means a coordinator ringing a long list.

## The last-minute list problem

A ward reports a sickness gap at 3 pm for a night shift. The coordinator now has to call through a pool of nurses, many of whom do not answer a withheld or unfamiliar number, and the best candidates are often on shift themselves. By the time enough people have been reached, the window may have closed.

An agent compresses this. It calls everyone on a prepared list at once, asks the same question, and reports who said yes. The coordinator reads the answers and makes the placement. Speed improves; accountability does not move.

## What an AI voice agent does for healthcare staffing

- **Checks availability for specific shifts.** It states the date, time, site and specialty, and records a yes, no or maybe.
- **Screens basics on registration.** It captures specialty, location, travel radius, notice period and preferred shift pattern.
- **Asks about registration status.** The agent records what the candidate says about their licence or registration, then flags it for compliance to verify.
- **Confirms placements.** A call before the shift confirms where to report and who to ask for.
- **Answers practical questions.** Pay dates, timesheet routes, parking and uniform come from your knowledge base.
- **Calls outside office hours.** Inbound candidates ringing after hours reach an agent instead of voicemail.
- **Passes sensitive calls to people.** Any mention of a patient safety concern, complaint or clinical question goes to a human.

## What the agent asks, and what it does not

| Topic | Agent's role | Human role |
|---|---|---|
| Availability for a shift | Asks, records | Confirms the placement |
| Registration or licence | Records candidate's statement | Verifies against the register and documents |
| Specialty and experience | Captures summary | Judges suitability for the ward |
| Pay and rate | States published band | Negotiates exceptions |
| Right to work | Asks the screening question | Checks documents |
| Clinical questions | Declines and hands over | Answers |

## A sample call

Illustrative. Details are generic.

**Agent:** Hello, this is an AI assistant for the staffing team. We have a night shift tomorrow, 8 pm to 8 am, on a medical ward at the city hospital. Are you available?
**Nurse:** Possibly. Which ward?
**Agent:** A general medical ward. Do you have recent experience on medical wards?
**Nurse:** Yes, the last two years.
**Agent:** Thank you. I will note that. The rate for this shift is on the shift sheet, and a coordinator will confirm details with you. Should I tell them you are available?
**Nurse:** Yes, please call me back on this number.
**Agent:** Done. If your registration details have changed recently, the coordinator will ask about them. Is there anything else I should flag?

## What to set up first

1. **Call only people already cleared.** Build the list from compliant staff for that shift type; do not use the agent to find out who is qualified.
2. **Write the agent prompt carefully.** Forbid clinical advice and instruct the agent to say it will pass certain topics to a person.
3. **Load the knowledge base** with site addresses, shift patterns, rate bands and your timesheet process.
4. **Set campaign hours.** Nurses working nights often sleep in the day, so choose hours deliberately and avoid very early or very late calls.
5. **Configure warm transfer** to a coordinator for urgent calls from candidates already on a placement.
6. **Enable consent confirmation and AI disclosure.**
7. **Test-call your own phone,** including a caller who raises a patient-safety concern, to check the handoff works.

## Where it falls short

Healthcare staffing carries safeguarding and regulatory duties that a phone agent cannot take on. Verification, DBS-style or equivalent checks, references and competency assessments stay with trained staff, and the agent's note about a registration is only a prompt for that work. It may also misunderstand strong accents or specialist terminology, so review ambiguous transcripts before relying on them. Do not use it to decide who is fit to treat patients.

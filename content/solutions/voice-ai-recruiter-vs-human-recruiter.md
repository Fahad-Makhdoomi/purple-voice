The honest answer to the AI recruiter vs human recruiter question is that they are good at different parts of the same job. A voice agent is dependable at the repetitive phone work that comes first, and a recruiter is far better at everything that needs judgement, empathy and a real relationship. This page is for recruiters and hiring leads deciding where to draw the line.

If you want the basics first, start with our overview of the [voice AI recruiter](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter).

## What an AI voice agent does for a recruiter

- **First-contact calls.** It phones every new applicant, which is the part most likely to slip when a recruiter is busy.
- **Fixed screening questions.** It asks about notice period, location, availability and certificates the same way each time.
- **Role FAQs.** It answers pay range, shift pattern and next steps from your knowledge base.
- **Scheduling.** It books interviews on Google Calendar while the candidate is still on the phone.
- **Chasing.** It retries unanswered numbers up to 5 times within the hours you choose.
- **Write-up.** It leaves a transcript, a summary and a recording for each call.

## Who does what

| Task | Voice agent | Human recruiter |
|---|---|---|
| Calling every applicant promptly | Strong | Slows as volume grows |
| Asking identical questions | Strong | Varies by day and mood |
| Reading between the lines | Weak | Strong |
| Selling the role to a hesitant candidate | Limited | Strong |
| Salary and offer negotiation | Not suited | Owns it |
| Briefing a hiring manager | Not suited | Owns it |
| Out-of-hours first contact | Works within set hours | Not practical |

## A sample handoff

*Illustrative only.*

**Agent:** Thanks for those answers. You have the certification we need and you are free to start in two weeks. Would you like to speak with one of our recruiters now, or book a time?
**Caller:** Is someone free now?
**Agent:** I will check. One moment while I connect you.
**Agent (to recruiter, summary):** Candidate has the required certificate, can start in two weeks, asked about overtime rates.

The recruiter takes the call already knowing the answers and the one open question. If nobody is free, the agent books a call instead.

## What to set up first

1. Map your process and mark the steps where the same questions are asked every time.
2. Write the agent prompt around only those steps, and tell it plainly to avoid judging candidates.
3. Define the triggers for a warm transfer, such as a strong answer set or a candidate who asks for a person.
4. Make sure the opening says it is an AI assistant, and give candidates an easy way to ask for a human.
5. Set campaign hours that fit your candidates and keep retries within what you consider reasonable.
6. Test with your own phone and check that summaries are accurate enough to act on.

## Why the human recruiter still matters in an AI recruiter vs human recruiter workflow

Recruiting is partly persuasion. A candidate weighing two offers wants someone who understands their situation, and an agent working from a script cannot do that well. Recruiters also hold context the agent lacks: how a hiring manager really feels about a gap, which team is under pressure, what the company can bend on. Treat the agent as a way to free up recruiter hours for these conversations, not to remove them.

## Where it falls short

A voice agent will take answers at face value and cannot verify them. It may misunderstand a heavy accent or a poor line. It has no judgement about unusual career paths, and it can be thrown by candidates who ramble or answer a different question. It should not rank or reject candidates without a person reviewing the output, and local rules on automated decision-making and disclosure may apply. This is general information rather than legal advice, so check current rules where you hire.

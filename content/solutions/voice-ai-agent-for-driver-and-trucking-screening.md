Driver pre-screening AI handles the questions every trucking and delivery recruiter asks a hundred times a week: what licence do you hold, what have you driven, which routes do you want, and when can you start. Driver leads arrive at all hours and go cold quickly, so a fast first call matters. This page covers how an AI agent runs that screen and where a person still has to step in.

## What an AI voice agent does for driver screening

- **Calls leads fast.** Drivers often apply to several fleets at once; a same-day call keeps you in the running.
- **Captures licence and experience.** Class, endorsements, years on the road and equipment types, recorded as stated.
- **Matches route type.** Local, regional, over-the-road or last-mile, with the home-time pattern each driver expects.
- **Answers fleet questions.** Pay structure, sign-on terms, equipment age, home time and benefits from your knowledge base, nothing improvised.
- **Respects schedules.** If the driver is behind the wheel, the agent offers a callback window and tries again.
- **Hands over strong fits.** Warm-transfer to a recruiter if one is free, or book a call on your Google Calendar.

## Call outcomes at a glance

| Driver says | Agent does | Recruiter next step |
|---|---|---|
| Holds required class, matches lane | Books a recruiter call | Verify licence and record |
| Wants a different route type | Records preference, asks about flexibility | Decide if another opening fits |
| Cannot talk now | Offers a callback time, retries later | None until reached |
| Questions about pay or home time | Answers from knowledge base | None unless unanswered |
| Does not hold required class | Confirms once, closes politely | Review before final decision |

## A sample call

*Illustrative only.*

**Agent:** Hello, this is an AI assistant calling for a regional carrier about your driver application. Is now a good time, or are you on the road?

**Caller:** I'm at a rest stop, I've got ten minutes.

**Agent:** Thanks. What licence class do you hold, and any endorsements?

**Caller:** Class A, with tanker.

**Agent:** How many years have you driven tractor-trailers?

**Caller:** Six, mostly regional.

**Agent:** This opening is regional with home on weekends. Does that suit you?

**Caller:** That's what I want, honestly.

**Agent:** Good. I'll pass this on, and a recruiter can call you tomorrow afternoon. Does that work?

## What to set up first

1. **Write the opening precisely.** Licence class, endorsements, lane, schedule, equipment and pay structure.
2. **Decide the knockouts.** Licence class and a clean-enough record are common; keep them few and read [knockout questions](/solutions/voice-ai-agent-for-knockout-questions).
3. **Load the knowledge base.** Pay per mile or hour, sign-on terms, home time, benefits and how hiring proceeds.
4. **Set calling hours around drivers.** Early morning and evening, with up to five retries at spaced hours.
5. **Enable callbacks and booking.** Connect Google Calendar so recruiter slots are real.
6. **Turn on consent confirmation** and have the agent say it is an AI.
7. **Make a test call to your own phone** and check that the agent copes with road noise and short answers.

## Where it falls short

- It cannot verify licences, driving records or medical status. Those checks are yours.
- Poor mobile coverage on the road can garble calls. Offer a callback and read the transcript for anything unclear.
- Pay and home-time promises must match your actual offers. The agent will repeat whatever is in the knowledge base, so keep it accurate.
- Regulated transport roles carry compliance rules that vary by region. This is general information, not legal advice; confirm current requirements.

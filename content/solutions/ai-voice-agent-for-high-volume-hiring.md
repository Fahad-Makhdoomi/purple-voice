An AI voice agent for high volume hiring phones every applicant, asks your screening questions in a consistent order and books interviews with the people who qualify. It exists for one problem: when a single role draws hundreds of applications, recruiters cannot call all of them, so good candidates are missed or contacted too late. This page is for talent teams and agencies hiring in bulk.

## The arithmetic of volume

Say a role gets 400 applications and each screening call, including voicemails and callbacks, costs a recruiter about ten minutes. That is roughly 65 hours of phone work (illustrative numbers). In practice, recruiters sample the first batch, call the ones with the cleanest CVs, and leave the rest. The cost is not just time: you shortlist the people who applied first or wrote the best CV, not necessarily the best fit.

An agent turns that into a campaign. It calls the whole list in your calling hours, and recruiters read the results.

## What an AI voice agent does for high volume hiring

- **Calls everyone, not a sample.** Each applicant gets the same opening and the same questions, which makes comparison easier.
- **Runs knockout checks early.** Right to work, shift availability, minimum age or a required certificate are checked in the first minute.
- **Answers the repeated questions.** Pay, location, hours and start date come from your knowledge base, so applicants self-select out where the role does not fit.
- **Retries intelligently.** Up to 5 attempts at the times you set, rather than a single try on a Tuesday afternoon.
- **Books interviews or assessment days.** Qualified applicants choose a slot while on the line, using Google Calendar.
- **Records not-reached lists.** Campaign analytics show who never answered.
- **Summarises every call.** One short summary per applicant, with the transcript available when you want the detail.

## A sample call

Illustrative. Role: seasonal packing operative.

**Agent:** Hi, I am an AI assistant calling for the recruitment team about your application for the packing role. Is this a good moment?
**Applicant:** Yeah, I can talk.
**Agent:** The role is nights, Sunday to Thursday, 10 pm to 6 am, at the east distribution site. Does that pattern suit you?
**Applicant:** I thought it was days.
**Agent:** The advert listed both, but this vacancy is nights. Would you still like to be considered?
**Applicant:** No, I can only do days.
**Agent:** Thank you for being straightforward. I will note that, and we may contact you when a day shift opens. Is that all right?
**Applicant:** Yes, thanks.

That call took under a minute and saved a recruiter a conversation that was never going to progress.

## What to set up first

1. **Pick three to five questions that really matter.** Long scripts lose applicants. Keep them job-related and defensible.
2. **Write unambiguous knowledge-base facts** for pay, shifts, location and process. Applicants ask the same questions every time.
3. **Split large lists into batches.** Plans below Pro cap a campaign at 100 contacts, so stage the calling and learn from the first batch.
4. **Set calling hours and retries** that fit the audience, including retry windows in the evening where appropriate.
5. **Define the handoff.** Decide whether strong candidates are booked directly or transferred warm to a recruiter.
6. **Turn on consent confirmation and disclose that the caller is an AI.**
7. **Test-call your own phone,** including the awkward case of someone who changes their mind mid-call.

## Where it falls short

A call cannot verify documents, and a candidate who sounds confident may still not be right. Applicants with limited English or poor reception may be screened out unfairly if the agent misreads them, so review calls where the outcome was negative. Roles with a heavy skills test need a human assessor. And a campaign cannot fix a vague advert: if the pay or hours are unclear, you will simply screen out more people faster.

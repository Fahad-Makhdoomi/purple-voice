An AI voice agent for recruitment operations takes over the phone work that follows a script: first-contact screening, interview scheduling, confirmation calls and database re-engagement. Recruiters keep the work that needs judgement, such as assessing fit, handling concerns and closing offers. It suits agency owners, in-house talent teams and operations leads whose recruiters spend more hours dialling than deciding.

## Where recruiter time actually goes

Most recruiters would say their job is finding and persuading good people. Look at a calendar and the picture is different: dozens of short calls to check the same four facts, voicemails left and chased, interview slots negotiated back and forth, and reminder calls the day before. None of it is difficult. All of it is time-sensitive, and it scales with applicant volume rather than with recruiter headcount.

That is the shape of work a voice agent handles well. It is repetitive, bounded and benefits from fast response. It is also the work where a delay costs you: a candidate who applied this morning and was not called until Thursday may already have accepted elsewhere.

## What an AI voice agent does for recruitment operations

- **Screens applicants by phone.** It asks the questions you define (right to work, location, notice period, pay expectation, relevant experience) and records the answers in a call summary.
- **Books interviews.** It checks availability on Google Calendar and confirms a slot while the candidate is still on the line.
- **Chases no-replies.** Campaigns retry up to 5 times within the calling hours you set, so a missed first attempt does not end the conversation.
- **Answers role questions.** Using your knowledge base, it explains pay band, location, shift pattern or next steps without a recruiter on the line.
- **Runs reminder and confirmation calls.** A short call before an interview or start date catches problems early.
- **Revives old candidate records.** It calls people already in your database to check whether they are open to work now. See [candidate re-engagement calls](/solutions/ai-voice-agent-for-candidate-reengagement).
- **Hands over warm candidates.** When someone is a strong match or asks for a person, it transfers the call to a recruiter or flags it in the summary.

## What to automate and what to keep human

| Recruiting call | Automate? | What the agent does | Recruiter keeps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Application acknowledgement and basic screening | Yes | Asks fixed questions, summarises answers | Deciding who progresses |
| Interview scheduling | Yes | Offers slots from the calendar, confirms | Interview itself |
| Reminder and confirmation calls | Yes | Confirms, offers reschedule | Unusual cases |
| Database re-engagement | Yes | Checks interest and availability | Conversations with interested candidates |
| Client briefings and intake | Mostly no | Can take messages | The relationship and the brief |
| Offer negotiation | No | Nothing | Everything |
| Sensitive conversations (rejections after interview, concerns) | No | Nothing | Everything |

A useful rule: if the call has a script and a clear next step, automate it. If a good answer depends on reading the person, keep it human.

## A sample call

This is illustrative, with the details changed for a generic role.

**Agent:** Hello, this is the recruitment team calling on behalf of the hiring company about your application for the customer service role. Is now an okay time for a few quick questions? This call is handled by an AI assistant.
**Candidate:** Yes, go ahead.
**Agent:** Thanks. The role is based in the Leeds office, Monday to Friday. Does that location work for you?
**Candidate:** Yes, I live twenty minutes away.
**Agent:** Great. Are you able to start within a month, or is your notice period longer?
**Candidate:** Four weeks, so about a month.
**Agent:** That fits. I can book a video interview with a recruiter. Would Wednesday at 2 pm or Thursday at 10 am suit you better?
**Candidate:** Thursday morning.
**Agent:** Booked for Thursday at 10 am. A confirmation call will follow the day before. Anything you would like the recruiter to know in advance?

## What to set up first

1. **Write the agent prompt.** State the role, the company name, the questions in order and the tone. Our [AI voice agent prompt template](/resources/ai-voice-agent-prompt-template) gives a starting structure.
2. **Load the knowledge base.** Add pay range, location, benefits, shift pattern and the next steps in your process, so the agent answers from facts rather than guesses.
3. **Define the pass and handoff rules.** Decide what makes a candidate worth a warm transfer and what goes into the summary only.
4. **Connect Google Calendar.** Give the agent real interview slots to offer.
5. **Set campaign hours and retries.** Call at sensible local times and use up to 5 retries with retry hours.
6. **Turn on consent confirmation** at the start of the call, and disclose that the caller is an AI. See [how to disclose AI on calls](/blog/disclose-ai-on-calls).
7. **Test-call your own phone** several times, including interruptions and awkward answers, before any candidate hears it.

## Where it falls short

An agent does not judge cultural fit, notice hesitation the way an experienced recruiter would, or negotiate. Candidates with accents, poor lines or complex histories may produce transcripts that need a human read. Senior and specialist searches usually rely on relationship and trust, which a first call from an agent does not build. Some candidates prefer a person and will say so; the agent should offer a callback when they do.

Treat the summary as a screening aid, not a decision. Keep a recruiter responsible for who progresses, and review calls regularly for fairness. Our guide to [recruitment call compliance](/solutions/ai-voice-agent-for-recruitment-compliance) covers consent and fair-process points.

AI outbound calling compliance comes down to five questions: did the person agree to be called, are they on a do-not-call list, is the call within allowed hours, have you been open about who is calling, and can you prove it afterwards. The answers depend on where you and the recipient are and on what kind of call it is. This page is general information, not legal advice, and rules change, so check current requirements with the relevant regulator or a qualified adviser before you launch. For the system itself, see the [AI outbound calling system overview](/solutions/ai-outbound-calling-system).

## The five checks for AI outbound calling compliance

- **Consent and basis.** Do you have a lawful basis to call this number for this purpose? Marketing calls usually need more than service calls.
- **Do-not-call screening.** Check national or regional registers where they apply, and your own internal suppression list.
- **Calling hours.** Many places restrict calling before a morning cutoff or after an evening cutoff in the recipient's local time.
- **Identification and disclosure.** Say who is calling, and say it is an AI assistant. Where required, provide contact details.
- **Records and opt-outs.** Log consent, call times and requests to stop, and honour them quickly.

## A rough map of the major regimes

| Region | Commonly referenced rules | What it generally touches |
|---|---|---|
| United States | TCPA, state mini-TCPAs, National Do Not Call Registry, Telemarketing Sales Rule | Consent for automated or artificial-voice calls, DNC, hours, identification |
| United Kingdom | PECR, UK GDPR, TPS and CTPS, Ofcom rules on persistent misuse | Marketing call consent, TPS screening, caller ID, silent and abandoned calls |
| European Union | ePrivacy rules as implemented nationally, GDPR | Consent for automated marketing, data protection, national opt-out lists |
| Canada | CRTC unsolicited telecommunications rules, National DNCL | Registration, DNCL screening, hours, identification |
| Australia | Do Not Call Register Act, Telecommunications rules | Register screening, permitted hours, identification |

This table points to topics, not requirements, and detail varies by state and country. Use it as a list of things to research. The [TCPA post](/blog/tcpa-and-ai-voice-calls) goes deeper on the US, and the [sales call compliance checklist](/resources/sales-call-compliance-checklist) is a printable starting point.

## A sample call

Illustrative opening and opt-out handling for a service follow-up.

**Agent:** Hello, is this Janet? This is an AI assistant calling for a home energy company about the survey you completed. This call may be recorded. Is now a good time?

**Caller:** I do not want any more calls from you.

**Agent:** Understood. I will make sure you are not called again. Thank you for your time.

**System note:** Contact flagged as do-not-call in the campaign, summary recorded.

## What to set up first

1. **Classify the call.** Marketing, service, reminder, debt, survey or recruitment calls are treated differently.
2. **Audit your list source.** For each number, know where it came from and what the person agreed to. Drop anything you cannot explain.
3. **Screen against registers** that apply to the countries you call.
4. **Set calling hours** in the campaign inside the legal window for the contact's local time.
5. **Add disclosure** to the opening and, if you record, a recording notice. The [disclosure guide](/blog/disclose-ai-on-calls) has wording ideas.
6. **Turn on consent confirmation** in the campaign if your process needs a confirmation step.
7. **Write an opt-out rule** so a request to stop is honoured immediately and carried into future campaigns.
8. **Keep retries modest.** Up to 5 are available, but fewer reduce complaints.
9. **Test on your own phone** including "stop calling me" and "who gave you my number."
10. **Review transcripts** of early calls for anything that overpromises or sounds misleading.

## Where compliance guidance falls short

- **Rules change.** Regulators update guidance on AI voices and automated calls. Anything on this page may be out of date.
- **Local detail matters.** State, provincial and sector rules, such as financial services or healthcare, can be stricter than national ones.
- **Consent quality is the usual weak point.** A pre-ticked box on an old form is often weaker than it looks.
- **Cross-border calling is complex.** Calling people in other countries can bring that country's rules into play.
- **This page cannot judge your situation.** Treat it as a list of questions for your adviser.

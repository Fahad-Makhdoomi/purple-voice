Outbound appointment reminder calls ask a person to confirm, move or cancel a booking while there is still time to fill the slot. An AI voice agent can make those calls in the hours before the day starts, record each answer and leave your team with a short list of exceptions. It suits clinics, salons, garages, tutors, installers and any business where an empty slot costs money. For the wider setup, see the [AI outbound calling system overview](/solutions/ai-outbound-calling-system).

## What outbound appointment reminder calls cover

- **Confirm attendance.** The agent states the day, time and service, and asks the person to confirm.
- **Offer to reschedule.** If they cannot attend, the agent proposes open slots and moves the booking.
- **Take cancellations cleanly.** Releasing a slot early is better than a silent no-show.
- **Pass on instructions.** Arrive ten minutes early, bring documents, fasting before a test. These come from your knowledge base.
- **Flag special cases.** A person who says they are unwell or confused about the booking goes to staff.
- **Call a waiting list.** Offer freed slots to people who asked to be told.
- **Log everything.** Each call has a transcript and summary, so the morning team knows who is coming.

## Reminder methods compared

| Method | Effort to respond | What you learn | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Text reminder | Low, easy to ignore | Often nothing unless they reply | Light-touch nudges |
| Voicemail-only call | None | Nothing | Rarely enough |
| Human reminder call | High for staff | Full conversation | Small lists, sensitive bookings |
| AI reminder call | Moderate for caller | Confirm, move or cancel recorded | Daily volume with some uncertainty |

## A sample call

Illustrative, for a dental practice style booking. No clinical advice is given.

**Agent:** Hello, is that Ben? This is the reminder assistant for the practice. You have a check-up tomorrow at 9:15. Will you be able to make it?

**Caller:** Actually, I have a meeting. Can I move it?

**Agent:** Of course. I have Thursday at 8:30 or Friday at 4:00. Does either work?

**Caller:** Friday at 4.

**Agent:** I have moved you to Friday at 4:00 and released tomorrow's slot. You will get a confirmation. Is there anything else I can note for the team?

**Caller:** No, that is all.

## What to set up first

1. **Decide the timing.** One call the day before, and optionally a second shortly before, depending on how people book.
2. **Choose calling hours** that fit your clients, and keep retries modest, for example 2 or 3 of the maximum 5 within the same day.
3. **Prepare the list from your booking system.** Number, name, time, service and location are the fields that matter.
4. **Add practical details to the knowledge base**: address, parking, preparation, cancellation policy.
5. **Connect Google Calendar** so moves and cancellations are written back.
6. **Create a transfer rule** for people who want to speak to reception.
7. **Call your own number first**, accept, decline and ask an odd question.

## Where AI reminder calls fall short

- **They depend on your booking data.** If times are wrong in the list, the agent will confidently state the wrong time. Export fresh data each day.
- **Medical, legal or financial sensitivity.** Do not discuss clinical or confidential details on automated calls. Keep it to logistics.
- **Not everyone wants a call.** Some prefer messages. Offer an opt-out and respect it.
- **Consent still applies.** Reminder calls often rest on an existing customer relationship, but rules differ by region. This is general information, not legal advice, so check your obligations, and see the [compliance overview](/solutions/ai-outbound-calling-compliance).
- **Reschedule logic is only as good as the calendar.** Complex multi-resource bookings, such as a specific room plus a specific practitioner, may need a person.

## A simple way to measure it

Pick one service and one week. Compare no-show rate for reminded and non-reminded groups, or against the previous four weeks. Count how many slots were released early enough to be refilled. Treat the result as your own illustrative baseline, not a promise.

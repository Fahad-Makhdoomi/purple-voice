AI survey calls let a voice agent phone customers shortly after an experience, ask a handful of questions and record what they say. The value is in the spoken comment, which is usually more specific than a tick-box. They work best for short feedback and NPS-style questions, with unhappy customers routed to a person quickly. For the broader system, see the [AI outbound calling system overview](/solutions/ai-outbound-calling-system).

## What AI survey calls can collect

- **A rating question.** For example, "On a scale of 0 to 10, how likely are you to recommend us?" The agent records the number.
- **One open question.** "What is the main reason for that score?" Spoken answers often include detail a form would not capture.
- **Service-specific checks.** Was the technician on time, was the problem fixed, was the price as quoted.
- **Intent signals.** Whether the customer would rebook, or needs a follow-up.
- **Escalation triggers.** Low scores or complaints that should reach a human the same day.
- **Consistent delivery.** The same question wording on every call, which makes comparison easier.

## Survey type and call design

| Survey type | Questions | Length | Escalation rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| NPS after purchase | 1 score, 1 reason | Under 90 seconds | Score 0 to 6 flagged for review |
| Post-service feedback | 3 to 4 short questions | About 2 minutes | Unresolved issue goes to a person |
| Event follow-up | 2 questions plus interest in next event | About 1 minute | Interest passed to the organiser |
| Churn check | 2 to 3 questions, open reason | About 2 minutes | Retention call offered to the account owner |

## A sample call

Illustrative, for a bicycle repair shop.

**Agent:** Hello, is this Sofia? I am an AI assistant calling for the bike workshop. You collected your bike on Monday. Do you have two minutes for three quick questions about the service?

**Caller:** Okay.

**Agent:** On a scale of zero to ten, how likely are you to recommend the workshop to a friend?

**Caller:** Maybe a seven.

**Agent:** Thank you. What was the main reason for that?

**Caller:** The work was good, but I waited longer than they said.

**Agent:** That is useful to know. Would you like someone from the workshop to call you about the wait?

**Caller:** No, it is fine. Just tell them.

**Agent:** I will pass that on. Thanks for your time.

## What to set up first

1. **Write the questions as speech.** Short, no jargon, one idea each.
2. **State the length and the purpose** at the start, and say it is an AI assistant calling for your company.
3. **Upload a recent list.** Calling within a few days of the experience beats a month later.
4. **Choose calling hours** at the times customers answer, often early evening for consumers and mid-morning for businesses.
5. **Use 1 or 2 retries**, not the maximum of 5, since survey fatigue is real.
6. **Add a transfer or follow-up rule** for scores below your threshold.
7. **Test with friends** who give vague answers, and see how the agent reacts.

## Reading the results

Each call produces a transcript, an AI summary and a recording. For small surveys, read a sample yourself to confirm that scores were captured correctly. Group comments by theme and count how many customers raised each. On Pro, Insights and webhooks can help you move results into a spreadsheet or CRM. Report numbers with their sample size so nobody reads too much into twelve answers.

## Where AI survey calls fall short

- **People may refuse or hang up.** Phone surveys reach a different subset from email surveys, so avoid claiming the result represents everyone.
- **Spoken ratings can be unreliable.** Some people give a number to end the call. Pair the score with the open question.
- **Sensitive topics need care.** Health, finance or complaints may need a person, not an agent.
- **Survey calls are not exempt from calling rules.** Some regions treat them differently from marketing, others do not. This is general information, not legal advice, so check current rules and read the [compliance overview](/solutions/ai-outbound-calling-compliance).
- **Speech recognition is imperfect.** Accents, noise and cross-talk can cause errors, so check transcripts before quoting customers.

If a survey turns up an unhappy customer who might still be saved, a quick human callback beats a second automated call.

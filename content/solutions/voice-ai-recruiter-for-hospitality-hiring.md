An AI recruiter for hospitality is a voice agent that phones applicants for kitchen, bar, front-of-house and housekeeping roles, checks whether they can work your shifts and books a trial or interview. It is built for restaurant groups, hotels, bars and agencies where managers are on the floor and cannot spend an afternoon on the phone.

For the wider concept, read [what a voice AI recruiter is](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter).

## What an AI recruiter does for hospitality hiring

- **Calls within minutes.** Applicants often apply to several venues at once, and the first useful contact tends to win.
- **Checks shift fit.** Weekends, split shifts, late finishes and distance from the venue are asked about before anyone books a visit.
- **Confirms basic eligibility.** Right to work, age requirements for bar roles, and any food hygiene or licence requirements you specify.
- **Answers practical questions.** Pay, tips policy, uniform, location, transport, staff meals, whatever you add to the knowledge base.
- **Books trial shifts.** It offers open slots from Google Calendar and confirms one.
- **Reduces wasted trials.** Candidates who cannot work the hours are identified before they take a slot.
- **Reports clearly.** The manager sees a short summary and can listen to the recording.

## Call type to action

| Applicant says | Agent does | Hands off when |
|---|---|---|
| Free evenings and weekends | Offers a trial slot | Manager meets them on trial |
| Can only work mornings | Notes it, checks if a morning role exists | Manager decides on fit |
| Asks about tips | Answers from knowledge base | Question goes beyond the policy |
| Not sure about the commute | Gives the address, offers a call back | Applicant asks for a person |
| Wants a manager now | Tries a warm transfer | No manager is free, so it books a call |

## A sample call

*Illustrative only.*

**Agent:** Hello, this is an AI assistant calling about your application for the bar team role. Is this a good moment for a few quick questions?
**Caller:** I have got a minute.
**Agent:** Can you do Friday and Saturday evenings, finishing around midnight?
**Caller:** Yes, I can do that.
**Agent:** Do you have bar experience?
**Caller:** Two summers at a pub.
**Agent:** That helps. We run trial shifts on Thursday evenings. Would this Thursday at five work?
**Caller:** Thursday works.
**Agent:** Booked. Please wear dark trousers and bring photo ID.

## What to set up first

1. Put the real shift pattern, pay range and address in the knowledge base so the agent does not improvise.
2. Write questions in plain, quick language. Hospitality applicants dislike long calls.
3. Choose calling hours that avoid service times, often late morning and mid-afternoon.
4. Set retries up to 5 with retry hours that catch people between jobs.
5. Connect Google Calendar and mark trial shift slots as the available times.
6. Confirm the opening says it is an AI assistant and that candidates can ask for a person.
7. Test-call yourself and say you can only work Tuesdays to see how it handles a poor fit.

## Where it falls short

It cannot judge personality, warmth or how someone handles a rush, which matter more in hospitality than a CV does. It will not see a candidate's presentation or how they talk to guests in person. Some applicants are put off by a phone call from an AI and prefer a text conversation, and Purple Voice calls rather than messages. Always keep a person for the trial shift and the final decision. Check current rules on calling applicants and on age or right-to-work checks in your area.

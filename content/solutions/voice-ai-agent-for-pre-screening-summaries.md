Pre-screening call summaries are the part of an automated screen that your team actually touches. The agent makes the call, but a reviewer decides who goes forward, and that decision is only as good as the notes in front of them. A good summary is short, structured and honest about what is unclear, with the transcript and recording one click away for anything that matters.

## What a good summary does for reviewers

- **Puts the decision-relevant facts first.** Availability, location, requirement met or not, and the next step the agent suggests.
- **Uses the same fields every time.** Scanning twenty summaries is fast when the layout never changes.
- **Quotes what matters.** A direct phrase from the caller, such as "only two Saturdays a month", beats a paraphrase.
- **Flags uncertainty.** "Unclear: licence expiry" is more useful than a confident guess.
- **Notes questions the caller asked.** Unanswered ones become your follow-up list.
- **Links the evidence.** Transcript and recording per call, so a reviewer can verify in seconds.
- **Supports comparison.** Because every applicant answered the same questions, summaries line up side by side.

## Anatomy of a summary

| Field | What it holds | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome | Agent's suggested next step | Offered interview, accepted Thursday |
| Requirements | Each stated requirement and the answer | Night shifts: yes. Licence: says current |
| Availability | Start date and constraints | Two weeks' notice, no first Sunday |
| Caller questions | What they asked and whether it was answered | Asked about parking, not in knowledge base |
| Flags | Anything uncertain or sensitive | Line quality poor on licence answer |
| Evidence | Links to transcript and recording | Call detail |

## A sample summary

*Illustrative only.*

**Outcome:** Interview booked, Thursday 10:00.
**Requirements:** Nights Sunday to Thursday, yes with one exception. Forklift licence, says current. Team lead experience, eight people.
**Availability:** Two weeks' notice. Cannot do the first Sunday each month.
**Caller questions:** Asked about parking (answered) and sick pay (not in knowledge base, needs follow-up).
**Flags:** Licence expiry date not stated, verify before the interview.
**Evidence:** Transcript and recording in the call record.

A reviewer can act on that in under a minute and knows exactly what to check.

## What to set up first

1. **Pick the fields.** Take them from what your reviewers ask on every first call.
2. **Write them into the agent prompt.** Say how to record answers, and to write "unclear" rather than infer.
3. **Make wording consistent.** For example "requirement met", "not met", "unclear" so scanning is easy.
4. **Test on your own phone.** Give mixed, vague and contradictory answers and read what comes back.
5. **Agree a review habit.** Who reads summaries, how soon and what triggers listening to the recording.
6. **Use webhooks and automations if you are on Pro.** Send summaries into your ATS, CRM or spreadsheet.
7. **Add consent and disclosure** so recording and transcription are expected by the caller.

## Where it falls short

- Summaries compress. Tone, hesitation and context can vanish, so keep recordings available.
- Transcription errors, especially of names, numbers and accents, can flow into the summary.
- The agent can only summarise what it asked. A missing question means a missing field.
- Recordings and transcripts hold personal data. Retention and consent rules apply. This is general information, not legal advice; check current requirements.

After hours AI IVR answers the calls that arrive when your office is closed and turns them into bookings, messages or urgent transfers, instead of a voicemail that someone may or may not return. It is for any business whose customers call in the evening or at weekends: trades, practices, property firms, and shops with online customers.

The usual picture is a Monday inbox of voicemails, half of them already gone elsewhere. The agent changes what you find on Monday morning.

## What the agent does when you are closed

- **Greets the caller properly.** It says who it is, that it is an AI, and how it can help.
- **Answers the common questions.** Hours, prices, location, service areas, from your knowledge base.
- **Books the next slot.** Appointments go straight onto Google Calendar.
- **Captures the enquiry.** Name, number, what they want, how soon.
- **Flags urgent calls.** If the caller describes an emergency, the agent follows your rule and can warm transfer to an on-call number.
- **Leaves a summary.** You read two lines per call, not a stack of crackly recordings.

## Voicemail against an agent

| | Voicemail | After hours AI IVR |
|---|---|---|
| Caller experience | Beep, speak to no one | Short conversation |
| Questions answered | None | Common ones |
| Booking | You call back later | Booked on the call |
| Urgent triage | You find out in the morning | Transferred or flagged |
| Your morning | Listen to recordings | Read summaries |
| Lost enquiries | Many hang up | Fewer |

## Why after hours AI IVR often pays for itself

Callers outside business hours are often the most motivated: someone with a leak, a person comparing options after work, a customer who finally has time. If they reach a recording, a competitor with an answer wins. Illustrative arithmetic: if you miss ten after-hours calls a week and just two are real jobs worth $150 each, that is $300 a week in work you never see. Your own numbers will differ, so measure them with the [ROI calculator](/resources/ai-calling-roi-calculator).

## A sample call

Illustrative, for a letting agency at 8:40 pm.

Agent: Hello, you've reached the lettings office. We're closed now, but I'm an AI assistant and I can help. What's the call about?
Caller: I'm interested in the two-bed on Fenwick Road. Can I see it?
Agent: I can arrange a viewing. Are you free on Saturday morning?
Caller: 10 would be good.
Agent: Saturday at 10 is available. What name and number should I put on the booking?
Caller: Alex, and it's ...
Agent: Thanks, Alex. A colleague will confirm in the morning, and you'll get a call if anything changes.

## What to set up first

1. **Decide the coverage window** and how calls reach the agent, such as forwarding from your main number when you close.
2. **Write an honest opener**: closed, an AI, what it can do.
3. **Load the knowledge base** with the answers asked most after hours.
4. **Connect Google Calendar** with only the slots you are happy to have booked unseen.
5. **Define urgent** with examples, give the on-call number, and write the fallback if it rings out.
6. **Add consent wording** per [AI IVR recording and consent](/solutions/ai-ivr-compliance-and-recording).
7. **Test at night** from your own phone, then check the transcript the next morning.

## Where it falls short

It cannot resolve things only a person can: a custom quote, a dispute, an emergency needing someone on site. If you promise urgent response, you still need a real on-call person; the agent only finds them faster. Callers who are upset at midnight may not accept an AI, and should have a path to leave a message for a human. Also, overnight bookings need rules, so you are not surprised by a Sunday 7 am slot nobody wanted.

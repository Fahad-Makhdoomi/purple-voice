An AI voice agent for customer care answers the routine calls on your support line, such as opening hours, delivery questions and appointment changes, and passes anything that needs judgment to a person. It suits support leads, practice managers, ecommerce owners and operations teams whose phones ring all day with the same ten questions.

The aim is not to remove people from customer care. It is to make sure a caller is answered quickly, gets a correct answer to a simple question, and reaches a human with context when the question is not simple.

## What an AI voice agent does for customer care

A well-scoped agent covers a defined list of jobs rather than trying to be everything. These are the ones that tend to work.

- **Answers common questions from a knowledge base.** Hours, locations, pricing pages, policies and process steps, in the words you wrote. See [AI customer support phone agent](/solutions/ai-voice-agent-for-customer-support-calls) for how to build that knowledge.
- **Covers the hours nobody is at the desk.** Evenings, weekends and bank holidays get an answer instead of voicemail. More on this in [after-hours customer support](/solutions/ai-voice-agent-for-after-hours-support).
- **Gives order and delivery information.** Explains tracking, delivery windows and what to do about a late parcel. [Order status calls](/solutions/ai-voice-agent-for-order-status-calls) covers the limits.
- **Books, confirms and moves appointments.** Using Google Calendar booking, and outbound reminders that ask the person to confirm or reschedule.
- **Follows up on calls you missed.** Calls back people who rang when everyone was busy. See [missed call follow-up](/solutions/ai-voice-agent-for-missed-calls).
- **Transfers when it should.** Warm transfer to a human, with a summary, for complaints, exceptions and anything the agent was told to leave alone.
- **Leaves a record.** Every call has a transcript, an AI summary and a recording in the Calls section of the dashboard.

## What to automate and what to keep human

Sorting calls before you build anything is the most useful hour of the project. A rough guide:

| Call type | Agent handles it | Hand off to a person |
|---|---|---|
| Opening hours, address, directions | Yes, fully | Never needed |
| Appointment booking or rescheduling | Yes, with calendar booking | When the slot rules are unusual |
| "Where is my order" | Explains tracking and process | When it is lost or the customer is upset |
| Return or exchange policy | Explains the policy | When the case is outside policy |
| Billing disputes and refunds | Takes details, books a callback | Always |
| Complaints | Listens, records, escalates | Always |
| Vulnerable or distressed callers | Transfers immediately | Always |

The pattern is simple. If the answer is the same for every caller, automate it. If the answer depends on judgment, money or emotion, route it to a person early.

## A sample call

This exchange is illustrative and uses no real brand.

Agent: Hello, you have reached the support line. I am an AI assistant, and I can help with common questions or pass you to the team. How can I help?

Caller: I need to move my appointment on Thursday.

Agent: I can do that. What day would suit you better?

Caller: Friday morning if possible.

Agent: There is a slot at ten past nine on Friday. Shall I move you to that?

Caller: Yes. And I also want to complain about the last visit.

Agent: I am sorry to hear that. I will move your appointment now and then put you through to someone who can listen properly. One moment.

The agent handled the routine part, recognised the complaint as something it should not manage, and transferred with a summary so the person picking up already knew the context.

## What to set up first

1. **Agent prompt.** State what the agent may answer, what it must never promise, and how it speaks. The [AI voice agent prompt template](/resources/ai-voice-agent-prompt-template) is a starting point.
2. **Knowledge base.** Add real answers for your top ten questions. Short, factual, current.
3. **Handoff rules.** Name the topics and phrases that trigger a warm transfer, and the person or number that receives it. See [AI to human handoff](/solutions/ai-voice-agent-for-customer-care-handoff) and the guide to [warm transfer](/blog/warm-transfer-ai-to-human).
4. **Calendar.** Connect Google Calendar if the agent will book or move appointments.
5. **Disclosure and consent.** Add an opening line that says it is an AI, and confirm consent for any outbound calling campaign.
6. **Test calls.** Use the test-call feature to ring the agent from your own phone. Ask the awkward questions on purpose.
7. **Review summaries.** Read the first week of transcripts and summaries every day, and fix the knowledge base wherever the agent hesitated.

## Where it falls short

An AI voice agent is only as accurate as the knowledge you give it. If your policy changed last week and the knowledge base did not, the agent will state the old policy confidently. Someone needs to own that content.

It does not decide exceptions, approve refunds or read a room the way an experienced agent does. Callers with unusual accents, bad lines or several problems at once can trip it. It is not a clinical, legal or financial adviser, and it should not be set up to act as one.

It also works best on a voice channel that already exists. If most of your customers contact you in writing, a phone agent will not move that. Voice suits the people who ring.

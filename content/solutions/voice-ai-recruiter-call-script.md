A good AI recruiter call script is short, clear and honest about being an AI. It opens with who is calling and why, asks five or fewer questions that change your decision, answers a few predictable queries and knows when to stop. Below is a script and the prompt behind it, written for recruiters and hiring managers who want something to adapt rather than a blank page.

For the bigger picture, see the [voice AI recruiter overview](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter).

## What a good script does

- **Opens with disclosure.** It says it is an AI assistant, names the company and the role, and states how long the call will take.
- **Asks permission to continue.** A quick check that now is a good time avoids pushing through an unwelcome call.
- **Orders questions sensibly.** Easy facts first, sensitive topics such as pay later, and optional questions last.
- **Handles interruptions.** It stops talking when the candidate speaks and returns to the point afterward.
- **Has fixed answers.** Pay range, location, hours and process come from your knowledge base.
- **Has an exit.** It can end politely, offer a recruiter call, or transfer when conditions are met.

## Script structure at a glance

| Section | Purpose | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Disclose and set expectations | "This is an AI assistant calling for [company] about the [role]. It takes about three minutes." |
| Permission | Respect the candidate's time | "Is now a good moment?" |
| Questions | Gather decision facts | "Which days can you work?" |
| FAQs | Answer common queries | "The hourly range is [range]." |
| Close | Next step | "I can book a call with the team. Which day suits you?" |
| Exit | Handle refusal or opt-out | "No problem, I will note that and not call again." |

## A sample call

*Illustrative only, for a customer support role.*

**Agent:** Hello, this is an AI assistant calling for a company about your application for the customer support role. It will take about three minutes. Is now a good time?
**Caller:** Okay, go on.
**Agent:** Are you able to work evenings and some weekends?
**Caller:** Evenings yes, weekends only occasionally.
**Agent:** Noted. Have you worked in a customer-facing role before?
**Caller:** Yes, two years in retail.
**Agent:** Thank you. What notice do you need to give?
**Caller:** Two weeks.
**Agent:** I can book a call with a recruiter this week. Would Wednesday at three work?

## A prompt to start from

Adapt this and keep it short:

> You are a friendly AI assistant calling on behalf of [company] about the [role]. State at the start that you are an AI assistant. Ask permission to continue. Ask these questions in order: availability, relevant experience, notice period, location. Answer questions about pay, hours and process only from the knowledge base. If you do not know, say so and offer a recruiter call. Do not promise a job or discuss offers. If the candidate asks for a human, offer a transfer or book a call. If the candidate says stop, end the call and note the request.

## What to set up first

1. Paste the prompt into the agent editor and fill every bracket with real details.
2. Add the knowledge base: pay range, hours, location, benefits, interview process.
3. Choose a voice and listen for pacing that suits the role.
4. Use the test call on your own phone and try an interruption, a silence and an off-topic question.
5. Set campaign hours and retries, up to 5, then confirm consent for contacting each person.
6. Check the first ten transcripts and edit the prompt wherever the agent drifted.

## Tuning an ai recruiter call script over time

Read transcripts for the places candidates hesitate or misunderstand, then reword the question. If many people say "can you repeat that", shorten the sentence. If calls run long, cut a question. If you are comparing performance, the [metrics guide](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter-metrics) lists what to track. Candidates also react to tone, covered in [candidate experience](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter-candidate-experience).

## Where it falls short

A script cannot cover every conversation. Candidates give long, tangled answers, and an agent can misread them. It should not ask about protected characteristics or anything you would not ask a person, and it should not promise what you have not approved. Have a human review the script for fairness and for local rules on disclosure and recording, which are general information here and not legal advice.

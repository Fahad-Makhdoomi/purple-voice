To qualify home service leads with AI, you let a voice agent answer calls and phone enquiries while you are under a sink or up a ladder, then have it collect the job type, location and urgency, and book visits or alert you to emergencies. It is for small trades firms, cleaners, landscapers and local service companies where missed calls are lost jobs. The wider method sits in [AI call qualification](/solutions/ai-call-qualification).

## What an AI voice agent does for home service leads

- **Answers every call.** Customers ring several firms in a row; the one that answers usually gets the job.
- **Checks the postcode first.** Out-of-area jobs are filtered before anyone wastes time.
- **Identifies the job.** Leak, rewire, boiler service, gutter clean: the agent records it in the customer's words.
- **Separates urgent from routine.** Emergencies are flagged and routed; routine work is booked.
- **Answers basic questions.** Opening hours, call-out fees and what you do not cover come from the knowledge base.
- **Books site visits.** Google Calendar slots are offered and confirmed.
- **Sends you a summary.** At the end of the day you read what came in instead of listening to voicemails.

## Call type to action

| Call type | What the agent asks | What happens next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emergency | Address, what is happening, is anyone at risk | Transfer to on-call or alert, safety note if relevant |
| Routine repair | Job, access, photos available, preferred time | Visit booked |
| Quote request | Size of job, property type, rough timescale | Estimate visit booked |
| Out of area | Postcode | Polite decline, logged |
| Price shopper | Which job, rough scope | Published ranges, offer a visit |

Keep emergency rules short and unambiguous. If a caller mentions gas smell, fire or danger to life, the agent should tell them to contact emergency services first and not wait for you.

## A sample call

*Illustrative only. Figures and trade are made up for the example.*

**Agent:** Hello, you've reached a local plumbing company. I'm the automated assistant. How can I help?
**Caller:** My kitchen tap is leaking under the sink and it's getting worse.
**Agent:** I'm sorry to hear that. Can you tell me your postcode?
**Caller:** It's a central one, within your area, I think.
**Agent:** Yes, we cover that. Is water actually flooding the floor right now?
**Caller:** Just dripping steadily into a bowl.
**Agent:** Understood. Is the stop tap under the sink easy to reach? Turning it off will stop the drip until we arrive.
**Caller:** Yes, I'll do that.
**Agent:** We have a visit available this afternoon at two or tomorrow at nine. Which would you prefer?

## What to set up first

1. List the services you take and the postcodes you cover, and write what you refuse.
2. Define an emergency in a sentence, plus the transfer or alert route for one.
3. Write the intake questions: job, address, access, urgency, best time.
4. Add call-out fees, rough price ranges, hours and parking or access notes to the knowledge base.
5. Connect Google Calendar with realistic visit windows, including travel time.
6. For web enquiries, upload them as a campaign with calling hours and retries up to 5.
7. Test-call your own phone with an emergency, a quote and an out-of-area caller.

## Where it falls short

- It cannot see the problem. Descriptions are vague, and some jobs will turn out different on site.
- It should not give technical or safety advice beyond the lines you write. Anything risky goes to a person.
- Price estimates from a phone call can mislead. Stay with published ranges and book a visit for firm figures.
- Customers with access issues, tenants without the landlord's approval and similar situations need a human decision.
- Peak weeks, like a cold snap, may need more calendar capacity than the agent can create.

## Making the most of the first week

Forward your business line to the agent for a week and keep a simple tally: calls answered, jobs booked, jobs declined for area, and emergencies routed. Most owners are surprised by two things. First, how many calls arrived while they were on site or after hours. Second, how many were price checks that never turn into jobs. Use the tally to tighten the knowledge base, for example by adding a clear call-out fee line, so the agent can answer the price question honestly and move on. Read a handful of summaries each evening and adjust the prompt where the agent asked something awkward or missed something you needed, such as parking, access codes or whether pets are in the house.

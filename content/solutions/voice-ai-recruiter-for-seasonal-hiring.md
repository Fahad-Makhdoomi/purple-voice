Seasonal hiring AI calls solve a timing problem. A retailer, warehouse or events company might need dozens of temporary staff within a few weeks, and the applications arrive faster than a small team can phone. A voice agent calls each applicant, checks the basics and books the next step, so the team spends its hours on interviews and decisions.

If you are new to the idea, read the [voice AI recruiter overview](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter) first.

## What the agent does when volume spikes

- **Contacts every applicant.** No one waits in a queue because the recruiter ran out of afternoon.
- **Checks availability windows.** Start date, end date, weekend and evening cover, and any blackout dates.
- **Confirms eligibility.** Right to work, minimum age, required licences or certificates you specify.
- **Checks location.** Distance to the site and transport, so you avoid booking people who cannot get there.
- **Books interviews or group sessions.** Slots come from Google Calendar.
- **Retries politely.** Up to 5 attempts with retry hours, so you reach people who did not pick up the first time.
- **Gives you the not-reached list.** Others can be followed up manually.

## Peak-week planning table

| Phase | What happens | Agent's role |
|---|---|---|
| Two weeks before | Roles posted, first applications arrive | Pilot with a small batch, tune the script |
| Launch week | Applications climb daily | Call new applicants in daily batches |
| Peak | Interviews and offers overlap | Book slots, run reminders, surface warm candidates |
| Wind-down | Remaining roles are hard to fill | Re-call not-reached and earlier applicants |

## A sample call

*Illustrative only.*

**Agent:** Hello, I am an AI assistant calling about your application for temporary warehouse work this winter. Do you have a minute?
**Caller:** Yes.
**Agent:** Are you available from the first week of November through to the end of December?
**Caller:** I can do until the twentieth.
**Agent:** Thanks, I have noted that. Can you manage evening shifts until ten?
**Caller:** Yes, evenings are good.
**Agent:** Great. We are holding short interviews on Wednesday and Thursday. Which is better for you?
**Caller:** Wednesday.
**Agent:** I have you down for Wednesday morning. A confirmation is coming through now.

## What to set up first

1. Run a pilot on a few early applicants and fix any misheard questions before the surge.
2. Write one agent prompt per type of role if the questions differ, such as pickers and drivers.
3. Put dates, pay, site address and shift patterns in the knowledge base.
4. Set calling hours across weekdays and early evenings, and spread retries over different times.
5. Check how many calls your phone provider and plan allow at once.
6. Keep a person on call for warm transfers when a strong candidate wants to talk now.
7. Test-call yourself with an awkward availability answer.

## Where it falls short

A surge magnifies mistakes. A wrong shift time in the prompt reaches hundreds of people before you notice, so monitor early calls closely. The agent cannot judge reliability, and temporary roles often depend on turning up. Candidates who do not answer unknown numbers will be missed, and a stretched team may still be the bottleneck at the interview stage.

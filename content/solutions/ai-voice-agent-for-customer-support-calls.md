An AI customer support phone agent answers the repeat questions that fill a support line, using a knowledge base you control, and hands anything unusual to a person. It is for support managers and small teams who notice that most of the calls they take are variations on a short list.

The quality of the result depends far more on the knowledge you write than on the technology. A voice agent with a vague knowledge base sounds fluent and says little. One with a tight, current knowledge base sounds like your best front-line person on a good day.

## What an AI customer support phone agent does

- **Answers FAQs in plain speech.** Pricing structure, plan differences, account steps, policies, locations and hours.
- **Walks callers through simple procedures.** Resetting a setting, finding an invoice, where to click, read out in steps the caller can follow.
- **Collects details before a transfer.** Name, account reference and reason for calling, so the human does not start cold.
- **Handles interruptions.** Callers talk over, correct themselves and ask side questions. The agent adapts rather than reading a script.
- **Admits what it does not know.** With the right prompt, it says so and offers a callback or a transfer.
- **Records every call.** Transcript, AI summary and recording sit in the Calls section for review.

## Writing a knowledge base for a voice agent

Written support content is built to be read. Voice content is built to be heard. The difference matters.

| Written help article | Voice-ready version |
|---|---|
| "See the table below for plan limits" | "The Basic plan includes one agent and one number. Pro includes unlimited of both." |
| Long paragraph with three exceptions | One sentence for the rule, a separate sentence for each exception |
| "Click Settings, then Billing, then Invoices" | "Open Settings. Choose Billing. Your invoices are listed there." |
| Links to other pages | The relevant answer stated in full |

Keep each entry short and factual. If a fact changes, change it in one place. Add a line to the prompt that says what the agent should do when the answer is not present.

## A sample call

Illustrative only; no real company involved.

Caller: Hi, do you offer a refund on the annual plan?

Agent: I can tell you what our policy says. Annual plans can be cancelled at any time, and refunds depend on how long the plan has been active. I do not have your account in front of me, so for an actual refund I would pass you to the billing team. Would you like me to do that?

Caller: Yes please.

Agent: One moment. I will tell them you are asking about an annual plan refund so you do not have to repeat yourself.

The agent gave the policy, declined to decide the case, and transferred with a short summary.

## What to set up first

1. **Pick the first ten questions.** Count them from real tickets, not from guesses.
2. **Write the answers aloud.** If it sounds odd spoken, rewrite it.
3. **Set the boundaries in the prompt.** List what the agent may answer and what it must pass on. The [prompt template](/resources/ai-voice-agent-prompt-template) helps.
4. **Define the handoff.** Choose who receives transfers and during which hours. The [warm transfer guide](/blog/warm-transfer-ai-to-human) explains the options.
5. **Test with awkward phrasing.** Ring the agent from your own phone and ask the same thing three different ways.
6. **Read transcripts daily for a week.** Fix gaps as you find them.
7. **Add a disclosure line.** Open the call by saying it is an AI assistant.

## Where it falls short

A voice agent cannot reliably answer questions your knowledge base does not cover, and it should not try. It also cannot see a customer's account unless your team builds that connection through webhooks or the API, which is available on the Pro plan.

Long, multi-step technical troubleshooting is a poor fit, because callers cannot hold ten steps in their head. Those calls are better handled by a person or by pointing to written help sent separately.

Voice also cannot show a screenshot. If a procedure depends on seeing something, the agent can describe it, but a person may do it better.

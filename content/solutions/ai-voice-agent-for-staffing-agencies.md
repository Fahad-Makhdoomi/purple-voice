An AI voice agent for staffing agencies handles the high-repetition calls on both sides of the desk: candidates who need screening, confirming or rebooking, and clients who ring in to ask about orders. Consultants stay on the work that earns the fee, which is matching the right person to the right job and managing the relationship. It is built for agency owners and desk managers who want more placements from the same headcount.

## Two phone lines, one bottleneck

A staffing agency runs two markets at once. Candidates call and are called constantly, often about availability for tomorrow. Clients call with urgent orders, changes and questions about who is arriving. The consultant is the bottleneck for both, and every minute spent re-asking a candidate their postcode is a minute not spent filling a client order.

An agent reduces that load without changing how you do business. It uses your own numbers, so candidates and clients see the number they already know.

## What an AI voice agent does for staffing agencies

- **Screens new registrations.** It confirms right to work, location, transport, availability and pay expectation, then writes up an AI summary.
- **Checks availability for shifts.** Before a consultant builds a roster, the agent calls the pool and asks who can work which days.
- **Confirms placements.** A call the day before a start date confirms time, address and who to ask for.
- **Answers candidate questions.** Pay day, how to submit timesheets and where to report are answered from your knowledge base.
- **Takes client enquiries.** It collects order details or message requests when consultants are busy, and passes the summary on.
- **Handles out-of-hours calls.** Inbound calls at night and weekends do not go to voicemail.
- **Transfers urgent calls.** A client reporting a no-show can be warm-transferred to the on-call consultant.

## Candidate side and client side

| Call | Agent does | Hands off when |
|---|---|---|
| New candidate registration | Collects core details, screens | Candidate is a strong match for a live role |
| Shift availability check | Asks for days and times | Candidate wants to discuss pay or conditions |
| Start-date confirmation | Confirms and records | Candidate cancels or raises a problem |
| Client order enquiry | Captures role, location, dates | Client is upset or negotiating rates |
| Client check-in (who is arriving) | Reads the confirmed list from the knowledge base | Any change to the list |

## A sample call

Illustrative only. Names and details are placeholders.

**Agent:** Hello, this is the agency's assistant, an AI, calling about the temporary reception cover next week. Do you have two minutes?
**Candidate:** Sure.
**Agent:** The shift is Monday to Wednesday, 8 am to 4 pm, near the city centre. Can you do all three days?
**Candidate:** Monday and Tuesday, not Wednesday. I have an appointment.
**Agent:** Understood. I will note Monday and Tuesday only. Do you have a reliable way to get there by 8?
**Candidate:** Yes, the train.
**Agent:** Thank you. A consultant will confirm by this afternoon. If the client needs all three days, would you like us to call you about other work for Wednesday?
**Candidate:** Yes please.

## What to set up first

1. **Separate agents for separate jobs.** A candidate screening agent and a client-enquiry agent need different prompts and tone.
2. **Write the screening fields.** Keep to what the placement needs, and avoid questions the role does not require.
3. **Load the knowledge base** with pay dates, timesheet instructions, dress code and site directions.
4. **Connect Google Calendar** for interview slots and consultant callbacks.
5. **Set campaign hours and retries** of up to 5 with retry hours, suited to shift workers who answer at odd times.
6. **Configure warm transfer** to a named consultant for urgent client calls.
7. **Enable consent confirmation and AI disclosure,** then test-call your own phone.

## Where it falls short

An agent is not a consultant. It cannot sense a candidate who says yes but will not turn up, and it should not make promises about pay or hours that your team has not confirmed. Client relationships are personal; clients usually want a named person when something goes wrong. Keep the agent away from rate negotiation, grievances and anything involving a candidate's wellbeing.

Check each call summary against what you actually need before you place someone. A summary speeds up the decision; it does not replace it.

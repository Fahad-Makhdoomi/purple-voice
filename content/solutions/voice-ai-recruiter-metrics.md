The AI recruiter metrics worth tracking are contact rate, completion rate, time-to-first-call, interview booking rate and show-up rate, read together with a sample of real transcripts. This page is for recruiting leads who want to know whether a voice agent is helping or just generating activity, and it avoids invented benchmarks: compare against your own baseline.

For context on what the agent does, see the [voice AI recruiter overview](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter).

## The core AI recruiter metrics

- **Contact rate.** Candidates reached divided by candidates called. Retries up to 5 and well-chosen retry hours affect this most.
- **Completion rate.** Calls where the candidate answered all required questions divided by connected calls. A low number may mean the script is too long.
- **Time-to-first-call.** From application to first call attempt. Compare with your manual average.
- **Booking rate.** Interviews booked divided by completed screens.
- **Show-up rate.** Candidates who attend divided by those booked. This catches bookings that look good but do not convert.
- **Transfer rate.** Warm transfers made divided by calls, a rough sign of how many are worth a live conversation.
- **Opt-out rate.** People who ask not to be called again, which is a signal about tone and targeting.

## Metrics, where to find them and what they tell you

| Metric | Where to look | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| Contact rate | Campaign progress, not-reached analytics | Hours, retries and list quality |
| Completion rate | Calls, transcripts | Script length and clarity |
| Time-to-first-call | Campaign start vs application time | Process speed |
| Booking rate | Appointments | Offer and handoff quality |
| Show-up rate | Appointments vs attendance | Candidate commitment, reminders |
| Opt-outs | Call notes and summaries | Tone, relevance, consent |

## A sample reading

*Illustrative only, with made-up numbers.*

A team calls 200 applicants. 110 are reached, so contact rate is 55 percent. 88 complete all questions, so completion is 80 percent of connected calls. 40 book an interview, and 28 attend, a 70 percent show-up rate.

**Reading it:** contact is fine, completion is healthy, but a 30 percent drop between booking and attending suggests reminders or a better handoff matter more than another script tweak. Listening to ten of the no-shows' calls may show they sounded unsure.

## What to set up first

1. Write down your current numbers for the same role, even if rough.
2. Keep one script and one calling window during the pilot.
3. Check the not-reached analytics daily and try a different retry hour if the same people are missed.
4. Read at least ten transcripts per batch rather than relying on numbers alone.
5. Record booking and show-up outcomes outside the agent, in your usual system, so you can match them.
6. Review weekly and change one variable at a time.

## Reading ai recruiter metrics without fooling yourself

Metrics drift when the work changes. If you shorten the script, completion rises, but you may be collecting less. If you widen the calling window, contact rises but so might opt-outs. Pair these numbers with [candidate experience](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter-candidate-experience) feedback and your [cost per screen](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter-cost). Where reminders are the problem, the [call-ahead reminder approach](/blog/ai-appointment-setting) is relevant. The [call script](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter-call-script) page helps you reword weak questions.

## Where it falls short

Numbers cannot tell you whether you hired well. Small samples swing wildly, so a week of 30 calls proves little. Metrics also do not show candidates who disliked the call and said nothing. Do not set targets that push the agent to rush people, and do not use any single metric to judge a candidate. Show-up and quality of hire depend on your whole process, not only the call.

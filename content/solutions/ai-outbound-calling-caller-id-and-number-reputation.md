Caller ID for AI outbound calls matters because many people decide whether to answer from the screen alone. A number labelled "spam likely" or unfamiliar will often go unanswered, whatever the agent would have said. You can improve your odds by using numbers you own, building volume gradually and calling people who expect you. You cannot control how carriers and apps label calls, and no vendor can promise otherwise. For the wider system see the [AI outbound calling system overview](/solutions/ai-outbound-calling-system).

## What shapes number reputation

- **Volume and pace.** A fresh number making hundreds of calls in a day looks like automated abuse.
- **Answer and call length.** Many unanswered or very short calls can lower a number's standing.
- **User reports and blocks.** People marking calls as spam feeds labelling apps.
- **Registration and identity.** In some countries carriers support business registration or verified calling programmes.
- **Consistency.** A stable number that people recognise and can call back tends to fare better than constantly changing numbers.
- **Content of the call.** Calls that are relevant and expected get fewer complaints.

## What you control and what you do not

| Factor | You control it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Which number you call from | Yes | Numbers from Twilio, Plivo or SuperPhone |
| Daily volume per number | Yes | Start low, scale gradually |
| Calling hours and retries | Yes | Up to 5 retries, use fewer for cold lists |
| Who you call | Yes | Relevant, consented contacts get fewer reports |
| Voicemail callback number | Yes | Make sure it rings somewhere |
| How carriers label you | No | Algorithms differ and change |
| Branded display on every phone | No | Support varies by country, carrier and device |
| Third-party spam apps | No | Based on reports and patterns |

## A sample call

Illustrative. A caller returns a missed call and the agent handles the callback.

**Caller:** Hi, someone called me from this number earlier. Who is this?

**Agent:** Thanks for calling back. This is the booking assistant for a tile showroom, and we called about the sample you requested on Monday. Is this a good time to confirm your delivery address?

**Caller:** Oh, right. Yes.

**Agent:** Thank you. Is it still Flat 3, Harbour Road?

**Caller:** Yes, that is correct.

**Agent:** Great, it is confirmed. If you ever need to reach us, this number reaches the showroom.

## What to set up first

1. **Pick numbers you can answer.** If people call back to silence, they report the number. Route inbound calls to an agent or a person. Purple Voice supports inbound as well.
2. **Use a number that fits the audience.** A local or national-format number may suit your market, within local rules.
3. **Warm up gradually.** Begin with a small campaign and increase over days, not hours.
4. **Use fewer retries on cold lists.** Five attempts in a few days looks like harassment. Spread them out.
5. **Say who is calling right away.** A clear opening reduces complaints. See [disclosing AI on calls](/blog/disclose-ai-on-calls).
6. **Check provider options.** Ask your carrier about business registration, verified calling or branded caller ID in your country.
7. **Watch the numbers.** Track not-reached analytics in the campaign. A sudden fall in answers is a signal to slow down.

The [campaign setup guide](/solutions/ai-outbound-calling-campaign-setup) covers hours and retries in detail.

## Branded caller ID: useful, not magic

Branded calling shows a business name, and sometimes a logo or reason, on supported networks and devices. It can make an expected call easier to recognise. It does not override a recipient's settings, and it is not available everywhere. Treat it as one helpful layer, not a fix. If your list contains people who never agreed to hear from you, branding will not save the campaign.

## Where this falls short

- **No guarantee.** Carriers decide labels and blocks. Purple Voice cannot promise your calls will be shown as you wish.
- **Number rotation is a trap.** Frequently switching numbers to avoid blocks can breach carrier terms and local rules, and it harms trust.
- **Spoofing is not the answer.** Displaying a number you do not own is restricted in many places.
- **Rules differ by region.** Caller identification, registration and consent requirements vary. This is general information, not legal advice, so check current rules and read the [compliance overview](/solutions/ai-outbound-calling-compliance).
- **Reputation is slow to rebuild.** Prevention beats recovery.

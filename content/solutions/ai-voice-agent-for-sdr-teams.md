An ai sdr voice agent does the repetitive front end of a sales development role: it dials, asks the qualifying questions, answers the obvious ones and books the meeting on a calendar. Your human SDRs keep the work that needs research and judgement. This page is for sales leads deciding how to divide that work without breaking what already works.

## What an AI SDR voice agent does for SDR teams

- **Works the long tail of leads.** Every form fill, list import or trial signup gets a call, not only the ones an SDR had time for.
- **Runs first-pass qualification.** It asks the same questions in the same order every time and records what it hears.
- **Handles the routine objections.** "Send me some info", "now is not a good time" and "who are you" get a calm, scripted response, then a clear next step or a clean exit.
- **Books meetings.** It offers real slots and creates the event on Google Calendar.
- **Retries on a schedule.** Campaigns can retry unanswered numbers up to 5 times within the calling hours you set.
- **Documents everything.** Summaries and transcripts mean a rep opens a call already briefed.
- **Frees the human calendar.** The time not spent dialling goes to named-account work and follow-up with live prospects.

## Division of labour

| Task | AI voice agent | Human SDR |
|---|---|---|
| Inbound lead, first call | Yes | Reviews summary |
| Mass list follow-up | Yes | Takes the warm ones |
| Target-account research and outreach | No | Yes |
| Handling a pricing objection | Acknowledges, books a call | Takes the conversation |
| Multi-threading into an account | No | Yes |
| Re-contacting no-shows | Yes | Only if the account is high value |

## A sample call

This is illustrative and uses no real brand names.

> **Agent:** Hello, this is the assistant calling for a field service software company. You downloaded our scheduling guide yesterday. Do you have a minute?
> **Caller:** Yeah, but I do not want a pitch.
> **Agent:** Understood, no pitch. I only want to check whether the guide was useful and if you are looking at scheduling tools this quarter.
> **Caller:** We are, actually. Our dispatcher is swamped.
> **Agent:** How many technicians are you dispatching?
> **Caller:** Twenty-two.
> **Agent:** That sits well within what our team supports. Would a short call with a specialist this week help?
> **Caller:** Wednesday afternoon.
> **Agent:** I can do Wednesday at 3 pm. I will confirm it now.

The rep who inherits this knows the size, the pain and the timing before saying hello.

## What to set up first

1. **Agree the qualification definition with your SDRs.** If the team cannot agree on what qualified means, the agent cannot enforce it.
2. **Write the agent prompt from your best SDR's real calls.** Keep their phrasing, remove the filler.
3. **Add a knowledge base** with positioning, who you do not serve and approved answers on pricing.
4. **Set the transfer rule.** Decide which answers trigger a warm transfer and which trigger a booking.
5. **Connect Google Calendar** and map which rep owns which slots.
6. **Run test calls on your own phone** and ask your SDRs to try to break it.
7. **Choose campaign hours, time zones and retries.**
8. **Plan a weekly transcript review** in the first month.

## Where it falls short

- **Account-based selling.** Senior buyers expect a researched, human approach.
- **Reading the room.** An experienced SDR hears hesitation and adapts. The agent follows the structure it was given.
- **Messy data.** If the list has wrong numbers or stale contacts, the agent just calls them faster.
- **Morale and role design.** Be open with your SDRs about how the role changes; the best setups give them the warm transfers and the better leads.
- **Rules on outbound calling** differ by place. This is general information, so check current requirements before calling a list.

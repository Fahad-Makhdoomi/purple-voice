AI call qualification is the practice of letting an AI voice agent hold the first conversation with a lead: it asks your questions, records what the lead says and decides, against criteria you wrote, whether a salesperson should spend time on them. It suits sales leads, founders and agency owners who get more enquiries than they can phone back quickly. This page covers how it works and how to set criteria. The pages linked below go deeper on each piece.

## What AI call qualification does for a sales team

A qualification call is a short, structured conversation. The agent is not trying to sell; it is trying to find out whether a sale is plausible. In practice the agent can:

- **Reach leads fast.** An outbound campaign calls a new list within your calling hours, and inbound calls are answered on the first ring, so the lead is not left waiting for a callback. See [speed to lead with AI calling](/blog/speed-to-lead-ai-calling).
- **Ask the same questions every time.** Consistency is the main gain. Two human reps will qualify the same lead differently; the agent follows the same prompt on every call.
- **Handle interruptions and side questions.** Leads ask about price or location mid-answer. The agent answers from its knowledge base and returns to the next question.
- **Retry unreached leads.** Campaigns retry up to 5 times with retry hours you choose, and the not-reached analytics show who never picked up.
- **Book or transfer.** Qualified leads can be booked on Google Calendar or warm-transferred to a person while they are still on the line.
- **Write it down.** Every call produces a transcript, an AI summary and a recording, so the salesperson starts with context instead of a blank notes field.

## How AI call qualification works step by step

The flow is the same whether the lead came from a form, an ad or an old list. The agent opens by saying who it is and why it is calling, confirms it is speaking to the right person, and asks permission to continue. It then works through your questions, adapting the order slightly if the lead volunteers an answer early. At the end it applies your rules: qualified, not qualified, or unclear.

The agent's behaviour comes from its prompt and knowledge base, which you edit in the Agents section of the dashboard. The AI layer underneath is managed by the Purple Voice team, so you do not choose or maintain models. For a plain-language view of the technology, read [how AI voice agents work](/blog/how-ai-voice-agents-work).

## Setting criteria an agent can apply

Good criteria are specific enough that two people would reach the same verdict. A useful structure has three buckets.

| Bucket | Example rule | What the agent does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qualified | Needs the service within 90 days and is the decision maker | Books a slot or transfers the call |
| Not a fit | Outside your service area, or no budget at all | Thanks them, ends politely, logs the reason |
| Unclear | Interested but vague on timing, or someone else decides | Flags for human review with a summary |

The third bucket matters most. If you force every lead into yes or no, the agent will either wave through weak leads or reject good ones. A review bucket keeps the person in charge of the grey areas.

To write the rules, look at your last 20 closed deals and your last 20 lost leads. Which answers separated them? Those answers are your criteria. A bank of starter questions is in the [lead qualification questions bank](/resources/lead-qualification-questions-bank).

## A sample call

*Illustrative only; your agent will use your own wording.*

**Agent:** Hello, this is the booking assistant for a local solar installer. You asked about a quote on our website. Is now a good time for a few quick questions?
**Caller:** Sure, go ahead.
**Agent:** Do you own the property?
**Caller:** Yes, I own it.
**Agent:** Is it a house or a flat?
**Caller:** A detached house, south-facing roof.
**Agent:** And are you looking to go ahead in the next three months, or just gathering prices?
**Caller:** Probably the next couple of months.
**Agent:** That fits. I can put you through to a surveyor now, or book a call for tomorrow. Which do you prefer?

## What to set up first

1. Write your three buckets and the four to six questions that decide them.
2. Create the agent and paste the questions into its prompt, with the tone you want.
3. Add pricing ranges, service areas and common objections to the knowledge base.
4. Set campaign calling hours and retry hours (up to 5 retries), or connect an inbound number.
5. Turn on consent confirmation where your rules require it.
6. Connect Google Calendar and choose a warm-transfer number for hot leads.
7. Test-call your own phone, interrupt the agent, give odd answers, and fix the prompt.

## Where AI call qualification falls short

- It applies the criteria you wrote. If the criteria are weak, the output is weak, only faster.
- Unusual situations, emotional callers and complex buying committees need a person.
- It cannot verify what a lead says. Someone who claims a budget they do not have will be recorded as having it.
- Some people dislike talking to an AI. Disclosing it plainly and offering a human option protects the experience; see [disclosing AI on calls](/blog/disclose-ai-on-calls).
- Calling rules such as TCPA, PECR and Do Not Call lists apply. This is general information, not legal advice, so check current rules for your market.

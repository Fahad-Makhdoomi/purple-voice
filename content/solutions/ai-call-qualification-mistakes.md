AI call qualification mistakes are mostly design mistakes, not technology failures: too many questions, criteria that nobody checked against real deals and a tone that sounds like a form being read aloud. The good news is that each one leaves a trace in your transcripts, and each has a small fix. This page is for anyone whose agent is live and not performing as expected. For a clean starting point, read [AI call qualification](/solutions/ai-call-qualification).

## What an AI voice agent will faithfully do wrong

An agent follows its prompt. That is its strength and its risk. Whatever you wrote, it will do on every call, so one badly worded question or one over-strict rule is multiplied across hundreds of conversations. The upside is that fixing the prompt fixes all future calls at once.

Typical symptoms you can spot in the Calls section:

- **Hang-ups before the third question.** The opening is unclear or too long.
- **Repeated "sorry, can you say that again?"** A question is ambiguous or compound.
- **Many leads marked unqualified who sound fine.** Criteria are too strict.
- **Many leads marked qualified that reps reject.** Criteria are too loose.
- **Summaries that reps ignore.** They are too long or leave out key facts.

## Eight mistakes and their smallest fix

| Mistake | How it shows up | Smallest fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Too many questions | Drop-off mid-call | Cut to the four to six that change the next step |
| Vague criteria | Reps and agent disagree | Write numeric or yes/no thresholds |
| Budget asked first | Early hang-ups | Ask need and timing first, budget later |
| Robotic tone | Callers answer in single words | Rewrite lines as speech, with contractions |
| No exit for "not now" | Callers get irritated | Offer a callback time and end gracefully |
| Forcing yes or no | Weak leads passed, good ones dropped | Add a human review bucket |
| No knowledge base | Agent dodges simple questions | Add prices, hours and FAQs |
| No review loop | Quality drifts | Read transcripts weekly and edit the prompt |

Each of these is explained in more detail in the related pages: [question design](/solutions/ai-call-qualification-questions) and [scoring](/solutions/ai-call-qualification-scoring).

## A sample call that goes wrong, then right

*Illustrative only.*

Before:

**Agent:** Hello, I am calling to qualify you. What is your budget, timeline, decision-making authority and current provider?
**Caller:** Sorry, who is this?

After:

**Agent:** Hi, I'm the assistant for a local web design studio. You sent an enquiry this morning. Is now an okay time for a couple of quick questions?
**Caller:** Sure.
**Agent:** What are you hoping the new site will do for you?
**Caller:** Bring in more bookings.
**Agent:** Got it. And when would you like it live?
**Caller:** Within two months.
**Agent:** Thanks. A designer will call you to talk through options. Would Thursday work?

Same lead, different design. The second version says who is calling and why, asks one question at a time and leads somewhere.

## What to set up first when fixing a live agent

1. Pull a sample of ten qualified and ten rejected calls from the Calls section.
2. Mark where callers hang up and where they ask for clarification.
3. Check the criteria against your last 20 closed deals and lost leads.
4. Rewrite the opening so it states who is calling, why, and how long it will take.
5. Add a review bucket and decide who reads it and within what time.
6. Update the knowledge base for any question the agent could not answer.
7. Check calling hours and retries (up to 5) so you are not calling at awkward times, then test-call your own phone.

Use the [AI voice agent prompt template](/resources/ai-voice-agent-prompt-template) if you want a clean structure to rewrite into.

## Where fixes fall short

- Some drop-off is simply people who do not want to talk. Not every hang-up is a design fault.
- Changing several things at once hides what worked. Edit one thing, then compare.
- Transcripts show what was said, not what the caller felt. Listen to a few recordings as well as reading.
- A disliked offer will not be rescued by better questions.
- If a caller is not told it is an AI, trust suffers and rules may be breached. See [disclosing AI on calls](/blog/disclose-ai-on-calls). This is general information, not legal advice, so check current rules.

## Mistakes in the setup around the agent

Not every problem is in the script. Check the surroundings too. Calling hours that fall during dinner or school runs lower answer rates, so test different windows and set retry hours at different times of day. A number that shows up as unknown or spam on the lead's phone will be ignored however good the script is. A calendar with no open slots means a qualified lead hears "we will be in touch" instead of a booking. A warm-transfer number that rings out sends hot leads to silence. Walk the whole path once a month as if you were the lead: form, call, qualification, booking, confirmation.

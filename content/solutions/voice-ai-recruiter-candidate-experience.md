The AI recruiter candidate experience is shaped by a few choices you control: say clearly that it is an AI, keep the call short, listen when candidates interrupt and give everyone an easy way to reach a person or opt out. Candidates remember how you treated them, so a poor first call can cost you the people you most want. This page is for recruiters and employer-brand owners using a voice agent for first contact.

Background is in the [voice AI recruiter overview](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter).

## What a good candidate experience includes

- **Honest opening.** The agent says it is an AI assistant, names the company and the role, and gives the call length.
- **Permission to continue.** It asks whether now is a good time and offers to call back.
- **Short and relevant questions.** Only things you will actually use, asked clearly.
- **Responsive turn-taking.** The agent stops when the candidate speaks and does not talk over them.
- **A real answer or an honest gap.** It answers from the knowledge base and says when it does not know.
- **A visible exit.** Candidates can ask for a person, end the call or opt out of future calls.
- **A follow-through.** What the candidate was told will happen next actually happens.

## Candidate signals and responses

| Candidate says or does | Agent response | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Is this a robot?" | Confirms it is an AI assistant | Honesty builds trust |
| "Can I talk to a person?" | Offers a warm transfer or booked call | Respects their choice |
| "Not now" | Offers a callback time | Avoids pressure |
| "Stop calling me" | Ends call politely, notes opt-out | Respect and consent |
| Long silence | Waits, then repeats once | Gives time to think |
| Gets angry | Apologises, offers a human, ends if needed | De-escalates |

## A sample call

*Illustrative only.*

**Agent:** Hello, this is an AI assistant calling for a company about your application for the care assistant role. It takes about three minutes. Is now okay?
**Caller:** I am at work, can you call later?
**Agent:** Of course. Would this evening at six suit you, or tomorrow lunchtime?
**Caller:** Six tonight.
**Agent:** I have booked that. If you would rather speak to a person, say so any time. Thank you.

The call lasted under a minute, and the candidate chose the time.

## What to set up first

1. Rewrite the opening so the first sentence discloses the AI and the second says who it is calling for.
2. Set calling hours that suit candidates, not only your team, and avoid unsociable times.
3. Limit retries to a number you would find reasonable if you were the candidate, up to 5 at most.
4. Add the rule to the prompt: stop calling and log it when someone opts out, and respond to any request for a human.
5. Confirm consent before a person goes into a campaign.
6. Use the test call to try rude, confused and shy responses and listen for tone.
7. Read transcripts for the first week looking for moments candidates sounded uncomfortable.

## Improving the ai recruiter candidate experience over time

Look at calls that ended early and ask why. Common causes are a long opening, a question that feels intrusive or an agent that answers too slowly. Fix them in the prompt and test again. Disclosure guidance in [disclose AI on calls](/blog/disclose-ai-on-calls) and the [warm transfer guide](/blog/warm-transfer-ai-to-human) cover the details. Teams with a heavy surge may also read about [seasonal hiring](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter-for-seasonal-hiring), where care slips first.

## Where it falls short

An agent cannot read emotion the way a person does, and some candidates will find any automated call impersonal. Accents, background noise and poor lines can cause misunderstandings that feel rude. Not everyone wants a call at all. Offering no alternative to phone contact excludes people with hearing or speech difficulties, so provide another route for them through a person. This is general information, not legal advice, and rules on disclosure and calling vary, so check what applies where you hire.

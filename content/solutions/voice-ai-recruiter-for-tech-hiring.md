An AI recruiter for tech hiring is useful for a narrow slice of the process: calling people who have shown interest, confirming practical details and booking a conversation with a recruiter. It is not a technical interviewer. This page is for in-house talent teams and agencies hiring engineers, data and product people who want to save time on logistics without pretending a phone call can assess code.

Start with the general picture in our [voice AI recruiter overview](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter).

## What an AI recruiter does in tech hiring

- **Calls applicants fast.** Strong engineers are often off the market within days, so a call soon after applying beats a reply a week later.
- **Confirms logistics.** Location, time zone, remote or hybrid preference, work authorisation and notice period.
- **Captures expectations.** Salary range and what the candidate wants from the next role, recorded in their words.
- **Notes stack at a surface level.** Languages, frameworks and tools they say they use, logged for a recruiter to follow up.
- **Answers basic role questions.** Team size, interview stages, benefits and process, from your knowledge base.
- **Books the next step.** A call with a recruiter or hiring manager on Google Calendar.

## What the agent should leave alone

| Topic | Agent's job | Who decides |
|---|---|---|
| Notice period and start date | Ask and record | Recruiter |
| Salary expectations | Ask and record | Recruiter and hiring manager |
| Stack and experience | Capture what they say | Technical interviewer |
| Code quality and system design | Do not attempt | Technical interviewer |
| Culture and motivation | Note stated reasons | Recruiter |
| Offer details | Do not discuss | Recruiter |

## A sample call

*Illustrative only.*

**Agent:** Hi, this is an AI assistant calling for a company that received your application for the backend engineer role. Do you have two minutes?
**Caller:** Sure.
**Agent:** Are you based in the UK, and would remote work suit you?
**Caller:** UK, and remote is ideal.
**Agent:** Thanks. What is your notice period?
**Caller:** One month.
**Agent:** And a rough salary range you have in mind?
**Caller:** I would rather discuss that with a person.
**Agent:** Understood. I will note that and offer a call with a recruiter. Would Tuesday afternoon suit?

Notice the agent does not push on salary. If a candidate declines a question, it records that and moves on.

## What to set up first

1. Write a prompt that names the role, the company and the stack at a high level, and states that the agent will not assess technical skill.
2. Add the interview stages, remote policy and benefits to the knowledge base.
3. Limit the questions to five or fewer, so the call respects the candidate's time.
4. Set calling hours around working days and consider early evening for people with day jobs.
5. Connect Google Calendar and choose which recruiter's availability the agent offers.
6. Test by calling yourself and answering like a busy engineer who wants the point quickly.

## Using an ai recruiter for tech hiring without losing candidates

Engineers dislike wasted time and vague calls. Make the opening specific, state that it is an AI, say how long it will take and let people reschedule or ask for a human. A candidate who feels herded will withdraw. Read the [candidate experience guidance](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter-candidate-experience) and compare with how a [human recruiter handles the same calls](/solutions/voice-ai-recruiter-vs-human-recruiter).

## Where it falls short

It cannot tell a strong engineer from a confident talker. Passive candidates, who are often the best, rarely respond to unexpected calls, and cold calling people who never applied raises consent and data protection issues you should check with current rules. Technical vocabulary may be transcribed imperfectly, so summaries need a human skim. Treat its notes as leads for a recruiter, not as a shortlist.

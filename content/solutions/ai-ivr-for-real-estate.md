AI IVR for real estate is a phone agent that picks up when someone rings about a listing, answers the standard property questions, finds out whether the caller is a serious buyer or tenant, and books the viewing. It is built for agencies and independent agents whose phones ring most while they are out at appointments, which is exactly when a listing enquiry is hottest.

The pattern is familiar: the board number is called, nobody answers, and the caller moves on to the next listing within minutes.

## What the agent does with a listing call

- **Identifies the property.** "Which house are you calling about?" ties the call to the right knowledge entry.
- **Answers the factual basics.** Price as advertised, bedrooms, parking, pets, availability.
- **Sorts the caller.** Buyer, seller, landlord or tenant, each with a different route.
- **Asks light qualifying questions.** Timeline, budget range, whether they have a property to sell, financing status.
- **Books the viewing.** It offers open slots on Google Calendar and confirms the details.
- **Escalates the hot ones.** A motivated cash buyer can be warm transferred to the agent on shift.
- **Records everything.** Transcript, summary and recording for each enquiry.

For adjacent playbooks, see [ai calling for real estate agents](/blog/ai-calling-for-real-estate-agents) and the outbound side, [AI outbound calling for real estate](/solutions/ai-outbound-calling-for-real-estate).

## Call type, agent action, hand-off

| Caller | Agent does | Hand-off |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer asking about a listing | Answers, qualifies, books viewing | Transfers if ready to offer |
| Tenant | Checks move date, budget, pets, books viewing | Landlord queries to the lettings desk |
| Would-be seller | Takes address and timeline, books valuation call | To a senior agent |
| Portal tyre-kicker | Answers, notes interest | None |
| Existing client | Takes a message | To their agent |
| Offer or negotiation | Declines to discuss | Immediate transfer |

## A sample call

Illustrative, for a lettings agency.

Agent: Thanks for calling about the property. I'm an AI assistant for the agency. Which address are you calling about?
Caller: The two-bedroom flat near the park.
Agent: That one is available from the first of next month. Is that the right timing for you?
Caller: Yes, I'd move quickly. Are pets allowed?
Agent: Small pets are allowed with the landlord's agreement. How many people will be living there?
Caller: Two of us and a cat.
Agent: That should be fine to discuss. I have viewings Thursday at 5:30 or Saturday at 11. Which works?
Caller: Thursday.
Agent: Booked. A member of the team will confirm by phone.

## What to set up first

1. **Create a listing sheet.** For each property: address, price, size, features, availability, viewing rules, pet and parking policy.
2. **Write a prompt for your market.** Whether a caller is "buying" or "renting" changes wording and questions.
3. **Choose the qualifying questions** and keep them to three or four. The [questions bank](/resources/lead-qualification-questions-bank) has ideas.
4. **Connect Google Calendar** for the person doing viewings, with travel buffers.
5. **Name transfer triggers:** offers, "I'm ready to buy now", complaints, existing clients.
6. **Add disclosure and recording wording.** See [AI IVR recording and consent](/solutions/ai-ivr-compliance-and-recording).
7. **Test with a live board number** and call it yourself from outside the office.

## Where AI IVR for real estate falls short

It does not know a property the way someone who has stood in it does: the noise from the road, the smell of damp, what the vendor will accept. Keep valuation, negotiation and sensitive conversations with a person. Stale listings are the biggest practical risk: if a property is let and the knowledge base still says available, the agent will invite people to a viewing that wastes everyone's time. Someone has to own that update daily. Property regulations about what you may say or advertise also vary, so check them yourself.

Phone screening questions for an AI agent need to work by ear: short, one idea at a time, and answerable in a sentence. The same question that reads well on an application form can fall flat out loud. This page gives you a bank to adapt, explains how to split it into knockout and scored questions, and shows the wording that holds up on a live call.

## What an AI voice agent does with your questions

- **Asks them in a fixed order.** Every applicant gets the same sequence, which makes answers comparable.
- **Listens for category, not just words.** "Not before March" and "I'd need about eight weeks" both map to a start date window.
- **Probes when you tell it to.** One follow-up on vague answers, then moves on rather than interrogating.
- **Lets callers ask back.** Questions about pay, hours and location are answered from the knowledge base.
- **Records answers per question.** The summary lists each question with the caller's response, so reviewers can scan.
- **Hands off on trigger phrases.** "Can I speak to someone?" ends the script and routes to a person.

## A question bank to adapt

| Type | Question (spoken form) | Knockout or scored |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Can you start within a month? | Scored |
| Location | Is a daily commute to the north site realistic? | Knockout, if no remote option |
| Eligibility | Are you able to work in this country without sponsorship? | Knockout, worded neutrally |
| Schedule | This role includes weekends. Does that work? | Knockout or scored, your call |
| Experience | How long have you done this kind of work? | Scored |
| Credentials | Do you hold the required licence? | Knockout, verified later by a person |
| Pay | The range is X to Y. Is that in line with what you expect? | Scored |
| Motivation | What made you apply? | Nice to know, one sentence only |

## A sample call

*Illustrative only.*

**Agent:** Two quick questions about the schedule. This role includes every second weekend. Does that work for you?

**Caller:** Hmm, I have a standing weekend commitment once a month.

**Agent:** Understood. Is that every second weekend, or just one a month?

**Caller:** Just one a month, the first.

**Agent:** Thanks, I'll note it. And the shift starts at six in the morning. Is that fine?

**Caller:** Yes, I'm an early riser.

**Agent:** Good. I'll pass this on with your note about the first weekend.

Here the agent probed once, recorded the nuance and did not decide anything.

## What to set up first

1. **Collect the questions you actually use.** Take them from your last three successful hires, not from a template.
2. **Cut to eight at most.** Merge or drop anything that does not change a decision.
3. **Write each as a spoken line.** Read it aloud; if you stumble, shorten it.
4. **Tag each: knockout, scored or note.** Put the tag in the agent prompt along with what to say if the answer is a miss.
5. **Add probing rules.** One follow-up for vague answers, never more.
6. **Load the knowledge base** with pay, schedule and location so candidate questions are answered accurately. The [lead qualification questions bank](/resources/lead-qualification-questions-bank) shows the same approach for sales.
7. **Run a test call on your phone** and listen to the recording for pacing.

## Phone screening questions that work: wording tips

Avoid double-barrelled questions ("Do you have a licence and your own vehicle?"); split them. Avoid yes/no where you want detail ("Have you led teams?" gets "yes"; "How many people did you lead?" gets a number). Put the question people find easiest first so the call starts smoothly. If you hire across the board, the wider page on [candidate pre-screening](/solutions/voice-ai-agent-for-candidate-pre-screening) shows how these slot into a full workflow.

## Where it falls short

- Open-ended questions produce long answers that summarise poorly. Keep them rare.
- A scripted screen cannot read hesitation the way a person can.
- Nuanced answers ("it depends") may be categorised too neatly; reviewers should be able to open the transcript.
- A long script gets abandoned. If a question does not change a decision, remove it.

AI IVR for home services is a phone agent for trades and field-service firms that answers every job call, collects the details a dispatcher needs, books routine visits, and sends real emergencies to an on-call technician. It is for owner-operators and small crews who lose work every time the phone rings while their hands are busy.

A home-service caller is rarely browsing. They have water on the floor or a boiler that has quit, and they ring the first number that picks up.

## What the agent handles

- **Answers instantly.** The caller gets a person-like conversation instead of voicemail.
- **Captures job intake.** Type of problem, address, access notes, how long it has been happening, best number.
- **Triages urgency.** Active leaks, no heat, electrical burning smells go through your emergency rule.
- **Checks coverage.** Whether the address is in your service area.
- **Books routine visits.** Quotes, maintenance and non-urgent repairs land on Google Calendar.
- **Gives standard info.** Call-out fees, hours, payment methods, what you do not do.
- **Summarises.** One tidy summary per job, with a transcript behind it.

## Urgency tiers

| Situation | Agent response | Hand-off |
|---|---|---|
| Active leak or flooding | Tells caller how to stop it if you have written that guidance, collects address | Warm transfer to on-call |
| No heating in cold weather | Collects details | On-call within your hours |
| Dripping tap | Books next available visit | None |
| Quote request | Books assessment slot | None |
| Gas smell or electrical danger | Tells caller to use emergency services | Per your rule |
| Complaint on past job | Takes details | Owner callback |

## A sample call

Illustrative, for an electrician.

Agent: Thanks for calling. I'm an AI assistant for the electrical team. What's happening?
Caller: Half the sockets in my kitchen have stopped working.
Agent: Understood. Is there any burning smell, sparking or a tripped breaker you can't reset?
Caller: The breaker keeps tripping, no smell.
Agent: Thank you. Not an emergency, but we'll treat it as priority. Can I take your address and postcode?
Caller: 14 Alder Close, postcode...
Agent: We cover that area. I can book a visit tomorrow between 8 and 10 or between 1 and 3. Which suits you?
Caller: Morning.
Agent: Booked. The technician will call when he's on the way.

## What to set up first for AI IVR for home services

1. **Write an emergency list** with plain examples and what the agent says for each.
2. **Choose the intake fields** your dispatcher actually uses; skip the rest.
3. **Add your service area** as towns or postcodes in the knowledge base.
4. **Give call-out fee and hours** as text the agent can repeat precisely.
5. **Connect Google Calendar** with realistic time windows, not exact times, if you cannot be that precise.
6. **Set the on-call transfer** and a second-choice number.
7. **Test at night and with a bad line**, like a caller on a site or in a car.
8. **Add recording and disclosure wording** per [AI IVR recording and consent](/solutions/ai-ivr-compliance-and-recording).

## Where it falls short

The agent cannot see the fault. Callers describe problems vaguely, so a "burst pipe" might be a dripping joint, and you still need a person to judge. It must never give safety instructions you have not written yourself; for gas or electrical danger, point the caller to emergency services. Pricing a job sight unseen invites disputes, so keep quotes with people. Strong accents, noisy sites and caller stress mean addresses and numbers must be read back. Finally, an emergency line is only as good as your on-call rota: transferring to a phone nobody answers is worse than a clear message.

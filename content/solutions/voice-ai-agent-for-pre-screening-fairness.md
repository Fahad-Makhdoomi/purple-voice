Fair AI pre-screening is mostly about decisions made before the first call and habits kept after it. An AI agent brings real consistency: the same questions in the same order for every applicant, no tired Friday-afternoon version of the script. But consistency applied to a bad criterion is just a bad criterion applied faster. This page covers what a voice agent genuinely helps with, what it does not, and a practical routine to keep your screening defensible.

## What an AI voice agent does for consistency

- **Asks identical questions.** Same wording and sequence for everyone, removing the drift that creeps into human phone screens.
- **Records what was said.** Transcript and recording give you evidence of what was asked and answered.
- **Applies stated criteria only.** The agent follows the prompt, so criteria are written down, not carried in someone's head.
- **Discloses itself.** It states it is an AI and confirms the person is happy to continue.
- **Offers a person.** A request for a human is honoured by routing or a callback.
- **Gives everyone a call.** Applicants who would have been skipped because of time pressure still get the screen.
- **Supports review.** Summaries line up side by side so reviewers compare like with like.

## Where fairness actually comes from

| Area | Risk | Practical safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| Criteria | Requirements unrelated to the job exclude people | Write the reason for each; drop those you cannot justify |
| Questions | Asking about protected characteristics | Remove them from the script and test for drift |
| Speech recognition | May work less well for some accents | Spot-check transcripts across accents; keep recordings |
| Decisions | Automation makes final calls | Human reviews every adverse outcome |
| Disclosure | Applicants unaware it is an AI | Disclose at the start, offer a person |
| Monitoring | Problems go unnoticed | Review outcomes and drop-offs on a schedule |

## A sample call

*Illustrative only.*

**Agent:** Hello, this is an AI assistant calling for the hiring team. I will ask a few questions about the role and record your answers for the team. You can ask for a person at any time. Would you like to continue?

**Caller:** I'd rather speak to someone, actually.

**Agent:** Of course. I will pass your request to the team, and someone will call you back. What is the best time?

**Caller:** Tomorrow after two.

**Agent:** Noted, tomorrow after two. Thank you for your time.

The agent did not argue, push on or penalise the request, which is the behaviour you want.

## What to set up first

1. **Audit the criteria.** For each knockout or scored item, write why it matters to the job. Remove the rest. See [knockout questions](/solutions/voice-ai-agent-for-knockout-questions).
2. **Lock the script.** Put the exact questions and order in the agent prompt, and keep protected characteristics out.
3. **Write the disclosure and human-option lines.** Make them the first thing the caller hears; see [disclosing AI on calls](/blog/disclose-ai-on-calls).
4. **Turn on consent confirmation.** Confirm the caller is happy to continue before questions begin.
5. **Set the rule that no outcome is final without review.** Summaries tagged as a miss go to a person.
6. **Sample monthly.** Read ten random summaries and listen to five recordings, including some from callers who dropped out.
7. **Test on your own phone** with different accents, speeds and edge-case answers.

## Where it falls short

- It does not make unfair criteria fair. If a requirement excludes people without a good reason, scripting it consistently makes it consistently exclusionary.
- Speech recognition and summarisation can be less accurate for some voices. Reviewers need access to the recording.
- Laws on automated decisions, hiring tools and housing screening differ by place and change. This is general information, not legal advice; check current rules and take advice for high-stakes uses.
- A human reviewer who rubber-stamps AI output is not meaningful oversight. Reviewers need time and the authority to disagree.

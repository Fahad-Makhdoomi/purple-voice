After hours insurance answering with an AI voice agent means every call to your number gets a real conversation instead of a voicemail beep. The agent asks why the person is calling, captures quote details or a first claim report, sends urgent calls to a person and leaves a summary for the morning. It suits small agencies and brokers who cannot staff evenings but know calls come in.

## What goes wrong with voicemail

Callers do not like leaving messages, and many do not. Those who do ramble, forget their number or skip the detail you need. The next morning someone listens to a dozen messages, rings back and starts again. Meanwhile, the person who wanted a quote has called the next provider.

An agent that asks the questions removes that delay. It also separates the claim that cannot wait from the quote that can.

## Calls by urgency

| Call | Urgency | Agent response | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quote request | Medium | Takes the basics, offers a callback slot | Producer next morning |
| Policy question | Low | Answers from knowledge base or takes a message | Account manager |
| Accident, no injury | Medium | Takes first report, see [claims intake](/solutions/ai-voice-agent-for-claims-intake) | Claims team |
| Burst pipe, fire, theft | High | Advises on safety, flags priority | On-call transfer if available |
| Injury | Highest | Tells caller to contact emergency services | Transfer, then escalate |

## What an AI voice agent does after hours

- **Answers every call** on your own number, so callers see a familiar line.
- **States what it is.** A short AI disclosure at the start.
- **Asks why they are calling** in plain words and listens.
- **Checks urgency.** A fixed short list of triggers, such as injury or active damage.
- **Takes structured details.** Name, number, policy type and reason.
- **Books a callback** on Google Calendar for the first slot of the morning.
- **Gives accurate hours and contacts** from the knowledge base.
- **Writes a summary** you can scan in two minutes.

## A sample call

Illustrative only.

**Agent:** Thank you for calling. This is an automated assistant for an insurance agency, and the office is closed. I can take details or book a callback. What is the call about?
**Caller:** I had a minor accident on the way home. Nobody is hurt.
**Agent:** I am glad you are safe. I can take a first report so the claims team has it in the morning. May I ask where it happened and roughly what time?
**Caller:** At a junction near my house, about half an hour ago.
**Agent:** Thank you. Was another vehicle involved?
**Caller:** Yes, one other car.
**Agent:** I have that. A claims handler will call you tomorrow morning. Is this the best number to reach you?

## What to set up first

1. **Define your hours** and forward calls to the agent outside them. Overflow during the day is optional.
2. **Write the urgent branch** and keep it short. Include an on-call number if you have one and enable warm transfer.
3. **Add the knowledge base** with opening times, claims line details and what to do in common emergencies.
4. **Set the disclosure** in the greeting.
5. **Connect Google Calendar** for morning callback slots.
6. **Decide who reads summaries** and by what time, so nothing sits unread.
7. **Test overnight** by calling from your own phone at awkward hours with different scenarios.

## Where it falls short

It does not replace a claims line for serious incidents, and callers in distress need a human quickly. The agent can misjudge urgency if a caller downplays an injury, so train the prompt to err toward escalation. Background noise, strong accents and bad connections can reduce accuracy, and reading back key details helps. It also needs your team to act on summaries every morning, or it simply moves the backlog.

Rules on call recording and disclosure vary by location. This is general information, not legal advice; check current requirements, and see [disclosing AI on calls](/blog/disclose-ai-on-calls).

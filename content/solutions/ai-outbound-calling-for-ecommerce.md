AI outbound calling for ecommerce is worth using on a small set of moments where a conversation does more than a message: a high-value abandoned cart, a failed delivery, a large order that needs an address check, and a lapsed customer who bought from you before. For everything else, email and text remain cheaper and less intrusive. An agent calls, asks one helpful question, answers from your policy pages and passes anything unusual to your team. The general model is in the [AI outbound calling system overview](/solutions/ai-outbound-calling-system).

## Ecommerce calls an agent can make

- **High-value abandoned carts.** Ask whether something went wrong at checkout and help finish the order.
- **Failed delivery follow-up.** Confirm the address, offer a new delivery window and note it for the courier workflow.
- **Order confirmation for large or unusual orders.** Check sizes, address and delivery notes before dispatch.
- **Post-purchase check-in.** Ask whether the item arrived and whether it is as expected. See [AI survey calls](/solutions/ai-outbound-calling-for-surveys).
- **Win-back for lapsed buyers.** Ask why they have not returned and mention a fixed offer if you have one.
- **Back-in-stock and restock notices.** Call people who asked to be told about a specific item.
- **Subscription pause or renewal prompts.** Confirm whether a customer wants to continue.

## Message or call: a choice guide

| Situation | Better channel | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Low-value abandoned cart | Email or text | Call cost outweighs the basket |
| High-value cart with questions | Call | A person can answer sizing, shipping and returns |
| Failed delivery | Call | Time-sensitive, needs an answer |
| Review request | Message | Low friction, no conversation needed |
| Lapsed high-spend customer | Call | Worth the effort, can learn why they left |
| Customer who asked for a callback | Call | They requested it |

## A sample call

Illustrative, for a furniture store.

**Agent:** Hi, is that Hannah? I am an AI assistant calling for the furniture store. You left a sofa in your basket yesterday, and I wanted to see whether anything got in the way. Is now a good time?

**Caller:** Yes. I was not sure it would fit through my door.

**Agent:** That is a common concern. The sofa is 92 centimetres deep and arrives in two parts. If you tell me your doorway width, I can say whether it is likely to pass.

**Caller:** It is about 80.

**Agent:** The two-part delivery should manage that. Delivery takes seven to ten days, and returns are accepted within 30 days of delivery. Would you like me to send the checkout link again, or ask the team to call you?

**Caller:** Send the link.

## What to set up first

1. **Choose one trigger and one value threshold.** A cart above a value where a call makes economic sense.
2. **Confirm consent at checkout.** If numbers are collected for delivery only, check whether marketing calls are allowed.
3. **Load policies into the knowledge base**: shipping times, returns, sizing, warranty. Tell the agent not to invent anything beyond them.
4. **Keep offers simple and fixed.** If you use a discount, state it exactly once in the prompt.
5. **Set calling hours** for evenings and weekends where appropriate, and 1 or 2 retries rather than 5.
6. **Add a transfer or callback rule** for complaints and refund requests.
7. **Test on your own phone** with an awkward question about returns.

## Where AI outbound calling for ecommerce falls short

- **Most carts do not justify a call.** If margins are thin and baskets are small, stay with messages.
- **Unexpected calls feel intrusive.** Shoppers who gave a number only for delivery may not expect a sales call. Respect that.
- **It does not know your stock or orders unless you tell it.** Real-time inventory and order lookups are outside the built-in feature set, so pass context in the list or use webhooks and the API on Pro where available.
- **Refunds and complaints need a person.** The agent should capture details and hand over.
- **Rules differ by region.** Consent and calling-hours rules vary. This is general information, not legal advice, so check current rules and the [compliance overview](/solutions/ai-outbound-calling-compliance).

## Measuring whether it pays

Take one trigger for four weeks. Count calls placed, people reached, orders recovered and complaints raised. Compare cost per recovered order with your email baseline. The [ROI calculator](/resources/ai-calling-roi-calculator) can help you put your own numbers in, and your results will differ from anyone else's.

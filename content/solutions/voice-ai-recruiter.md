A voice AI recruiter is an AI agent that handles the phone side of recruiting: it calls applicants, asks screening questions in a natural conversation, answers basic questions about the role, and books the next step. It is built for recruiters, talent teams and agency owners whose day disappears into first-contact calls that follow the same pattern every time.

It does not choose who gets hired. It gets you to the point where a person can spend their time on the candidates who answered well and are free to talk.

## What a voice AI recruiter does for your hiring

- **Reaches applicants quickly.** A campaign starts calling the moment a list is uploaded, inside the hours you set, so candidates hear from you while they still remember applying.
- **Asks the same questions every time.** Right to work, availability, notice period, location, pay expectations, required certificates. The wording stays consistent from the first call to the five-hundredth.
- **Answers common questions.** Pay range, shift pattern, location, benefits, next steps. It draws on the knowledge base you provide and does not guess beyond it.
- **Books interviews.** It checks Google Calendar and offers real open slots, so the interview lands without a round of back-and-forth.
- **Retries people who did not pick up.** You set retry hours and up to 5 attempts, and the not-reached analytics show who never answered.
- **Hands off strong candidates.** A warm transfer sends a promising candidate to a recruiter while they are still on the line, when someone is available.
- **Leaves a record.** Each call has a transcript, an AI summary and a recording in the Calls section.

## Voice AI recruiter vs the usual process

| Step | Typical manual process | With a voice AI recruiter |
|---|---|---|
| First contact | Recruiter dials down a list between other tasks | Campaign dials inside your calling hours |
| Screening questions | Asked from memory or a loose script | Same questions, same order, logged |
| Missed calls | Often forgotten or retried once | Retried up to 5 times on your schedule |
| Scheduling | Several messages to land a slot | Booked on Google Calendar during the call |
| Notes | Typed after the call, if at all | Transcript and summary created automatically |

## A sample call

*Illustrative only, not a real call.*

**Agent:** Hello, this is an AI assistant calling for a company that received your application for the warehouse operative role. Is now an okay time for three quick questions?
**Caller:** Yes, go ahead.
**Agent:** Thanks. Are you able to work early shifts, starting at six?
**Caller:** Yes, mornings suit me fine.
**Agent:** Great. Do you hold a forklift licence?
**Caller:** Not yet, but I have used one on the job.
**Agent:** Noted. I can pass that to the hiring team. Would Thursday at ten or Friday at two work for a short interview?
**Caller:** Thursday at ten.
**Agent:** Booked. You will find the details in your confirmation. Thanks for your time.

## What to set up first

1. Write the agent prompt: who it represents, the tone, the exact screening questions and the order to ask them.
2. Add the role details, pay range and common objections to the knowledge base, and tell the agent what to say when it does not know.
3. Make the opening state clearly that it is an AI assistant.
4. Upload a small candidate list and confirm consent for contact before anything dials.
5. Set campaign hours and retries, up to 5, with retry hours that suit your candidates.
6. Connect Google Calendar so interviews are booked against real availability.
7. Use the test call to ring your own phone and play the awkward candidate: interrupt, go quiet, ask about pay.

## Where a voice AI recruiter falls short

It can sound capable and still miss what a recruiter would catch: hesitation about a gap, a career change that deserves a longer conversation, or a candidate who needs reassurance before they commit. It works from the answers it hears, so unusual accents, bad lines and noisy places can produce incomplete answers. It should not make final hiring decisions, and fair-hiring rules still apply to how you use its output. Reading the summaries is part of the process, not an optional extra.

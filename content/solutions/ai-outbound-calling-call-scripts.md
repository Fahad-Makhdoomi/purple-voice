Outbound AI call scripts work when they are short, goal-led and written for the ear. The person on the other end did not expect your call, so the first ten seconds must say who you are, why you are calling and what you want, then ask permission to continue. This page gives a six-part structure, tells you what belongs in the prompt rather than the script, and annotates one example. The template for the prompt itself is in the [prompt template resource](/resources/ai-voice-agent-prompt-template).

## The six parts of an outbound AI call script

- **1. Goal.** One sentence for the agent only: "Book a 20-minute demo" or "Confirm the delivery address." If it needs two goals, you need two agents.
- **2. Opening.** Name, who the call is for, that this is an AI assistant, the reason and a request for a moment.
- **3. Questions.** Two to four, each answerable in a sentence, ordered from easiest to most specific.
- **4. Facts and answers.** Prices, hours, eligibility and policies the agent may be asked about, kept in the knowledge base.
- **5. Next step.** Book on Google Calendar, transfer to a person, send details or end politely.
- **6. Exit.** What to do when the person is busy, disinterested, upset or asks to stop calling.

## Script versus prompt

| Element | Put in the script | Put in the prompt or knowledge base |
|---|---|---|
| Greeting wording | Yes, as an example | Tone and length limits |
| Questions | Order and purpose | Which to skip if already answered |
| Facts | Not inline | Knowledge base |
| Objections | One sentence each | Rule: never argue, accept a no |
| Booking | The ask | Calendar slots and confirmation text |
| Stop requests | Immediate acknowledgement | Mark the contact so they are not called again |

## An annotated example

Illustrative, for a bookkeeping service following up a website enquiry. Annotations are in brackets.

**Agent:** Hello, is that Ravi? [name check] This is an AI assistant calling on behalf of a bookkeeping firm. [disclosure] You asked about monthly bookkeeping on our website this morning. [reason] Is now a good moment for two quick questions? [permission]

**Caller:** I am between meetings. What is this about, again?

**Agent:** Just whether the service fits your business, and I can book a call with a bookkeeper. It will take about a minute. [short answer, repeats the benefit]

**Caller:** Fine.

**Agent:** How many invoices do you send in a typical month? [question 1, easy]

**Caller:** Maybe thirty.

**Agent:** And are you registered for VAT? [question 2, qualifying]

**Caller:** Yes.

**Agent:** Thank you. A bookkeeper can go through pricing with you in fifteen minutes. I have Thursday at 11 or Friday at 2. [next step]

## What to set up first

1. **Write the goal and the success outcome** before touching wording.
2. **Draft the opening aloud.** If you run out of breath, it is too long.
3. **Add the disclosure.** Say it is an AI assistant, in line with the [disclosure guidance](/blog/disclose-ai-on-calls).
4. **Write the five likely objections** with one honest reply each, and a rule to accept a no.
5. **Load facts into the knowledge base** and instruct "if not listed, say you will ask the team."
6. **Connect Google Calendar** and set a warm transfer for people who ask for a human.
7. **Test on your own phone** at least ten times, with different personalities: rushed, chatty, hostile, confused.
8. **Read transcripts after the first batch** and cut every sentence the agent repeats without helping.

## Common script mistakes

- **Starting with a pitch.** Ask permission and explain why you are calling first.
- **Too many questions.** Each extra question lowers completion.
- **Pretending the agent is a person.** It damages trust when discovered.
- **Over-long objection handling.** One sentence, then a graceful exit.
- **No plan for voicemail.** Decide whether the agent hangs up or leaves a brief message, based on your rules.

## Where scripts fall short

- **No script covers every call.** Unusual questions will appear. The agent should say it will ask the team, not guess.
- **A good script cannot fix a weak offer.** If people do not want the thing, wording will not change that.
- **Compliance wording is yours to check.** Disclosure, recording and opt-out language depend on your location. This is general information, not legal advice, so check current rules and the [compliance overview](/solutions/ai-outbound-calling-compliance).
- **Tone is partly the voice.** Pick a voice that suits the audience and test it.
- **Scripts age.** Review them monthly against what callers actually say.

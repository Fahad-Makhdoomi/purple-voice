Knockout questions in pre-screening are the few questions where a particular answer means the applicant does not meet a stated requirement. They save time, but each one also removes people from consideration, so they deserve more care than any other part of the script. When a voice AI agent asks them, the risk is repetition at scale: a badly chosen knockout applies to everyone, every time. Used well, they are narrow, neutral and reviewed.

## What an AI voice agent does with knockout questions

- **Asks them identically.** The same wording and order for every person, which is the main fairness advantage of scripting.
- **Confirms before it concludes.** A single "just to check, is that a firm no?" catches misheard answers.
- **Records the answer, not a verdict.** The summary says "stated no to Sunday shifts", not "rejected".
- **Closes politely.** Callers who do not fit are thanked and told what happens next rather than left hanging.
- **Escalates doubt.** If an answer is unclear, noisy or contradictory, the agent flags it for a person rather than picking a side.
- **Keeps a full trail.** Transcript and recording are there if you need to check what was asked and answered.

## Choosing what deserves to be a knockout

| Criterion | Good knockout? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Holds required licence or certificate | Usually | Objective and the role cannot run without it |
| Can work the posted shifts | Usually | Concrete and stated in the advert |
| Lives within travel distance | Careful | Many applicants will move or accept the commute; better scored |
| Minimum years of experience | Rarely | Often arbitrary; better scored |
| Salary expectation above the band | Careful | Can be discussed by a person first |
| Anything about age, family, health or background | No | Risks discrimination; do not ask |

## A sample call

*Illustrative only.*

**Agent:** The role requires a current forklift licence. Do you hold one?

**Caller:** I did, but it ran out last year.

**Agent:** So it is not current at the moment. Is that right?

**Caller:** Yes, I'd need to renew it.

**Agent:** Thank you for being clear. The team will review your application and be in touch. Is there anything else you would like me to pass on?

**Caller:** I can renew within a few weeks.

**Agent:** I will note that. Thanks for your time.

The agent neither argues nor rejects. It records the fact and the caller's point for a recruiter.

## What to set up first

1. **Write the justification next to each knockout.** If you cannot state why a criterion is a hard requirement, make it scored.
2. **Cap them at three.** Review the list any time you notice the pool shrinking.
3. **Word them about the requirement.** "Do you hold a current licence?" not "Are you old enough to drive?".
4. **Put the soft-close rule in the agent prompt.** Confirm once, record, close politely, no promises or rejections.
5. **Route every knockout to review.** Summaries tagged as a miss go to a reviewer before any decision is final.
6. **Add disclosure and consent.** The agent states it is an AI and confirms the person is happy to continue; see [disclosing AI on calls](/blog/disclose-ai-on-calls).
7. **Test with edge cases on your own phone.** Try expired, almost, and "it depends" answers.

## Knockout questions in pre-screening, kept fair

Consistency helps, but it does not make a bad criterion fair. Review outcomes occasionally: if a knockout removes a large share of applicants, check whether it is truly necessary. ## Where it falls short

- A knockout cannot understand context. "Only on weekdays" may be negotiable in a way the script cannot see.
- Hard rules applied at volume can quietly exclude good people. A human check on misses is not optional.
- Eligibility and employment questions are regulated in many places. This is general information, not legal advice; confirm current requirements.
- The agent cannot verify documents. A "yes" on a licence still needs checking by someone.

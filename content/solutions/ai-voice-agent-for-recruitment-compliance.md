AI recruitment calls compliance comes down to five habits: have a legitimate reason to call each candidate, say it is an AI, keep to sensible calling hours, ask only job-related questions, and keep a person responsible for decisions. This page is general information, not legal advice. Rules on calling, recording, automated decision-making and data protection vary by country and change, so check current requirements for your region before you launch.

## Why recruitment is a special case

Recruitment calls carry different expectations from sales calls. A candidate is handing over personal information that can affect their livelihood, and may feel unable to refuse. That raises the bar on transparency and fairness. There are also overlapping regimes: telephone marketing rules, data protection law, employment and equality law, and sometimes specific rules on automated decision-making. A voice agent does not change those obligations; it makes the way you meet them visible.

## What an AI voice agent can do to support compliance

- **Confirm consent at the start.** Purple Voice includes consent confirmation as a campaign setting, so the call opens with a clear check.
- **Disclose the AI.** You write the opening, so it can state that the caller is an AI assistant and who it is calling for.
- **Keep scripts consistent.** The same questions in the same order for every applicant makes an audit easier than a mix of informal calls.
- **Record what was said.** Transcripts, summaries and recordings give you evidence of what was asked and answered.
- **Respect calling hours.** Campaign hours and retry hours let you avoid unsociable times.
- **Offer a human.** The agent can be briefed to arrange a callback or transfer if a candidate prefers to speak to a person.
- **Honour opt-outs.** Candidates who ask not to be called are noted in summaries so you can update your records.

## Compliance checklist by issue

| Issue | What to consider | Practical step |
|---|---|---|
| Consent and basis for calling | How the number was obtained, what the candidate agreed to | Document it, and call only suitable lists |
| AI disclosure | Expectations and rules vary by place | State it at the start of every call |
| Recording | Consent and notice rules vary | Tell the candidate; keep only what you need |
| Calling hours | Local rules and courtesy | Set hours in the campaign |
| Fair process | Equality and fairness obligations | Ask only job-related questions; review outcomes |
| Data protection | Retention, access and security | Set retention rules; limit who sees transcripts |
| Opt-outs | Candidates can ask to stop | Update your records promptly |

## A sample opening

Illustrative. Adjust to your own legal advice.

**Agent:** Hello, this is an AI assistant calling on behalf of the recruitment team about your application for the administrator role. This call may be recorded and summarised. Is it all right to continue, or would you prefer a person to call you?
**Candidate:** Go ahead.
**Agent:** Thank you. I will ask a few questions about availability and location. You can decline any question, and a recruiter makes every decision about your application.

The last sentence matters: it tells the candidate where the human sits.

## What to set up first

1. **Audit your lists.** Know where each candidate number came from and what they agreed to.
2. **Write the disclosure and consent opening** and have whoever advises you on legal matters review it.
3. **Trim the question list** to job-related items only. Remove anything not needed for the decision.
4. **Set campaign hours and retries** conservatively; remember that up to 5 retries is a ceiling, not a target.
5. **Decide on recording and retention.** Choose how long transcripts and recordings stay and who can open them.
6. **Plan a human review.** Sample calls and outcomes on a schedule, and look for patterns affecting particular groups.
7. **Test-call your own phone,** including a candidate who refuses to continue or asks for a human.

## Where it falls short

A tool cannot make you compliant. If your consent records are weak, the agent will simply call faster. It cannot tell whether a candidate needs a reasonable adjustment unless they say so, and then it should hand over to a person. Automated screening that decides who is rejected without human review attracts extra scrutiny in several jurisdictions, so keep a recruiter responsible. Where a rule is unclear, ask a qualified adviser rather than guess.

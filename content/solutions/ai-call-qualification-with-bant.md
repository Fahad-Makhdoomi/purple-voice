BANT qualification AI means using a voice agent to check budget, authority, need and timing on a first call, then passing on only the leads that clear your bar. It suits teams with a straightforward sale and plenty of enquiries, who want a consistent filter before a salesperson spends 30 minutes on a call. For the wider picture, start with [AI call qualification](/solutions/ai-call-qualification).

## What an AI voice agent does with BANT

- **Asks about need first.** "What are you hoping to fix?" is a gentler opener than "What is your budget?"
- **Translates each letter into plain speech.** Nobody on a call says "authority"; the agent asks who else is involved.
- **Accepts ranges.** "Somewhere between five and ten thousand" is recorded as a range and compared with your minimum.
- **Notes what is missing.** A lead who will not discuss budget is marked as unknown, not as zero.
- **Applies your pass rule.** For example: need and timing required, budget or authority optional.
- **Sends the four answers to the rep.** The summary shows all four letters, so the salesperson does not repeat the questions.

## Turning each BANT letter into a spoken question

| Letter | Spoken question | What counts as a pass | If unclear |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Need | What made you start looking at this? | A problem you solve, in their own words | Ask once for an example |
| Timing | When would you like this in place? | Inside your sales cycle, for example 6 months | Offer options: this quarter, next, later |
| Authority | Who else would be part of choosing? | Caller decides or influences strongly | Flag for review and ask for the other person's name |
| Budget | Have you set aside a range, or are you still exploring? | Any range at or above your minimum | Mark unknown, do not push |

Budget is the question that most often ends calls. Phrase it as an offer of two honest answers, so a lead who has no figure yet can say so without feeling caught out.

## A sample call

*Illustrative only. Figures are made up for the example.*

**Agent:** Thanks for requesting a quote on the new website. I have a few quick questions so the right person calls you. Is that okay?
**Caller:** Go ahead.
**Agent:** What prompted you to look at a new site?
**Caller:** The current one doesn't work on phones and we're losing enquiries.
**Agent:** When would you want it live?
**Caller:** Before our summer season, so by May.
**Agent:** Will anyone else help decide?
**Caller:** Just me and my business partner.
**Agent:** Have you set aside a budget range, or are you still exploring?
**Caller:** Maybe four to six thousand.
**Agent:** That's helpful. I'll book you with a designer. Does Wednesday afternoon suit?

## What to set up first

1. Write your pass rule for each letter, with the exact threshold for budget and timing.
2. Decide which letters are required and which only flag a lead for review.
3. Put the four questions in the agent prompt, with an instruction to accept answers in any order.
4. Add pricing bands and package info to the knowledge base so the agent can answer "how much does it cost?" without improvising.
5. Configure Google Calendar booking and a warm-transfer number for passing leads.
6. Set campaign hours and retries, up to 5, so unreached leads are tried again.
7. Test-call your own phone: one strong lead, one with no budget, one who defers to a boss.

Then run the output through a [scorecard](/solutions/ai-call-qualification-scoring) if you want to rank passes instead of treating them equally.

## Where BANT falls short

- It was built for simple, single-buyer deals. A six-person committee does not fit on four letters.
- Budget answers are often guesses. Some buyers genuinely do not know until they see a proposal.
- Strict required-budget rules can drop leads that would have closed after a conversation with a person.
- It says nothing about fit, such as industry, size or technical environment. Add one or two fit questions of your own.
- BANT lead quality depends on how you wrote the thresholds. Review rejected leads for the first few weeks.

## Adding fit questions to BANT

BANT says nothing about whether the lead is the kind of customer you serve well. Add one or two fit questions to the agent: company size, industry, current tool, or a technical requirement that rules you in or out. Keep them factual and short. For example, a software firm might ask which system the lead uses today, since integration decides whether a deal is possible. Treat the fit answer as a knockout when it is a hard requirement and as a scoring input when it is merely a preference. This keeps the four letters as a minimum filter while letting your real deal history shape the rest of the call.

AI qualification for marketing agencies means giving each client a voice agent that phones the leads your campaigns produce, checks whether they are real prospects and reports the result. It is for agency owners who deliver form fills and then hear "the leads were rubbish" a fortnight later. A call within minutes gives you evidence either way. Method background: [AI call qualification](/solutions/ai-call-qualification).

## What an AI voice agent does for an agency

- **Calls every lead fast.** Campaign leads are phoned shortly after they arrive, before the client's team has even opened the email.
- **Uses the client's definition of good.** Each agent carries that client's questions, not a generic checklist.
- **Filters junk.** Wrong numbers, fake entries and people who do not remember enquiring are noted in the summary.
- **Books the good ones.** Qualified leads go onto the client's Google Calendar.
- **Gives you proof.** Transcripts, summaries and recordings sit in the Calls section.
- **Shows reach.** Not-reached analytics reveal how many leads never answered after up to 5 retries.
- **Keeps clients separate.** One agent, number and campaign per client avoids cross-contamination.

## Reporting that changes the conversation

| Metric | Why it matters to the client |
| --- | --- |
| Leads delivered | The number you were already reporting |
| Leads reached by phone | Shows contact rate, not just form volume |
| Qualified | Meets the client's own criteria |
| Booked | Appointments the client can attend |
| Not reached after retries | Separates "bad leads" from "unanswered leads" |
| Common disqualifiers | Tells you what to fix in targeting |

If most of the lost leads are "outside service area", that is a targeting problem you can fix. If they are "no longer interested", that points to ad copy or offer. Reports with reasons are more useful than totals; the Pro plan adds [insights](/pricing) to help with that.

## A sample call

*Illustrative only; no real brand names.*

**Agent:** Hello, I'm calling on behalf of a local dental clinic. You filled in a form about teeth whitening. Do you have a minute?
**Caller:** I think so, yes.
**Agent:** Are you looking to get it done in the next month or so, or just checking prices?
**Caller:** Prices at the moment.
**Agent:** The clinic lists whitening from a set price, and a consultation is free. Would you like me to book you in?
**Caller:** Maybe later, can you send me the price?
**Agent:** I'll note that, and a team member will follow up with the price list. Thank you for your time.

The summary tells the client: price-shopper, no booking, asked for information, so the agency can show the lead was real but early.

## What to set up first

1. Interview each client: what makes a lead worth a call, and what do they do with one.
2. Create one agent per client with the client's questions, hours and answers in its knowledge base.
3. Write consent wording for the forms, and agree who confirms what, so contact is expected.
4. Upload each campaign's leads, set calling hours in the client's time zone and retries up to 5.
5. Connect the client's Google Calendar or a transfer number for hot leads.
6. Test-call your own phone for every agent before real leads go in.
7. Agree a weekly report format, using call counts, summaries and sample recordings.

## Where it falls short

- It cannot fix a bad offer. If the campaign attracts the wrong audience, the agent will only measure that quickly.
- Multi-client accounts need per-client numbers and phone-provider billing. Plan this cost before you promise it.
- Clients may dislike an AI representing them. Agree disclosure wording and offer a human option.
- Lead consent comes from how the leads were gathered, and agencies often do not control that. Check current rules.
- The agent can only apply criteria the client has made clear, so vague briefs produce vague results.

## Objections you will hear from clients

Clients usually raise three worries. The first is brand risk: they do not want a robotic call going out in their name. Answer it by letting them hear test calls and approve the opening line and the disclosure wording. The second is data: they want to know where call recordings and summaries are kept and who can see them. Agree this in writing before launch. The third is cost: phone usage and AI usage are billed by those providers, not Purple Voice, so quote the client a realistic per-minute estimate and review it after the first batch. Handling these early turns a nervous client into one who asks for the agent on every campaign.

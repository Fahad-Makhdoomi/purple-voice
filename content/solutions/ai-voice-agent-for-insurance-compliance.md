AI insurance calls compliance comes down to four questions: is the person told it is an AI, did they agree to be called, is anything regulated being said, and is the data handled properly. This page is general information, not legal advice, so check current rules in your country and state and ask your compliance lead before you run a campaign.

The agent does not make you compliant, and it can make you non-compliant faster if it is configured carelessly. What it does is carry out the script you give it consistently, which is useful when the script is right.

## The boundary that matters most

Insurance is a regulated sector. Selling, advising and binding cover often require a licence, and conduct rules cover what you can say about price, benefits and exclusions. The sensible approach is to let the agent do information gathering and scheduling, and to keep every regulated step with a licensed person.

| Activity | Safe for the agent? | Keep with a licensed person |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirm contact details | Yes | No |
| Collect vehicle or property basics | Yes, if you approve the fields | Verification before pricing |
| Explain a plain term from your knowledge base | Usually | Interpretation of a policy |
| Quote a price | No, unless you add a rating link and compliance approves | Yes |
| Recommend a product or plan | No | Yes |
| Take first notice of loss | Yes | Coverage and liability decisions |
| Bind or change cover | No | Yes |

## What an AI voice agent should do for compliance

- **Disclose at the start.** A plain sentence that it is an automated assistant.
- **Refer on advice.** Phrases such as "an adviser will cover that" belong in the prompt as fixed responses.
- **Handle opt-outs at once.** "Do not call me" ends the call and removes the contact from the list.
- **Stay inside calling hours** and retry rules you set per campaign, up to five retries.
- **Record consent confirmation** where you have it, using the campaign's consent step.
- **Keep records.** Transcripts and recordings are available in Calls for audit.
- **Avoid sensitive questions.** No health details, ID numbers or payment data on the call.

## Setting up for ai insurance calls compliance

1. **Write down the rules that apply.** Calling windows, consent type, recording notice and licensing limits. Ask a qualified adviser to confirm.
2. **Put the disclosure and referral lines into the agent prompt** and have your compliance lead approve the wording.
3. **Load only approved facts** into the knowledge base. Remove anything that reads like advice.
4. **Build your list from consented sources.** Document where each number came from.
5. **Use the consent confirmation** step on campaigns where your rules require it.
6. **Set campaign hours and retries** to respect local rules and customer goodwill.
7. **Test the boundaries on your own phone.** Ask for a price, a recommendation and "is that covered". Each should be referred on.
8. **Review a sample of transcripts weekly** and keep the findings.

## A sample call

Illustrative only.

**Agent:** Hello, this is an automated assistant for an insurance agency. This call may be recorded. You asked about contents cover. Is now a good time?
**Caller:** Which policy is best for me?
**Agent:** I cannot recommend a policy. A licensed adviser can go through your options. Shall I book a call?
**Caller:** Actually, please do not call me again.
**Agent:** Understood. I have removed your number from our list. Thank you, and goodbye.

## Where it falls short

Rules change, differ across borders and are enforced by people who read the details. A good prompt reduces the risk and does not remove it, and models can occasionally say something outside the script. Wrong-number calls, shared phones and unclear consent on purchased lists are all risks that sit outside the agent. Documentation, training and review are on you.

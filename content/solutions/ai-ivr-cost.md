AI IVR cost has four layers: the platform, the phone line, the AI model behind the conversation, and any set-up or integration work. Quotes look wildly different because vendors bundle those layers in different ways, so the only reliable comparison is your own call volume priced through each layer. This guide is for anyone who has collected two or three quotes and cannot tell them apart.

## The cost layers in an AI IVR

- **Platform fee.** The monthly subscription for the agent, dashboard and features. With Purple Voice: Free $0, Basic $19, Pro $299, Enterprise custom.
- **Included minutes.** Free includes 100 minutes a month, Basic 1,000, Pro 20,000. Check what happens beyond the allowance.
- **Telephony.** Number rental and per-minute carrier charges, billed by your provider (Twilio, Plivo, SuperPhone), not by Purple Voice.
- **AI usage.** The language model and speech layer. At Purple Voice this layer is managed per customer and the usage is billed by those providers.
- **Set-up.** Writing the prompt, loading the knowledge base, connecting Google Calendar. Your time, or an adviser's.
- **Extras.** Seats, extra numbers, integrations, API access, priority support.

## Questions that make quotes comparable

| Ask the vendor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What is included in the monthly fee? | Some include minutes, some do not |
| Who bills telephony and AI usage? | Determines the second and third invoice |
| What is the overage rate? | Avoids a shock in a busy month |
| Are transcripts and recordings extra? | Often free, sometimes not |
| Is there a contract or minimum term? | Affects switching cost |
| What does a human transfer cost? | Transfers can consume two phone legs |

## A worked illustrative example

Imagine a small clinic with 40 inbound calls on a weekday and 15 at weekends: about 1,000 calls a month, averaging two minutes. That is roughly 2,000 minutes. This is not a benchmark; substitute your own figures.

- A Basic-sized plan covers 1,000 minutes, so you would need a larger tier or accept overage.
- If only after-hours calls go to the agent, perhaps 25 percent of calls, that is about 500 minutes, which fits within Basic's allowance.
- Telephony and AI usage then sit on top, billed by the providers.

The lesson is to price the scope you actually intend. Covering every call and covering only the missed ones are different budgets.

## Comparing AI IVR cost against the alternative

A fair comparison includes what you pay today:

1. **Missed-call value.** Calls that went to voicemail, times your conversion rate, times average job or sale value.
2. **Staff time.** Hours spent on repeat questions and booking, valued at wages.
3. **Answering service.** If you pay one now, its monthly and per-call charges.
4. **Old IVR running costs.** Licences, maintenance, re-recording.

Use the [ROI calculator](/resources/ai-calling-roi-calculator) to put numbers on this. The general pricing logic is also covered in [AI voice agent pricing explained](/blog/ai-voice-agent-pricing-explained).

## A sample budgeting conversation

Illustrative.

Owner: We get about 600 minutes of calls a month. Is Basic enough?
Adviser: On minutes, yes, with room to spare. What's in your telephony bill today?
Owner: About the same as a number rental and some minutes.
Adviser: Then your added cost is the plan plus AI usage. Run it on Free for a month first and compare real minutes.

## What to set up first when budgeting

1. Export or estimate last month's inbound call count and average duration.
2. Decide the scope: every call, after hours only, or overflow only.
3. Start on the Free plan to measure real usage.
4. Build the agent properly so calls end fast and correctly; bad prompts cost minutes.
5. Check the transfer rules, since transfers add telephony cost.
6. Review minutes weekly, not monthly.

## Where cost comparisons mislead

A cheaper per-minute rate can hide a poor agent that needs more minutes per call. Conversely, the most feature-rich plan is wasted if you take thirty calls a month. Unpredictable call spikes, such as a seasonal rush, break flat estimates, so model a busy month too. And the cost of an AI IVR is not only money: someone must maintain the knowledge base. Finally, do not count savings you will not actually realise, such as hours freed that nobody redeploys.

An AI voice agent for warehouse hiring checks the handful of facts that decide whether an applicant can do the job: shift pattern, forklift or other certification, how they will get to site and when they can start. It books a site visit or trial shift for the people who pass. It is built for logistics firms and the agencies that supply them, where roles are numerous and the same screening call is repeated all week.

## Short roles, long lists

Warehouse and logistics hiring has a rhythm. Peak season demands fast headcount, turnover is high, and the roles are defined more by logistics than by CV: can you work nights, can you reach the site, do you hold the ticket? The answers are quick to collect and expensive to collect one by one.

The challenge is also contact: many applicants are on shift, driving or sleeping when a recruiter calls. A call at 2 pm gets voicemail; a call at 6 pm might get a human. An agent that retries at defined times closes that gap without a person watching the clock.

## What an AI voice agent does for warehouse hiring

- **Confirms the shift pattern.** Days, nights, rotating or weekend work are stated plainly, so mismatches drop out early.
- **Asks about certification.** Forklift, reach truck, first aid or driving category, plus expiry, recorded as the applicant states them.
- **Checks commute and transport.** Postcode or town, and whether they have a reliable way to reach the site at shift start.
- **States pay and terms.** Hourly rate, agency or direct, and start date come from your knowledge base.
- **Asks about physical requirements.** Lifting or standing requirements are described so applicants can self-select honestly.
- **Books site visits and trial shifts.** Slots are offered from Google Calendar.
- **Retries at sensible times.** Up to 5 attempts, including evening retry hours.

## Example applicant journey

| Step | What the agent does | What a person does |

| Applicant submits form | Calls the same day | Nothing yet |

## A sample call

Illustrative.

**Agent:** Hello, I am an AI assistant calling for the logistics team about your application for the warehouse operative role. Do you have a minute?
**Applicant:** Yes.
**Agent:** The role is a night shift, 10 pm to 6 am, Sunday to Thursday, at the site by the ring road. Can you work that pattern?
**Applicant:** Yes, I do nights now.
**Agent:** Do you hold a forklift ticket?
**Applicant:** Counterbalance, yes. It runs out in March.
**Agent:** Thanks, I have noted counterbalance and the March expiry. How would you get to the site?
**Applicant:** I have a car.
**Agent:** Good. I can book a site visit this week. Would Wednesday at 5 pm work?

## What to set up first

1. **Write the eligibility gates.** Order them by how often they exclude people: shift first, then location, then certificate.
2. **Add site facts to the knowledge base:** address, entrances, parking, start times, pay and what to bring to a first visit.
3. **Describe the work honestly.** Cold storage, heavy lifting and target rates should be stated in the script so applicants are not surprised.
4. **Set campaign hours and retries.** Think about when people finish a shift or school run, and use up to 5 retries.
5. **Connect Google Calendar** for site-visit slots, with sensible limits per slot.
6. **Turn on consent confirmation and AI disclosure.**
7. **Test-call your own phone,** including the case of an applicant who is driving or hard to hear.

## Where it falls short

The agent records a ticket; it cannot see it. Certificates and right-to-work documents must be checked in person or by your onboarding team. Noisy environments, poor reception and unfamiliar accents can lower transcription quality, so read the transcript when a negative outcome depends on one unclear answer. It also does not replace a supervisor's view of whether someone will cope with the pace of a site.

Good cold call opening lines do four things in about ten seconds: say who is calling, give a reason that is believable for this person, ask something easy to answer, and sound like a human being. Most openers fail because they try to pitch instead of simply earning another thirty seconds.

This article covers what each of those four jobs means, twelve examples for different situations, and how to hand an opener to an AI voice agent so it comes out natural rather than recorded.

## What the first ten seconds are for

The person on the other end is deciding two things almost instantly: is this a person or a pitch, and is it relevant to me? They are not deciding whether to buy. Your opener's only goal is to keep the conversation going for the next minute.

That changes what you write. You do not need to explain your product. You need to answer, quickly, the unspoken questions: who are you, why me, and how long is this going to take.

## The four jobs of an opener

1. **Identify.** Give a first name and company. Skipping this is the fastest way to sound evasive.
2. **Justify.** Give a reason this call is happening. If the person asked for something, say so. If not, say why you picked them.
3. **Lower the stakes.** Ask a small question or give a time limit. "Is now a reasonable moment?" or "Can I take thirty seconds to say why I called?" are small asks.
4. **Sound human.** Short sentences, plain words, no sales vocabulary. Read it out loud. If you would not say it to a colleague, rewrite it.

## Twelve openers, with why they work

### For warm leads (they asked for something)

The reason for the call is built in, so lead with it.

1. **"Hi Priya, this is Alex at Harbour Dental. You asked about whitening on our site this morning, so I'm calling to arrange a time. Is now okay?"**
   States the action the person took, so the call makes sense immediately.
2. **"Hello, is that Mark? It's the assistant at Brightside Roofing. You requested a roof inspection yesterday evening. I just wanted to confirm a few details. Have you got two minutes?"**
   The time ask is specific and small.
3. **"Hi Dana, I'm calling from Northwind about the demo you booked for Thursday. I wanted to check the time still suits you."**
   A confirmation call is the easiest kind to open because the person already agreed to something.
4. **"Hi Tom, you downloaded our pricing guide earlier. I'm calling to see if you had any questions about it."**
   Offers help rather than a pitch. Only use if the guide really was requested.

### For cold or semi-cold lists

Here you need a reason that is true and relevant. Avoid invented flattery.

5. **"Hi Sam, this is Jordan at Lantern Insurance. I'm calling because you're listed as the owner of a three-bedroom property in the area and we help with home cover renewals. Is this a bad time?"**
   Gives a specific reason you picked this person.
6. **"Hi, is that Ms Okafor? Jordan here from Lantern. I'll be honest, this is a cold call. Can I tell you in one sentence why I'm calling, and you can tell me to go away?"**
   Candour disarms people. It works best when the one sentence really is short.
7. **"Hi Chris, it's the assistant at Corvid Software. We work with small agencies on scheduling. I'm not sure it's relevant for you, so I wanted to ask one quick question first."**
   Admitting uncertainty lowers defences and the question qualifies.
8. **"Hello, this is the AI assistant at Corvid Software calling on behalf of Jess. Do you have a minute?"**
   Discloses the AI up front. Some teams prefer this because it removes the trust question; see [disclose AI on calls](/blog/disclose-ai-on-calls).

### For reactivation and re-engagement

9. **"Hi Leo, it's Alex at Brightside. You asked about solar quotes last spring and we never followed up properly. Is it still something you're thinking about?"**
   Takes responsibility for the gap, which is honest and disarming. For the wider approach see [reactivate old leads with AI calls](/blog/reactivate-old-leads-with-ai-calls).
10. **"Hi Ana, we spoke a few months ago about roof repairs. I'm calling to see if anything has changed."**
    Short, low pressure.

### For inbound-style and referral calls

11. **"Hi Ben, Maria at Northwind suggested I call you. She thought you might be looking at scheduling tools. Is that right?"**
    Only use if the referral is real and the referrer agreed.
12. **"Hello, you left us a missed call earlier. This is the assistant at Harbour Dental returning it. How can I help?"**
    Easy and useful.

## What to avoid

- **"How are you today?"** People have learned to hear it as the start of a pitch.
- **"Did I catch you at a bad time?"** It invites a yes. "Is now a reasonable moment?" is no better, but "Have you got two minutes?" is at least specific.
- **Reading a company slogan.** Nobody has ever stayed on a call for one.
- **Dishonest hooks.** Pretending to be returning a call, implying a prior relationship, or hiding the sales purpose. Beyond being a poor way to start a relationship, in some places it can cause legal problems. This article is general information, not legal advice, so check current rules where you call.
- **Opening with the pitch.** If your first sentence contains your product's features, you have skipped the stage where the person agrees to listen.

## Putting an opener into an AI voice agent

For an agent, the opener is the one part worth writing out nearly verbatim. Reasons:

- It is the least dependent on what the person says, since they have said nothing yet.
- Its wording has the largest effect on whether the call survives.
- Giving the agent a fixed opening removes a source of variation while you test.

Tell the agent to keep the structure (identify, justify, small ask) but vary the wording slightly, so it does not sound identical on every call. Then give it rules for the first reply:

| First reply | What the agent should do |
|---|---|
| "Yes, go ahead" | Move straight to the first question. |
| "Who is this?" | Repeat name, company and reason in one sentence. |
| "Not a good time" | Offer a specific callback window, confirm, end. |
| "Is this a robot?" | Answer honestly and offer to continue or arrange a person. |
| Silence or "hello?" | Repeat the opening once, then end the call politely. |

## Testing openers

Opener testing is simple but needs discipline. Change one thing at a time (the reason, the question, the length) and compare how many calls get past the first reply. In a small campaign the numbers will be noisy, so judge by reading transcripts as well as counting. A call that ends at "who is this?" with the agent's answer unclear is a clear sign the justification line is weak.

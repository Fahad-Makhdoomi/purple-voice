A sensible voicemail strategy for sales starts from a simple fact: most outbound calls are not answered, so what you do about the no-answer is a bigger part of your results than the script you use on the calls that connect. The choice is usually between leaving a voicemail, retrying quickly with an AI follow-up call, or combining them in a set cadence.

There is no single right answer. This article lays out the trade-offs, gives a sample schedule you can adapt, and explains how retries work with an AI agent. For related timing questions see [best time to call leads](/blog/best-time-to-call-leads) and [follow-up cadence for new leads](/blog/follow-up-cadence-for-new-leads).

## What a voicemail does and does not do

A voicemail is a low-cost way to give a lead a reason to call you back or to expect your next attempt. Its realistic effects are modest:

- Some people call back, mostly when they asked to be contacted.
- Some people recognise your number or name on the next attempt and are more likely to answer.
- Some people never listen to it. Many phones show a transcript or nothing at all.

Treat it as a supporting touch, not the main event. If you are building the whole plan on callbacks, you are depending on a behaviour most people do not have.

## When leaving a voicemail makes sense

Leave one when:

- **The lead asked to be contacted.** A message saying you called about their enquiry is relevant and expected.
- **You have a specific, useful reason.** For example confirming an appointment or a quote they requested.
- **The relationship is existing.** Customers and past leads often appreciate a short message.

Skip it, or leave it for later attempts, when:

- **The list is cold.** A message from an unknown company about something they did not ask for is more likely to annoy than help.
- **You are about to call again within hours.** Two voicemails in a morning looks like pressure.
- **Local rules limit it.** Pre-recorded and artificial voices are regulated in various countries, and what applies to a live person leaving a message may differ from an automated drop. This is general information, not legal advice. Check current rules where you call; see [is AI cold calling legal](/blog/is-ai-cold-calling-legal) for an overview.

## What a good voicemail says

Keep it under twenty seconds. A workable structure:

1. Who you are and where you are calling from.
2. Why you called, tied to something the person did.
3. One reason to respond or one clear next step.
4. How to reach you, said slowly, once.

Example for a warm lead (illustrative): "Hi Priya, this is Alex at Harbour Dental. You asked about whitening on our website this morning. I'll try you again this afternoon, or you can call us on the number you called from. Thanks."

Notice what is missing: pitch, features, and urgency. Notice what is present: a concrete expectation (another attempt this afternoon), which makes the later call less of a surprise.

## Voicemail drop vs AI follow-up call

They solve different problems.

| | Voicemail drop or message | AI follow-up call |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | Leaves a message for later | Tries again to reach the person live |
| Effort for the lead | Must listen and act | None unless they pick up |
| Best for | Warm leads, confirmations | Any lead where speed or reach matters |
| Main limitation | Often unheard | Only helps if timing and hours are sensible |
| Risk | Annoyance if repeated, rules on recorded voices | Over-calling if the retry schedule is aggressive |

The question to ask is: what is more likely to get this person talking to us? For a lead who asked to be contacted an hour ago, another live attempt in twenty minutes usually beats a voicemail. For a lead you reached last week who said "call back in a month", a short voicemail the day before is a courtesy.

In practice, many teams do not choose one. They retry live a set number of times at different hours, leave one voicemail near the start or the end of the sequence, and add a text or email.

## A sample retry cadence

This is an illustration, not a rule. Adjust it to your market and to the lead's warmth.

**Warm lead who just enquired:**

| Attempt | Timing | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Within minutes | Live call |
| 2 | Same day, a few hours later | Live call, different time of day |
| 3 | Next day, morning | Live call; leave one short voicemail if appropriate |
| 4 | Day 3 | Live call at a different hour |
| 5 | Day 5 | Final attempt; send a short email or text saying you tried |

**Cold list:**

| Attempt | Timing | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Weekday late morning | Live call, no voicemail |
| 2 | Different day, late afternoon | Live call |
| 3 | A week later, different time | Live call; optional voicemail |

The varied times matter. Calling at the same hour every time tends to hit the same meeting, commute or lunch, and gets the same result.

## Following up when the voicemail and retries fail

At the end of the cadence, the lead has not been reached. Options:

- **Send a short message through another channel** (from your own email or messaging tool for now, since Purple Voice does not send messages from the agent yet) noting that you tried and how to reach you.
- **Move the lead to a nurture list** and revisit in a few weeks.
- **Review the number.** Many unanswered calls are wrong or inactive numbers, which is a data problem, not a messaging problem.

If a campaign finishes with a large share not reached, look at the list and the hours before changing the script.

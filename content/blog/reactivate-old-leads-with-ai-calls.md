To reactivate old leads, call them with a short, honest message that refers to their earlier interest, ask whether anything has changed, and make it easy to say yes or no. A dormant database is one of the lower-risk places to start with AI calling, because the leads already know your name, the stakes per contact are small, and nobody is waiting for you to be fast.

This guide covers how to prepare the list, what to say, how to brief the agent, and how to read results without fooling yourself. For the full campaign mechanics see [how to launch an AI calling campaign](/blog/how-to-launch-an-ai-calling-campaign).

## Why dormant leads are worth a call

Every sales team has a list of people who enquired, got a quote, went quiet and were never contacted again. They cost money to acquire, and a follow-up costs very little now. Circumstances change: a budget gets approved, a competitor disappoints, a house is finally ready to renovate. A call is the cheapest way to find out.

A phone call also works better than email for this job because the question is simple ("is this still on your mind?") and the answer is quick. A voice agent can work through a few hundred contacts in a few days without anyone on your team giving up their afternoon.

The caveat is honesty about expectations. Most dormant leads will not answer, and most who do will say no. The value comes from the few who say yes.

## Step 1: clean and segment the list

List quality decides the result more than anything you write. Before any call:

- **Remove duplicates and obvious junk.** Fake numbers, test entries, staff and existing customers.
- **Check consent and source.** Know where each lead came from and what they agreed to. If you cannot say how you got a number, do not call it.
- **Scrub against suppression lists.** Do-not-call registries and your own opt-outs must be honoured; see [do not call lists and dialer hygiene](/blog/do-not-call-lists-and-dialer-hygiene).
- **Fix formatting.** Numbers in a consistent international format avoid failed calls.
- **Add context fields** such as the original enquiry, the date, and the product they asked about. The agent can use these to make the call relevant.

Then segment. A single message to everyone is weaker than three targeted ones:

| Segment | Example | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| Had a quote, did not proceed | Quote sent six months ago | Ask what held it up and whether anything has changed |
| Enquired, never spoke | Form fill with no contact | Apologise for the gap and ask if they still need help |
| Previous customer | Bought once, nothing since | Check in, ask if they need anything |

Segmenting also gives you a way to compare results.

## Step 2: write an honest, specific opener

The opener should do the work of reminding them who you are and why you are calling, without inventing urgency. A weak one: "We have a special offer for you." A better one:

> "Hi Leo, this is the assistant at Brightside Solar, calling on behalf of the team. You asked about solar quotes last spring and we never followed up properly. Is it still something you're thinking about?"

Note the pieces: who you are, honest about being automated, a specific past event, an admission about the gap, and a question with an easy answer.

For more opener structures see [cold call opening lines](/blog/cold-call-opening-lines).

## Step 3: brief the agent

Keep the brief tight. For a reactivation call, a good brief has:

**Purpose.** Find out whether the person is still interested, and if so book a short call or visit.

**Opener.** As above, close to final wording.

**What to find out.** Whether they still need it, what has changed, and when they would be ready.

**Branches:**

- Still interested: ask one or two qualifying questions, then offer a booking.
- Not now but later: ask roughly when and note it, then end politely.
- Not interested: thank them, end the call, mark for no further contact if they say so.
- Wrong person or number changed: apologise and end.
- Asks to be removed: confirm and end; remove from all future campaigns.

**Limits.** Do not quote prices not in the knowledge base. Do not push after a clear no. Transfer to a person if asked.

The structure is covered in more detail in [how to write an AI voice agent prompt](/blog/how-to-write-an-ai-voice-agent-prompt).

## Step 5: read the results realistically

After the pilot, review three things:

- **Reached.** How many contacts answered? Low numbers often mean old or wrong data rather than a bad script.
- **Outcomes.** Of those reached, how many were interested, how many said later, how many said no?
- **Transcripts.** Read a sample of calls, especially the short ones. They show where the opener fails or where people are confused.

Do not extrapolate from a handful of calls. If a pilot of 100 gave you a few bookings, that is encouraging, but small samples swing a lot. Run a second batch before planning around the number. ## Mistakes to avoid

- **Calling everyone at once.** A pilot catches problems before they affect the whole list.
- **Pretending the lead is fresh.** People can tell. Acknowledge the gap.
- **Ignoring opt-outs.** Anyone who says stop must be removed immediately and permanently.
- **Offering a discount as a hook.** Unless it is real and authorised, it creates trouble. A genuine question works better.
- **Treating old consent as permanent.** Check your records and the rules that apply.

This article is general information, not legal advice. Consent and calling rules vary by country and by how the data was collected, so check the current requirements before you launch.

## What to do with the results

Sort the outcomes into groups and act on each:

- **Interested and booked:** pass to sales with the transcript and summary.
- **Interested later:** add to a calendar reminder or a dated follow-up.
- **No:** mark and stop.
- **Not reached:** consider whether the number is bad; try one more window or move to email.

The call summaries and transcripts help here because the sales team sees what the person actually said before they pick up the phone.

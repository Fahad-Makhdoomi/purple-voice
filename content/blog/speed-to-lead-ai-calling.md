Speed to lead is how quickly you reach someone after they show interest. The first few minutes matter because the person is still at their screen, still thinking about the problem and probably still asking other providers. Calling in minutes instead of hours is one of the cheapest improvements a sales team can make, and an AI voice agent is a practical way to do it every time, including at 9pm on a Sunday.

This playbook explains why timing matters, what the first automated call should do and how to set it up.

## Why the first minutes matter

Think about what happens when someone submits an enquiry form.

- They are in the middle of a task. A few hours later they are in meetings, driving or have moved on.
- They have often enquired with more than one company. Whoever talks to them first sets the frame.
- Their interest is highest at the moment of the form. A cold callback is a different conversation from a warm one.
- A fast reply signals that you will be responsive later too.

Many teams report that response time is one of the strongest predictors of whether a lead becomes a conversation. Exact figures vary widely by industry and I would not trust a universal number, but the direction is consistent: faster tends to be better.

## The gap most teams have

Most businesses cannot guarantee a call within minutes. Typical gaps:

- Leads arrive out of hours or on weekends.
- The team is on other calls or on site.
- Leads are batched and worked once a day.
- The person responsible for follow-up is on holiday.

An automated first call closes that gap. It does not replace the human follow-up, it makes sure the first contact happens while the lead is still warm.

## What the first call should do

The automated call has one job: turn an enquiry into a conversation and a next step. It should:

1. **Identify itself and the reason for the call.** "Hi Priya, it's Sam from Northside Roofing, calling about the inspection you asked for just now."
2. **Check the timing.** "Is now an okay moment?" If not, offer to call back at a time they choose.
3. **Confirm the enquiry.** A single question to confirm what they want.
4. **Ask two or three qualifying questions.** Location, timeline, budget range, whichever are decisive for you. See [AI lead qualification](/blog/ai-lead-qualification-calls).
5. **Offer a next step.** A booked appointment, a transfer to a person right now, or a clear promise of what happens next.
6. **Log the outcome.** Transcript, summary and status so the human knows where things stand.

If the lead sounds hot and a person is available, the agent can transfer immediately. The design of that handoff is in [warm transfer from AI to human](/blog/warm-transfer-ai-to-human).

## A sample opening

> "Hi, is that Jordan? It's Alex calling from Brightside Solar. You filled in a quote request a couple of minutes ago, so I thought I'd call while it's fresh. Have you got two minutes?"

Why this works: it gives a real reason, shows the call is expected, and asks a low-effort question. A weak opener is "Hi, I'm calling about solar", which sounds like cold outreach even when it is not. For more on openings, see [cold call opening lines](/blog/cold-call-opening-lines).

## Setting up an automatic first call

### 1. Decide the trigger

Typical triggers are a website form, an ad lead form, a booking request or a chat enquiry. You need a way to get each new lead's name, number and enquiry into a campaign. You can upload lists in batches, or use webhooks and automations on Pro to push leads in as they arrive.

### 2. Write the agent's brief

- Who it is and who it represents.
- The context it should assume: "the person just enquired."
- Qualifying questions and what counts as qualified.
- What to offer: booking, transfer, or callback.
- What it must not do: quote prices it has not been given, make promises, argue.

### 3. Set calling hours

Calling at 3am helps nobody. Decide the hours in which the agent may call, and what happens to leads that arrive outside them. A reasonable approach is to call as soon as the window opens, and send a message in the meantime if you have one.

### 4. Plan retries

If the lead does not answer, retry. A common pattern is a second attempt a few minutes later, then a few hours later, then the next day. Purple Voice allows up to five retries with retry hours you set. See [follow-up cadence for new leads](/blog/follow-up-cadence-for-new-leads) for sequences.

### 5. Test it

Submit your own form with your own number and see how quickly the call arrives and how it sounds. Check that the context matches.

## Handling the awkward cases

| Situation | What the agent should do |
| --- | --- |
| Lead says it's a bad time | Offer a callback time and log it |
| Lead is confused why you called | Reference the enquiry, apologise briefly, offer to end the call |
| Lead asks for a person | Transfer or promise a specific callback time |
| Lead says they did not enquire | Apologise, confirm the number, end politely, flag for removal |
| Voicemail answers | Leave a short message or hang up, then retry per schedule |
| Lead is clearly not a fit | Thank them, close politely, log reason |

## Consent and form wording

The form should make clear that the person may be called, and by what means. Regulations differ by country and by type of call, and automated calls can have stricter requirements than manual ones. This is general information, not legal advice, so check the rules for each market and have your form wording reviewed. Also consider whether you disclose that the call is made by an AI; see [disclose AI on calls](/blog/disclose-ai-on-calls).

## Measuring whether it works

Compare before and after on a few simple numbers.

- **Median time to first contact attempt.**
- **Contact rate:** share of leads you actually spoke with.
- **Booked rate:** share of leads who booked.
- **Show rate:** share of bookings that attended.
- **Complaints or opt-outs.**

If contact rate rises and booked rate holds, the playbook is working. If contact rate rises and complaints too, review the opener and the consent wording. Details in [AI calling metrics that matter](/blog/ai-calling-metrics-that-matter).

## Common mistakes

- **Letting leads queue until morning** when the agent could call during permitted hours.
- **Opening as if cold** instead of referencing the enquiry.
- **Too many questions** on a first call; two or three is enough.
- **No human follow-up** for leads who asked to talk to a person.
- **Retrying too aggressively.** Several attempts a day will upset people.
- **Not reading transcripts.** The first week of transcripts tells you more than any dashboard.

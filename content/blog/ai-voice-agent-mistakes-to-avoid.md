The most damaging AI voice agent mistakes are not technical. They are decisions made before the first call: a vague goal, an untested prompt, a dirty list, no review habit. All of them are cheap to fix at launch and expensive to fix after a thousand calls.

Here are ten mistakes that come up again and again, with the reason each happens and a specific fix. Treat it as a pre-launch audit.

## 1. Giving the agent several jobs

**What happens:** The prompt says qualify, pitch, handle support, collect feedback and book. The agent tries to do all of it, and calls wander.

**Why:** It feels efficient to cover everything in one agent.

**Fix:** One agent, one outcome. If you need a second job, build a second agent. Write the goal as a single sentence at the top of the prompt, as set out in [how to create an AI voice agent](/blog/how-to-create-an-ai-voice-agent).

## 2. Writing a script instead of a brief

**What happens:** The agent reads lines at people and gets stuck when they answer off-script.

**Why:** Teams port their old call script directly into the prompt.

**Fix:** Describe the role, goal, questions and limits, and let the agent phrase things itself. [Outbound call script for AI agents](/blog/outbound-call-script-for-ai-agents) explains how to rework a script into a prompt.

## 3. Leaving gaps in the knowledge

**What happens:** A lead asks about pricing or terms and the agent improvises an answer that sounds confident and is wrong.

**Why:** Nobody wrote the answers down.

**Fix:** Add the ten most common questions to the knowledge base with real answers. Then tell the agent what to do with everything else: say a colleague will confirm, and take a contact detail. Never let it guess prices.

## 4. Skipping the real test

**What happens:** The first person to find a problem is a paying lead.

**Why:** The agent sounded fine in a quick demo.

**Fix:** Use the test call on your own phone and play friendly, awkward, sceptical and hostile leads. [Test your AI voice agent](/blog/test-your-ai-voice-agent) has the full checklist. Include the refusal test, because a mishandled "stop calling me" is the one mistake you cannot afford.

## 5. Launching on a messy list

**What happens:** Duplicate contacts get two calls, wrong numbers get dialled, and people who asked to be removed hear from you again.

**Why:** The list went straight from an export to a campaign.

**Fix:** Clean before you upload: one contact per row, full international numbers, duplicates removed, opt-outs and do-not-call numbers excluded. [Do-not-call lists and dialer hygiene](/blog/do-not-call-lists-and-dialer-hygiene) covers the routine, and [how to launch an AI calling campaign](/blog/how-to-launch-an-ai-calling-campaign) shows where it fits.

## 6. Calling at the wrong hours, too often

**What happens:** Leads get calls at inconvenient times and repeated attempts feel like harassment. Answer rates stay low.

**Why:** Defaults were never checked, and more retries seemed better.

**Fix:** Set calling hours in the lead's local time, space retries across different parts of the day, and keep attempts modest. Purple Voice supports up to 5 retries with retry hours, but most lists do not need all five. See [best time to call leads](/blog/best-time-to-call-leads).

## 7. Ignoring the rules

**What happens:** The campaign runs without anyone checking consent, disclosure or recording requirements, and the risk lands on your business.

**Why:** Compliance feels like a later problem.

**Fix:** Check the current rules for each country you call before you start, decide how the agent identifies itself, and confirm consent when you set up a campaign. This is general information, not legal advice, so take advice for your situation. Begin with [is AI cold calling legal](/blog/is-ai-cold-calling-legal) and [disclose AI on calls](/blog/disclose-ai-on-calls).

## 8. Launching to the whole list on day one

**What happens:** A flaw in the opening line reaches every contact before anyone notices.

**Why:** Enthusiasm, and a sense that volume equals speed.

**Fix:** Start with ten to twenty contacts. Read every transcript. Fix the first three problems. Then widen. The Free and Basic plans cap campaigns at 100 contacts, which is a sensible size for a first run anyway.

## 9. Never reading the transcripts

**What happens:** The agent runs for weeks with the same flaw, and metrics drift without explanation.

**Why:** Summaries look fine, and nobody owns the review.

**Fix:** Pick a day and twenty minutes each week. Read a mix of booked, transferred, short and random calls, tag the problems and fix the top pattern. The routine is laid out in [improve your AI agent with call transcripts](/blog/improve-ai-agent-with-call-transcripts).

## 10. Measuring the wrong thing

**What happens:** The team celebrates call volume or minutes while meetings are not happening.

**Why:** Activity is easy to count; outcomes take thought.

**Fix:** Track the funnel: answered, conversation, qualified, booked, attended. [AI calling metrics that matter](/blog/ai-calling-metrics-that-matter) explains each with formulas. Judge changes on enough calls to mean something, not on a handful.

## Quick audit table

| Mistake | One-line check |
|---|---|
| Several jobs | Can you state the goal in one sentence? |
| Script, not brief | Does it cope when the lead goes off-script? |
| Knowledge gaps | Are the top ten questions answered in writing? |
| No real test | Have you rung your own phone ten times? |
| Messy list | Is every number unique, valid and permitted? |
| Bad hours | Are hours set in the lead's local time? |
| Rules ignored | Have you checked the current rules per country? |
| Big-bang launch | Is the first batch under 20 contacts? |
| No review | Is there a weekly transcript slot in the diary? |
| Wrong metrics | Do you measure booked and attended meetings? |

## A note on expectations

Even a well-run launch will have rough calls. Some leads will be abrupt, a few will hang up on the opening and the agent will occasionally say something clumsy. The aim is not a flawless agent on day one; it is a short feedback loop, so each week's calls are better than the last. Keep a human in the loop for hot leads, and be clear about what the agent is.

## Order of fixes if you are already live

If your agent is already calling and you recognise several of these, do not try to fix everything at once. Work in this order: stop anything that could harm a lead or breach a rule (refusals, consent, hours), then correct wrong facts, then the opening, then the booking and handoff logic, and only then the finer points of tone. Each step is small, testable on your own phone and measurable in the next batch of calls.

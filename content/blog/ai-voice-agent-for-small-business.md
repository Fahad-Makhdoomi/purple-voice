An AI voice agent for small business is most useful for the calls you are currently missing: the ones that arrive while you are on a job, with a customer or asleep. It answers, finds out what the person needs, answers common questions and books them in. For a small team, the value is not replacing staff, it is not losing enquiries.

This guide covers what to expect, what it costs, how to set one up in an afternoon and where it should not be trusted.

## The small business problem it solves

Small businesses lose enquiries in predictable ways.

- A plumber is under a sink and cannot answer. The caller rings the next name on the list.
- A salon owner is with a client and the phone goes to voicemail. Many people do not leave a message.
- A web form lead arrives at 7pm and gets a reply at 10am, by which time they have booked elsewhere.
- Appointments are forgotten because nobody had time to send reminders.

An agent covers those gaps. It does not need breaks, and every call gets a transcript and summary so nothing relies on memory.

## What it can handle

### Inbound

- Answer your business number when you cannot, or out of hours.
- Answer common questions: hours, location, services, rough pricing, what happens next.
- Take a message with the right details, or book an appointment directly.
- Pass urgent calls to your mobile.

### Outbound

- Call new enquiries within minutes. See [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-ai-calling).
- Call a short list of past customers about a reorder or review.
- Confirm tomorrow's appointments.
- Reach people who asked for a quote and never replied.

## What it should not handle

- Complaints, disputes and anything emotional.
- Complex quotes where the details need judgement.
- Medical, legal or financial advice.
- Anything where a wrong answer is costly.

Set the agent to hand those off and say so honestly. Callers forgive "let me get someone to call you back about that" far more than a confident wrong answer.

## Setting up in an afternoon

Here is a realistic plan.

### Hour 1: Decide the job

Write down in one paragraph what the agent is for. For example: "Answer calls to the shop number when nobody picks up, answer questions about opening hours and services, and book appointments for fittings."

### Hour 2: Gather your information

Write short, accurate answers to the ten questions customers ask most:

- What do you do and not do?
- Where are you, and where do you cover?
- Opening hours and holidays.
- Rough prices or how pricing works.
- How booking works.
- Cancellation or reschedule policy.
- What to bring or prepare.
- Who to ask for.
- How long jobs take.
- What happens next.

This becomes the agent's knowledge. Keep it short and factual.

### Hour 3: Create and configure the agent

In the dashboard, create an agent, pick a voice, write the instructions and add the knowledge. Instructions should say who it is, how it should sound, what it can do, and what it must hand off. See [how to write an AI voice agent prompt](/blog/how-to-write-an-ai-voice-agent-prompt).

### Hour 4: Connect the phone and calendar

Connect a number from your provider, and connect Google Calendar if you want bookings. The Free plan allows one integration.

### Hour 5: Test properly

Use the test call to your own phone. Do not only try the happy path. Try:

- Interrupting mid-sentence.
- Asking something not in the knowledge base.
- Asking for a person.
- Giving a name and number, then checking the transcript is right.
- Asking for a time that is already booked.

Fix what you find, then test again.

## First week: go small

Do not route every call on day one. Start with after-hours or missed calls only, so the agent never replaces a call you would have answered. Read every transcript for a week. You will find gaps in the knowledge base, odd phrasings and questions you had not thought of.

## What to track

For a small business, three numbers are enough:

- Calls answered by the agent.
- Bookings or messages captured.
- Calls it transferred or could not handle.

If you want more detail, see [AI calling metrics that matter](/blog/ai-calling-metrics-that-matter).

## Mistakes small businesses make

- **Overloading the agent.** Start with one job.
- **Stale information.** Update the knowledge when prices or hours change.
- **Hiding the human option.** Make it easy to reach you.
- **Ignoring transcripts.** They are free market research.
- **Skipping the consent and recording check.** Rules on call recording and automated calls differ by country. This is general information, not legal advice, so check current requirements for where you operate.

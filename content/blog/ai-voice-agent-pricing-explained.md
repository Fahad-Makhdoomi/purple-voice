AI voice agent pricing is confusing because vendors bundle different costs into one number. The honest way to compare is to split it into three layers: the platform, the minutes, and the providers underneath. Once you price those separately for a realistic month, most quotes become easy to compare.

## The three layers of cost

| Layer | What it pays for | How it is billed |
|---|---|---|
| Platform | The software: agent builder, campaigns, dashboard, integrations, support | Monthly fee, often tiered |
| Minutes | Time the agent spends on calls | Included allowance, then per minute, or all per-minute |
| Providers | Phone numbers and calls from carriers, speech and language models | By the provider, per minute or per use |

Some vendors present a single per-minute price that includes all three. Others show a platform fee and leave providers separate. Neither approach is wrong, but they produce very different surprises.

### Per-minute bundles

A single rate per minute is easy to understand and simple to budget when volume is small. The downsides: the rate tends to include a margin on everything underneath, and rates can rise when you use higher-quality models or longer calls.

### Platform fee plus provider costs

You pay a monthly fee, get an included allowance, and pay phone and AI providers directly or through pass-through billing. The advantages are transparency and often better value at volume. The downside is that you need to understand what each piece costs.

### Seat-based pricing

Less common for voice agents, but you will see it from tools that started as dialers. If the agent does the calling, paying per human seat can be a poor fit.

## What actually drives the bill

A few things matter more than the headline rate.

1. **Call length.** A 90-second qualification call costs roughly half as much as a 3-minute chat. Prompts that waste time on small talk cost real money at volume.
2. **Answer rate.** Ringing and voicemail time may or may not be billed. Ask how unanswered calls and voicemail detection are charged.
3. **Retries.** If each contact is retried five times, a list of 1,000 contacts can mean several thousand dial attempts. Most of those are short, but they are not free.
4. **Model choice.** Speech-to-speech models and cascaded pipelines have different cost profiles and quality. See [realtime vs cascaded voice AI](/blog/realtime-vs-cascaded-voice-ai).
5. **Phone numbers and routes.** Local numbers, toll-free numbers and international calls have different carrier rates.
6. **Minimums and overage.** What happens when you go over your allowance: automatic overage, a hard stop, or a forced upgrade?

## A worked monthly example

These numbers are illustrative, not quotes. Suppose a small team follows up 400 inbound leads a month.

- Average connected call: 2.5 minutes.
- Answer rate: 45%, so 180 connected calls.
- Retries mean about 800 dial attempts, of which 620 go to voicemail or no answer and last roughly 20 seconds each.

Billable agent time: 180 x 2.5 = 450 minutes of conversation, plus about 620 x 0.33 = roughly 205 minutes of ringing and voicemail, giving about 655 minutes in total.

Now compare two illustrative price structures:

| | Bundled per-minute | Platform plus providers |
|---|---|---|
| Minutes billed | 655 | 655 against an allowance |
| Platform fee | None | Monthly fee covers an allowance |
| Phone number | Included or extra | Billed by provider |
| AI usage | Included in rate | Billed by AI provider |
| Total | Rate x 655 | Fee + provider costs |

The point is not which structure wins. It is that you can only compare them by putting the same 655 minutes through both.

## Questions to put to any vendor

Before you sign, ask each vendor the same short list, and write down the answers.

- What is the cost of a 3-minute connected call, all in, including phone and AI usage?
- How are unanswered calls, voicemail and failed connections billed?
- What happens when I use all my included minutes mid-month?
- Can I see usage by day, so I notice a runaway campaign quickly?
- Can I cancel or downgrade without a penalty, and do I keep my transcripts?

Vendors that answer these plainly are usually easier to work with later too.

## Hidden costs to ask about

- Setup, onboarding or "implementation" fees.
- Charges for extra phone numbers or agents.
- Overage rates and what triggers them.
- Costs for recording storage and retrieval.
- Fees for integrations or API access.
- Minimum commitments, annual contracts and auto-renewals.
- Whether failed calls and ring time count as minutes.

## How to estimate your own month

1. Estimate the number of contacts you will dial.
2. Estimate the answer rate. Be conservative. Many teams find it is lower than they hoped.
3. Estimate average connected call length, from a pilot if you can.
4. Add ringing and voicemail time at a lower weight.
5. Multiply by retries.
6. Price the total minutes against each plan and each provider.
7. Add 20% headroom for experiments and test calls.

A speed to lead playbook is a written process for getting from "lead arrives" to "lead spoken to" in minutes, every time, not just when someone happens to be at their desk. The model below has six steps. Each has an owner, a target and a failure check, so you can run it with a small team, with an AI voice agent, or with both.

For the reasoning behind it, read [speed to lead with AI calling](/blog/speed-to-lead-ai-calling). This page is the operating checklist.

## The six steps at a glance

| Step | Goal | Owner | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Capture | Every lead lands in one place with a phone number | Marketing or ops | Zero lost leads |
| 2. Validate | Number looks real, consent recorded | Ops | Seconds |
| 3. Route | Lead goes to a caller | Ops | Immediate |
| 4. Call | First attempt made | Rep or AI agent | Within [X] minutes |
| 5. Fall back | Next step if no answer | Process | Defined ladder |
| 6. Review | Weekly numbers and fixes | Process owner | Weekly |

Replace [X] with a number you can hit every day. Start where you are; do not promise five minutes if your current average is five hours.

## Step 1: capture every lead in one place

List every source: website forms, ad lead forms, chat, inbound emails, marketplace enquiries. Each should deliver to one system or one webhook, with name, phone number, source and timestamp.

Checks:
- Is the phone number a required field on every form?
- Do test submissions from each source arrive within a minute?
- Is the timestamp recorded when the lead arrives, not when someone opens it?

## Step 2: validate and record consent

Before anything calls, confirm the number is plausible (right length, right country format) and that the lead agreed to be contacted, with the wording and time stored. Add a line to forms such as "We'll call you about your enquiry". Rules differ by country, so check current requirements. This is general information, not legal advice. The [sales call compliance checklist](/resources/sales-call-compliance-checklist) lists the usual items.

## Step 3: route without a human in the middle

Decide in advance who gets which lead: by region, product, or source. Write the rule down. The best routing needs no approval step; a lead waiting for someone to assign it is a lead going cold.

Routing options:
- **Round robin** across available reps.
- **By territory or product.**
- **To an AI agent first**, which qualifies and books, with hot calls transferred to a human.

## Step 4: make the first call

Set the first-call script around one outcome: a conversation, then a booked next step. Keep it short and use the lead's own words from the form.

Opening pattern: "Hi [first name], it's [name] from [company]. You just asked about [topic]. Is now a good moment?"

If an AI agent makes the call, it should answer questions from a knowledge base, handle interruptions and be honest if asked whether it is an AI. See [appointment booking script templates](/resources/appointment-booking-script-templates) for wording.

## Step 5: build the fallback ladder

Most first calls will not connect. Write the ladder before launch.

1. Call within [X] minutes.
2. No answer: send a text within five to ten minutes.
3. Second call later the same day, at a different time.
4. Next day: call, then email.
5. After the last attempt, move to a nurture list.

Cap attempts and respect calling hours. The [follow-up sequence templates](/resources/follow-up-sequence-templates) give day-by-day versions.

## Step 6: review weekly

Pull these numbers every week:

| Metric | How to calculate |
| --- | --- |
| Median response time | Middle value of (first attempt time minus lead time) |
| Contact rate | Leads reached by phone divided by leads called |
| Booked rate | Meetings booked divided by leads |
| Out-of-hours share | Leads arriving outside staffed hours divided by all leads |
| Worst 10 | The ten slowest responses and why |

Median matters more than average, because a few very slow responses distort the average. Look at the worst ten each week and fix the cause, whether that is an empty rota, a broken form or a missing number.

## Common failure points

- **Evenings and weekends:** leads arrive when nobody is working. An automated first call covers these hours.
- **Missing numbers:** the form makes the phone field optional.
- **Silent failures:** a webhook breaks and nobody notices. Test weekly.
- **Slow human handover:** the AI books quickly but the closer calls back late. Agree a target for hot transfers.
- **Over-calling:** five attempts in one hour burns goodwill. Spread them out.

## A one-page version to print

1. Where do leads arrive? One list, tested weekly.
2. Is consent recorded? Yes, with timestamp.
3. Who owns the first call? Named person or agent.
4. What is our target response time? [X] minutes, 7 days a week or stated hours.
5. What happens after no answer? The ladder above.
6. Who reviews the numbers and when? Name, day, time.

Run a two-week trial before committing, and compare response time and booked rate with your previous period. The [AI calling pilot plan](/resources/ai-calling-pilot-plan-30-days) gives a structure for that.

A call quality scorecard lets you review calls the same way every time, so you can tell whether a change to the script or prompt made things better or just different. The rubric below works for human reps and for AI voice agents. Copy it into a spreadsheet, score a sample of transcripts each week and track the average.

If you are tuning an agent, pair this with [improving an AI voice agent from call transcripts](/blog/improve-ai-agent-with-call-transcripts).

## The call quality scorecard

Score each criterion from 1 to 5.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Failed or harmful |
| 2 | Weak, clear problems |
| 3 | Acceptable, room to improve |
| 4 | Good |
| 5 | Excellent, nothing to change |

### Criteria

| # | Criterion | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opening | Clear who, why and permission within 20 seconds; AI disclosure if you use it |
| 2 | Listening | Responds to what the person said, not to the script |
| 3 | Discovery | Asks relevant questions, one at a time, skips ones already answered |
| 4 | Accuracy | Facts are correct, nothing invented, says "I'll check" when unsure |
| 5 | Tone | Calm, plain, not pushy, matches the person's pace |
| 6 | Objections | Acknowledges, asks, answers, then accepts a no |
| 7 | Next step | Clear outcome: booked, transferred, follow-up set, or polite close |
| 8 | Flow | Natural pauses and turn-taking, no talking over the person |
| 9 | Compliance | Honours opt-outs, calling rules and disclosure |
| 10 | Wrap-up | Details read back, thanks, clean ending |

Maximum score is 50. Convert to a percentage if you prefer.

## Red flags

Any of these should be fixed regardless of the total score.

- [ ] Invented a price, date, guarantee or fact.
- [ ] Ignored a request to stop or to be removed.
- [ ] Continued after a clear no.
- [ ] Denied being an AI when asked sincerely.
- [ ] Gave legal, medical or financial advice.
- [ ] Booked the wrong time, date or contact detail.
- [ ] Talked over the person repeatedly.
- [ ] Loop: repeated the same question or line more than twice.

## AI-specific checks

| Check | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| Interruptions | Stops speaking and responds to the interruption |
| Silence | Checks in rather than filling it with long monologue |
| Wrong number or voicemail | Ends or leaves a message appropriately |
| Out-of-scope question | Says it will follow up and notes it |
| Names, emails and numbers | Reads back and confirms |
| Delay | Responses arrive quickly enough to feel natural |


## Weekly review routine

1. Pull 10 to 20 calls: a few booked, a few declined, a few unanswered or short, and any that looked unusual in the summary.
2. Read the transcript, not only the summary.
3. Score each call using the sheet.
4. Group the problems: opening, knowledge gaps, objections, flow.
5. Choose the two biggest issues and change one thing at a time in the prompt or knowledge base.
6. Test by calling your own phone, then compare the next week's average with this week's.

## Interpreting results

| Pattern | Likely cause | First fix |
|---|---|---|
| Low opening scores | Opening too long or vague | Rewrite the first line |
| Low accuracy scores | Missing or conflicting facts | Update the knowledge base |
| Low objection scores | No patterns in prompt | Add the top five from the [objection cheat sheet](/resources/objection-handling-cheat-sheet) |
| Good scores, few bookings | Offer or list problem | Review the list and the offer, not the agent |
| Good scores, poor next-step | Booking flow weak | Check calendar connection and read-back |

## Tips for consistent scoring

- Write one sentence of evidence for any score of 1 or 2.
- Re-score two old calls each month to check you still agree with yourself.
- Do not let one great or terrible call set the tone for the week.
- Keep the scale the same for months, so trends mean something.

A good follow-up sequence is a short, planned set of touches across more than one channel, with a clear end. The follow-up sequence templates below give you timing and message outlines for four common situations. Copy the one that matches, change the timing to suit your market, and keep the stop rules.

For the thinking behind cadences, see the [lead follow-up cadence playbook](/blog/follow-up-cadence-for-new-leads). This page is the paste-ready version.

## Principles that apply to every sequence

- **Speed first.** The earliest touch matters most. See the [speed to lead playbook](/resources/speed-to-lead-playbook).
- **Rotate channels.** Call, text, call, email beats four emails.
- **Give each touch a reason.** A question, a useful fact, a link to book.
- **Stop on a signal.** Reply, booking, opt-out, or end of sequence.
- **Respect hours.** Only contact people in sensible local hours, and honour opt-outs at once.

## Template 1: new inbound lead (10 days)

| Day | Channel | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0, within minutes | Call | Speak to them while interest is fresh |
| 0, if no answer | Text | Introduce yourself, link to book |
| 1 | Call | Second attempt, different time of day |
| 2 | Email | Short answer to the most likely question, booking link |
| 3 | Call | Third attempt |
| 5 | Text | Light check-in with one question |
| 7 | Call | Final live attempt |
| 10 | Email | Polite close, leave the door open |

**Text, day 0:** "Hi [first name], it's [name] from [company]. I tried you about your enquiry on [topic]. Want to pick a time? [booking link]"

**Email, day 2 subject:** "[Topic]: the short answer"

## Template 2: quote or proposal sent (14 days)

| Day | Channel | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Email | Send proposal with a three-line summary |
| 1 | Call | Check it arrived, offer to walk through it |
| 3 | Text | One question: any parts unclear? |
| 6 | Call | Ask about timing and decision process |
| 9 | Email | Address the most common concern |
| 14 | Call | Ask directly: go ahead, wait, or pass? |

**Email wording, day 9:** "Hi [first name], most people ask about [concern]. Here's how it works: [two sentences]. Happy to go through it on a quick call."

## Template 3: missed appointment (5 days)

| Day | Channel | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0, within an hour | Text | Light check, offer rebooking link |
| 0, same day | Call | Reach them if the text is unanswered |
| 2 | Email | Rebooking link and what the call would cover |
| 5 | Text | Final nudge, then stop |

**Text:** "Hi [first name], we missed you at [time]. No problem. Want to rebook? [link]"

See [appointment booking script templates](/resources/appointment-booking-script-templates) for the call wording.

## Template 4: dormant lead reactivation (3 weeks)

| Week | Channel | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Email | Re-introduce with one new thing |
| 1 | Call | Short, low-pressure check-in |
| 2 | Text | One question about whether it is still relevant |
| 2 | Call | Second attempt, different time of day |
| 3 | Email | Close the loop |

Keep the first message honest about the gap: "It's been a while since you asked about [topic]". More in [reactivating old leads with AI calls](/blog/reactivate-old-leads-with-ai-calls).

## Stop rules to build in

Write these down before launch:

1. **Replied, booked or spoke to a human:** end the sequence and hand over.
2. **Said no or asked to stop:** remove from all channels immediately.
3. **Wrong number or invalid email:** mark and stop.
4. **Sequence finished:** move to a long-term nurture list or close the lead.
5. **Daily cap:** never contact the same lead on more than two channels in a day.

## Message outline you can reuse

Every text or email in a sequence can follow this skeleton:

1. Who you are (company and first name).
2. Why now (their enquiry, quote, missed time).
3. One idea (answer, question or fact).
4. One action (reply, book, call).

## Measuring a sequence

Track by step, not just overall: which touch produced the reply or booking, and how many leads reached each step. If most conversions come from touch one, tighten the first hour. If late touches never convert, shorten the sequence. [AI calling metrics that matter](/blog/ai-calling-metrics-that-matter) covers the numbers worth tracking.

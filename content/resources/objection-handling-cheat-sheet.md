This objection handling cheat sheet lists twenty objections you will hear on sales calls, each with a response pattern rather than a rigid script. Use the patterns as raw material: change the wording to sound like you, and keep what you say honest. If someone clearly does not want to continue, the right response is to thank them and end the call.

For the longer approach to building an objection library, see [handling sales objections with an AI voice agent](/blog/handling-objections-with-ai-voice-agent).

## The four-step method

Nearly every response below follows the same shape:

1. Acknowledge: show you heard it ("That makes sense").
2. Ask: find the real concern with one open question.
3. Answer: respond to that concern with a fact, not a pitch.
4. Advance: suggest a small next step, or let go gracefully.

## Objection handling cheat sheet: 20 objections

### Interest and timing

| # | Objection | Response pattern |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Not interested." | Acknowledge, ask whether it is the timing or the topic, accept the answer. "Fair enough. Is it more the timing, or not something you'd use?" |
| 2 | "I'm busy right now." | Offer a callback. "No problem. When is a better time, later today or tomorrow?" |
| 3 | "Call me back later." | Pin down a time. "Happy to. Would morning or afternoon suit you better?" |
| 4 | "Now isn't the right time." | Ask what would change that. "Understood. When do you expect that to change?" |
| 5 | "I'm already looking at other options." | Be curious, not defensive. "Good to know. What matters most to you in comparing them?" |

### Information and trust

| # | Objection | Response pattern |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | "Send me an email." | Agree, and confirm the address. "Sure. What's the best email? I'll keep it short." |
| 7 | "Who is this? How did you get my number?" | Answer plainly with the real source. "You enquired about [X] on [date]. I'm calling to follow up." |
| 8 | "Is this a robot?" | Answer honestly. "Yes, I'm an AI assistant calling for [Company]. I can still help, or arrange a call with a person." |
| 9 | "Take me off your list." | Comply immediately. "Of course. I'll remove you now. Sorry to bother you." |
| 10 | "I don't talk to salespeople." | Acknowledge, shrink the ask. "That's fair. I only wanted to ask one quick question, then I'll let you go." |

### Price and value

| # | Objection | Response pattern |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | "How much does it cost?" | Give the facts you have. If pricing depends, say so and ask one qualifying question. |
| 12 | "That's too expensive." | Ask what they are comparing to. "What were you expecting to pay?" Then respond with fact, not discount. |
| 13 | "We don't have budget." | Ask about timing. "Understood. Would it be useful if I checked back next quarter?" |
| 14 | "We've got a cheaper option." | Ask what it includes. "What does that cover for you?" Avoid attacking it. |
| 15 | "What's the catch?" | Be direct about limits. State what is included and what is not. |

### Authority and process

| # | Objection | Response pattern |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | "I need to talk to someone else." | Ask who and offer to include them. "Who else would be involved? I can arrange a call with them." |
| 17 | "I'm not the right person." | Ask for the right contact. "Who would be best to speak to about this?" |
| 18 | "We already have a provider." | Acknowledge, ask about gaps. "Good to hear. Is there anything you wish it did better?" |
| 19 | "We tried something like this before." | Ask what happened. "What went wrong?" Then respond to that reason specifically. |
| 20 | "I need to think about it." | Ask what is unresolved. "Sure. What would you want to be clearer on?" Offer a follow-up date. |

## Rules that apply to every objection

- Never argue. If the person is firm, you lose by pushing.
- Never invent facts, prices or guarantees to win the point.
- One response, then one question. Do not stack rebuttals.
- A no is an acceptable result. End politely and record it.
- Opt-out requests (numbers 9 and sometimes 1) are final: stop the call and mark the contact as do not call.

## Writing these into an AI voice agent

An AI agent handles objections best with patterns plus facts, not memorised lines.

- Put the facts it may use (pricing ranges, what is included, how booking works) in the knowledge base.
- In the prompt, list the objections you hear most with a one-line pattern each, as above.
- Add hard rules: stop when asked, no pressure, transfer to a human when the person wants one.
- Tell it to log the objection in the call summary so you can see what comes up.

The [AI voice agent prompt template](/resources/ai-voice-agent-prompt-template) has an objections section ready to fill in, and the [cold call script templates](/resources/cold-call-script-templates) show how to open so that fewer objections arise.

## Tuning from real calls

After each batch, read the transcripts and find the objections that were not on your list. Add the three most frequent, rewrite any response that sounded defensive, and remove any line the agent used too often. Score a sample with the [call quality scorecard](/resources/call-quality-scorecard) to keep the review consistent.

American Heritage Credit Union's AI agent, Gigi, has handled more than half a million member conversations in under a year, SoundHound AI said in a press release on November 12, 2026. The release puts the total at about 598,000 voice and chat conversations over 11 months, from July 2025 through May 2026. All of the figures come from the companies and have not been independently verified.

## What was announced

American Heritage is a Philadelphia-area credit union with more than $5 billion in assets and about 325,000 members, according to the release. Gigi answers members by phone and chat, around the clock, and covers more than 30 request types. Those include:

- verifying members and reading balances and recent transactions
- moving money between accounts
- resetting online banking passwords
- activating and replacing debit cards
- quoting loan payoff amounts and branch hours
- guiding members through opening an estate account

The credit union has previously described Gigi as powered by Amelia, the conversational AI company SoundHound acquired in 2024. The new release describes Gigi as built with SoundHound.

## The numbers, as reported

| Metric (self-reported) | Figure |
|---|---|
| Member conversations, July 2025 to May 2026 | about 598,000 |
| Average phone conversations per day | about 1,897 |
| Fraud and dispute chats resolved without a person | 88% |
| Account maintenance chats resolved without a person | 78% |
| Rate and certificate inquiries resolved without a person | 70% |
| Inbound voice traffic screened out as silent or dropped calls | about 5% |

The release also says Gigi has handled a larger share of member requests each quarter since tracking began. Note that most of the resolution rates quoted are for chat, not voice; the release does not give an equivalent resolution rate for phone calls.

When a member needs a person, Gigi passes the whole conversation, including verification status, to a representative. A SoundHound executive said members care whether their problem gets solved, not whether an agent or a person solves it, and a credit union analyst said members feel like they are talking to an actual person.

## Why it matters for teams that sell or support by phone

Credit unions are a demanding test for voice agents: callers need to be verified, money moves, and mistakes are costly. A few points stand out for anyone running inbound calls:

- **Scope is specific.** Gigi handles a defined list of tasks rather than "anything a member asks". Narrow, well-defined jobs are where AI agents tend to perform best.
- **Handoff carries context.** Passing verification status and the conversation to a person avoids a familiar frustration with automated phone systems: having to repeat yourself. Our guide to [warm transfers from AI to a human](/blog/warm-transfer-ai-to-human) covers how to do this well.
- **Measurement is per task.** Reporting resolution by request type is more useful than a single containment number. The [AI calling metrics that matter](/blog/ai-calling-metrics-that-matter) article goes into which numbers to track.

## What to watch next

- The credit union says it is adding agentic workflows so Gigi can complete more complex requests on its own.
- It is also building proactive alerts, starting with mail returned as undeliverable, where Gigi can confirm or update a member's address. Unactivated debit cards, delinquent loans and negative balances are listed as later targets.
- Whether other credit unions publish comparable, task-level results, which would make these figures easier to judge.

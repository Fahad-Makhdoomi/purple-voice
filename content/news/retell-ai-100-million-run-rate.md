Retell AI, the voice agent platform founded in 2023, says it has passed a $100 million annualized run rate. Forbes reported the milestone on November 12, 2026, noting that the company has raised only $4.6 million in total and had about 80 employees. Retell published the same headline figure on its own blog the same day.

## What Retell announced

The numbers come from the company and from Forbes' reporting on it. None of them has been independently audited, and "annualized run rate" projects a current pace of revenue forward rather than counting a finished year.

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Annualized run rate | Over $100M | Retell, Forbes |
| Total funding raised | $4.6M | Retell, Forbes |
| Employees | About 80 | Forbes |
| Time from $10M to $100M run rate | 17 months | Forbes |
| Businesses in production | 4,000+ | Retell |
| Calls per month | 100M+ | Retell |

Forbes also reports that Retell is profitable, that its enterprise revenue grew ninefold in 2026, and that it supports calls in more than 55 languages. Named customers in the Forbes piece include Lenovo, CVS Health and Bill. On its blog, Retell highlights Asbury Automotive Group, which it says runs Retell agents across 157 dealerships.

According to Forbes, the seed round was led by Jack Altman of Alt Capital, with Y Combinator partner Michael Seibel and Lyft cofounder Rajat Suri among the early backers. CEO Bing Wu co-founded the company with Zexia Zhang, Evie Wang, Weijia Yu and Todd Li.

The same week, Forbes reports, Retell launched Tell-1, a language model the company says was trained on hundreds of millions of calls and built for live contact center conversations. Retell also sells a dialer product for outbound voice and SMS campaigns, including callbacks at times the contact asks for.

## Why it matters for teams that sell or support by phone

Two points stand out for anyone running calls.

**Small teams can now run voice agents at serious volume.** If the figures hold, roughly 80 people support more than 100 million calls a month. Retell's blog says it has tried to put deployment work into the product itself rather than relying mainly on large teams of engineers sent to each customer. For buyers, that is the trend to watch: voice agents that a sales ops lead or developer can configure and change without a services contract.

**Buyers want to own the workflow.** Forbes quotes analyst Zeus Kerravala saying enterprises increasingly want direct control of agent workflows rather than long platform contracts. Retell's blog makes a similar case, arguing that the team closest to the customer should be the one shaping the agent. A customer quote on the blog praises being able to "make changes ourselves."

For sales teams, the practical takeaway is that the market has moved past pilots. Voice agents are handling large shares of routine calls, and the tools to build them are becoming self-serve. That raises the bar on the basics: response speed, clean handoffs to humans, and honest reporting on what the agent did. ## What to watch next

- **Whether the run rate converts to revenue.** A run rate is a snapshot. Retention and usage over the next few quarters will show how durable it is.
- **Tell-1 in practice.** Retell has not published a model card, so independent latency and accuracy tests will matter more than launch claims.
- **Pricing pressure.** Retell's site lists per-minute pricing. As more platforms reach this scale, expect per-minute costs across the category to keep moving. Our [pricing explainer](/blog/ai-voice-agent-pricing-explained) breaks down where the money goes.
- **New funding.** A company this size on $4.6 million raised may not need outside capital, but a large round would not be a surprise.

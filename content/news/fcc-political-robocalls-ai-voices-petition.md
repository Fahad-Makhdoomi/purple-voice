An FCC political robocall waiver that would have let political groups call cell phones with AI-generated or prerecorded voices, without the recipient's prior consent, drew most of its public comments in opposition and did not get a ruling before the November 3 midterms. The petition, filed by the Club for Growth, was put out for comment by the FCC in September, and AP reported on October 7 that the agency could rule as soon as late October. As of November 13, we found no order either way.

## What the petition asked for

Federal law, through the Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA), generally bars calls to cell phones that use an artificial or prerecorded voice unless the person called has given prior consent. The Club for Growth asked the FCC to waive that consent requirement for noncommercial political calls.

According to AP's reporting, the request came with conditions:

- Callers would have to offer recipients a way to opt out.
- No more than three calls to any one recipient within 30 days.
- Other state and federal laws against election deception and voter intimidation would still apply.

The group argued the current rules are outdated and restrict speech, pointed out that automated political calls to landlines are already allowed, and said AI voices could help campaigns reach voters in more languages and improve polling. The FCC's Consumer and Governmental Affairs Bureau opened the request for comment in a public notice (DA 26-940) on September 4, 2026.

## Who weighed in

Opposition was broad. Commissioner Anna Gomez, the only Democrat among the three sitting commissioners, issued a statement on October 8 warning against the waiver, and reports say Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer sent a letter urging the agency not to grant it. AP quoted the Center for Democracy and Technology and the Brennan Center, which raised concerns about misleading AI conversations close to Election Day and about privacy. Communications Daily reported that most reply comments opposed the request, while a few groups, including the American Association of Political Consultants, backed it.

## Where it stands

The reply comment period ended October 19. On October 21, Communications Daily reported that the FCC had signalled quick action would not happen. Election Day came and went on November 3 without an order. The docket (CG 02-278) has not been closed, so the petition could in theory still be acted on later, but the pre-midterm urgency the petitioners cited no longer applies.

## Why it matters for teams that sell or support by phone

The waiver would not have touched commercial calling. The baseline that matters for sales teams is unchanged:

| Question | Current position |
|---|---|
| Is an AI voice an "artificial voice" under the TCPA? | Yes, per the FCC's February 2024 declaratory ruling (FCC 24-17) |
| Does an AI sales call to a cell phone need consent? | Generally yes, and telemarketing calls need prior express written consent |
| Did the political waiver change that? | No. It was limited to noncommercial political calls and was not granted |

The episode is still a useful signal. Regulators, lawmakers and consumer groups are paying close attention to AI voices on the phone, and the pushback here centred on two things legitimate callers can control: whether people agreed to be called and whether they can tell who, or what, is calling. - Whether the FCC formally dismisses the petition or lets it sit in the docket.
- Any similar requests ahead of the 2028 cycle, which could revive the same debate.
- State-level rules on AI in political communications, which continue to change independently of the FCC.

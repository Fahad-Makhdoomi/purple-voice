Convoso has made its no-code voice AI agent generally available, according to a November 3, 2026 report from Unite.AI, which says general availability began the day before. The Convoso voice AI agent sits inside the company's outbound dialer, qualifies leads on outbound calls, handles routine inbound calls, and passes sales-ready prospects to human agents. It uses the same campaign workflows, reporting and compliance controls as Convoso's human agents.

## What Convoso launched

Convoso positions the agent as a way for contact centers to automate first contact without engineering work. Here is what the company says it does:

- **Outbound:** opens the conversation, runs a qualification script, handles objections and routes or dispositions leads based on their answers.
- **Inbound:** answers routine questions, books appointments and sends callers to the right team.
- **Handoffs:** transfers qualified prospects, and any request it cannot resolve, to people. Convoso describes these as warm transfers; its FAQ says cold transfers are also possible.
- **Speed and conversation:** about 600 milliseconds response time, interruption handling, context retention and switching languages mid-call. Convoso says the agent was trained on 20 million sales conversations.
- **Voices and models:** a choice of AI models and text-to-speech providers, more than 700 voices, tone and speed controls, and optional voice cloning.
- **Builder tools:** an Auto Workflow feature that can draw on past call recordings to help build qualification flows, plus an in-browser test before going live.
- **Reliability:** a fallback chain that moves a call to a backup model if the primary one fails.

On compliance, Convoso lists do-not-call checks, state-level dialing restrictions, time-of-day rules and PII redaction, set to each customer's policies. Pricing was not disclosed in the launch coverage.

One limit matters for buyers outside Convoso's base: the agent works only with the Convoso dialing platform, connected over SIP/RTP.

## Early customer results

Convoso shared two customer examples, both company-provided:

| Customer | Use | Reported result |
|---|---|---|
| Tort Intake Professionals | Outbound qualification | Over 150 contacts in a 36-hour test, with about 10-12% validated and warm-transferred to specialists |
| Ace Building Materials | Inbound front desk | An agent named George answers store-hours and product questions, switches to Spanish, routes to dispatch; deployed in about two weeks |

These are early, small samples. They show the shape of the use case more than its long-run performance.

## Why it matters for teams that sell or support by phone

The launch reflects where most sales teams start with AI voice: the first minute of the call. An agent dials, confirms the lead is real and interested, asks the screening questions, and hands the good ones to a closer. That keeps human reps on conversations that need them. Our guide to [AI lead qualification calls](/blog/ai-lead-qualification-calls) walks through how to design those screening questions.

A few things to look at, whichever tool you use:

- **Transfer quality.** A warm transfer only works if the rep gets the context. Check what details pass to the person picking up. See [warm transfers from AI to a human](/blog/warm-transfer-ai-to-human).
- **Compliance in the flow.** Disclosures, opt-outs and calling hours need to be built into the call, not added later. Our [sales call compliance checklist](/resources/sales-call-compliance-checklist) is a starting point; it is general information, not legal advice.
- **Lock-in.** An agent tied to one dialer is simpler if you already use that dialer, and harder to move if you change platforms.

## What to watch next

- **Pricing.** Convoso has not published what the agent costs per minute or per seat.
- **Results at scale.** Larger customer numbers, such as transfer rates over months rather than a 36-hour test, would show how well it holds up.
- **Wider integrations.** Whether Convoso opens the agent to other telephony setups.

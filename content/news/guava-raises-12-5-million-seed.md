Guava, a Los Angeles voice AI company, has raised a $12.5 million seed round led by Acrew Capital, according to a Business Wire release published in early November 2026. Flourish Ventures, WillowTree Ventures, Clocktower Ventures and Commerce Ventures also joined the Guava seed round. Guava sells a developer platform for building voice agents that handle customer calls, and runs them on its own voice model, Daytona.

## The details

Guava was founded by Omri Dahan, Anthony Scodary, Nico Benitez and Mike Piechna. The company describes its product as a full-stack platform for building and controlling voice AI agents. Developers connect knowledge bases and business logic in code, rather than through a drag-and-drop builder.

Guava says it will use the money for model and product development and to build a go-to-market team aimed at enterprises and business process outsourcers (BPOs). No valuation or revenue figures were disclosed.

Key facts at a glance:

- **Round:** $12.5M seed
- **Lead:** Acrew Capital
- **Also investing:** Flourish Ventures, WillowTree Ventures, Clocktower Ventures, Commerce Ventures
- **Product:** developer platform for voice agents plus the Daytona voice model
- **Target buyers:** regulated industries, high-volume customer service teams, BPOs

## Daytona and the benchmark behind it

Guava launched Daytona in September 2026 as its first publicly available voice model. According to the funding release, Daytona supports more than a dozen enterprise customers and handles tens of thousands of calls a day. Those are company figures.

Alongside Daytona, Guava published the Guava Voice Index, a benchmark that scores voice agents from 0 to 100 across five areas: responsiveness, conversational flow, fidelity, resolution and speech quality. Content accuracy carries 60% of the weight and voice experience 40%.

In Guava's own results, Daytona scored 58.88, ahead of ElevenLabs paired with GPT 5.4 at 57.11. Daytona led on fidelity, speech quality and response time, while the ElevenLabs setup did better on actually resolving the caller's issue. No system scored above 60.

Two caveats apply. Guava built both the model and the benchmark, and we found no independent reproduction of the scores. And the benchmark's most useful finding may be the human comparison: evaluators preferred live human agents in 91% of response-timing comparisons and 93% of interruption-recovery comparisons, according to Guava.

## Why it matters for teams that sell or support by phone

The human comparison is worth sitting with. Even the best-scoring voice agents in Guava's test still trailed people on timing and on recovering when a caller cuts in. That matches what many teams see in practice: agents handle the routine middle of a call well, and the edges are where calls go wrong.

For a sales team, that points to a few practical choices:

- **Keep the agent's job narrow.** Qualification, answering common questions and booking a meeting are well within reach. Long negotiations are not.
- **Plan the handoff.** A clean [warm transfer to a human](/blog/warm-transfer-ai-to-human) covers the moments the agent cannot.
- **Test before launch.** Benchmarks measure averages. Your own scripts and objections are what matter, so [test your agent](/blog/test-your-ai-voice-agent) against real scenarios before it calls a list.

Guava's focus on resolution as well as voice quality is also a useful lens for buyers. A natural-sounding agent that cannot finish the task is not much help.

## What to watch next

- **Independent results.** Whether other teams reproduce the Guava Voice Index rankings will decide how much weight it carries.
- **BPO adoption.** Guava is targeting outsourcers. If large BPOs switch call volume to voice agents, pricing and expectations across the market will shift.
- **More model makers.** Guava joins a growing group of companies building their own voice models rather than stitching together third-party speech and language models.

Salesforce said on November 2, 2026 that it has signed a definitive agreement to acquire Cresta, a voice AI company that sells voice agents and real-time coaching software to large contact centers. The Salesforce Cresta acquisition is still subject to regulatory approval, and Salesforce did not disclose a price, though The Information reported it at about $2.7 billion.

It is one of several buying moves Salesforce has made in a short span, and the clearest signal yet that the big CRM vendors see voice as the next place customer data has to live.

## What was announced

According to Salesforce's announcement, Cresta's voice agents and conversation intelligence will be built into Service Cloud, so each call can be tied to the customer record and to an outcome such as a resolution, a retained account or revenue.

The key details so far:

| Item | What is known |
|---|---|
| Announced | November 2, 2026 |
| Deal type | Definitive agreement, not yet closed |
| Expected close | Q1 of Salesforce's fiscal 2028 |
| Conditions | Regulatory approvals and customary closing conditions |
| Price | Not disclosed; about $2.7B per The Information |
| Customers named | Marriott, Comcast, Alaska Airlines |

Cresta's product has two halves. One is voice agents that handle complex calls start to finish. The other coaches human reps live during calls and scores every interaction afterward. Kishan Chetan, who runs Agentforce and Service Cloud at Salesforce, said voice is where service is hardest to get right. Cresta CEO Ping Wu framed the combination as bringing Cresta's conversation intelligence into Salesforce's service platform.

Salesforce also said Cresta will keep supporting the contact center and telephony platforms its customers use today. Constellation Research notes that Cresta already integrates with platforms such as Avaya, Five9, Genesys and Cisco, and with CRMs from Microsoft, Oracle and ServiceNow.

## Part of a busy run of deals

Constellation Research described Cresta as Salesforce's third acquisition in two weeks, following Rune Technologies (aimed at US government work) and Braze, which Constellation priced at $5.66 billion. The firm's read is that Salesforce is pairing a few large deals with many smaller tuck-ins, and that Cresta fits its push toward outcome-based offerings.

Forrester's analysts went further, arguing that Salesforce held plenty of customer context but lacked strong conversation intelligence, and that Cresta fills that gap. They also flagged the risk: Salesforce is layering another product onto an already crowded portfolio, which can make buying more complicated, not less.

## Why it matters for teams that sell or support by phone

The phone call has been the part of the customer journey that CRMs captured worst. A rep's notes, a disposition code and maybe a recording link were often all that made it back to the record. Deals like this one aim to close that gap so the conversation itself becomes structured data.

For sales and support leaders, a few practical points follow:

- **Calls are becoming CRM data.** Expect more vendors to push transcripts, summaries and outcomes straight into the customer record. If your current setup does not do this, it is worth fixing now; see our guide to [AI call summaries and CRM notes](/blog/ai-call-summaries-and-crm-notes).
- **Enterprise voice AI is consolidating.** Large buyers will increasingly get voice agents bundled with their CRM. Smaller teams may find those bundles priced and scoped for big contact centers.
- **Platform neutrality matters.** Forrester advises buyers to separate what they can deploy today from what depends on future integration work, and to watch whether Cresta keeps working well with non-Salesforce telephony.

## What to watch next

- Regulatory review and the actual close date.
- How Cresta is packaged and priced inside Service Cloud and Agentforce.
- Whether support for third-party contact center platforms holds up after integration.
- Whether other CRM and contact center vendors respond with their own voice AI deals.
